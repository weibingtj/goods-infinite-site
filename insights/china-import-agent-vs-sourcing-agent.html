China Import Agent vs. Sourcing Agent: What Overseas Buyers Should Know

These two terms sound similar but move goods in opposite directions. An import agent helps you sell INTO China; a sourcing agent helps you buy OUT of China. Know which one you need.

Published 2026-08-18 · Last updated 2026-09-07 · By Bing Wei, Operations Director

Same country, opposite directions

The phrases "China import agent" and "China sourcing agent" get confused constantly, and the mistake is expensive. They describe opposite supply chains:

  • Sourcing agent — helps you buy FROM China (you are the overseas buyer; goods flow out).
  • Import agent — helps you sell INTO China (you are the overseas brand; goods flow in).

GOODSINFINITE is an import agent. We help foreign brands land their products in the China market.

Sourcing agent: foreign buyer, Chinese factory

A sourcing agent's job is procurement on your behalf:

  • Find and vet Chinese manufacturers.
  • Negotiate unit price and MOQ.
  • Handle quality control, inspections and exports.
  • Ship to your country.

This is the classic "I want cheap goods from China" service. It is mature, crowded, and largely a margin game on factory price.

Import agent: foreign brand, Chinese consumer

An import agent's job is market entry and fulfilment:

  • Structure the import (Hong Kong entity / importer of record).
  • Clear customs and store in bonded warehouses (1210).
  • Fulfil per order to Chinese consumers.
  • Run China e-commerce stores and marketing.

This is the service Western brands need when they want to sell to China, not buy from it.

Why the difference matters for you

If you are a Western brand with a product and you want Chinese customers, a sourcing agent will not help you — they optimise for getting goods out of China, not into Chinese homes compliantly. You need an import partner that owns customs access, bonded infrastructure and China e-commerce operations.

The single biggest risk with the wrong choice is compliance: importing into China triggers CCC, NMPA, food and labelling rules that a sourcing agent simply does not handle.

Our open-book model

Because import agents sit between you and the Chinese consumer, trust is everything. We use open-book pricing:

  • The factory / product cost is shown to you as-is.
  • Our service fee is stated separately.
  • There is no hidden rebate from any supplier.

This directly answers the fear every overseas brand voices: "How do I know my agent isn't taking a kickback?" — you see the line items.

FAQ

Which one do I need? If you want to sell into China, you need an import agent (customs + bonded + China ops). If you want to buy from China, you need a sourcing agent.

Can GOODSINFINITE source products for me? We can support light sourcing as a complement, but our core is helping overseas brands import and operate in China — that is where we add the most value.

How are import agents paid? Typically a transparent service fee plus bonded storage and per-order fulfilment, with factory cost shown separately (open-book).
