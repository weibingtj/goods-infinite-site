China Consumer Protection for Imported Goods: The 7-Day Return, After-Sales Duties and the Cross-Border Exception

China has no single 'return policy' for imported goods. Three separate legal routes apply — a quality-defect return, a 7-day no-reason return, and a customs-supervised cross-border return — and the 2024 implementing regulation plus two 2026 platform rules add duties that land on whoever sells to a Chinese consumer. This guide maps the rules, the deadlines, the penalties and who carries liability when the brand has no mainland entity.

Published 2026-11-04 · Last updated 2026-11-10 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

China's consumer-protection rules for imported goods are three separate legal routes, not one return policy — and which one applies is decided by how the goods were cleared, not by where the brand is incorporated

A foreign brand selling into China usually asks whether its goods "have to be accepted back". The answer is not a policy setting in a storefront. It is a legal chain: the Consumer Protection Law and the Implementing Regulation, State Council Decree No. 778 define the seller's duties toward a Chinese consumer; the Interim Measures on 7-Day No-Reason Returns, Order No. 90 define the procedure and the intact-goods test; and, where goods entered under the 1210 or 9610 cross-border retail codes, Notice 486 of 2018 and GACC Announcement 2020 No. 45 add a customs layer that most overseas sellers never see until a return goes wrong. None of these instruments exempts a product because it was imported. What changes is who stands behind the sale and what has to happen at the border.

Two consequences follow. First, a defective imported batch cannot be pushed into the no-reason route to shift freight to the buyer. Second, the customs return rules mean the commercial refund a platform shows the shopper is only half the transaction; the other half is a declaration-list procedure with a hard deadline.

What the 7-day rule actually requires of a seller

Art. 25 grants the right, but Order No. 90 is where stores fail. It sets the exclusion list, defines when goods are still "intact", and imposes a purchase-flow confirmation step that cannot be hidden in terms and conditions.

Art. 20 of Order No. 90 requires the seller to build a conspicuous confirmation step into the sales flow for conditional-exclusion goods, and states that without it the seller may not refuse the return. Art. 19 of Decree No. 778 goes further: the exclusion may not be a default ticked option. Enforcement is not theoretical — Arts. 30 and 31 of Order No. 90 route an unjustified refusal, a failure to provide a valid return address within 15 days, and a failure to refund within 15 days of receiving the goods to the penalty in Art. 56(1)(8) of the Consumer Protection Law. In other words, a product page that simply says "no returns on imported goods" is not a policy; it is a violation.

Decree No. 778: the 2024 implementing regulation most foreign brands have not mapped

Decree No. 778 (State Council Order No. 778, published 15 March 2024, in force 1 July 2024, seven chapters and 53 articles) does not create a new return right. It tightens the duties around it, and several of its articles land squarely on an overseas-brand-plus-mainland-operator structure.

For imported goods, Arts. 13 and 49 deserve a second read. Art. 13 is why a storefront cannot show only the overseas brand while a mainland company quietly handles declaration, warehousing and after-sales. Art. 49 is often quoted as a "labelling error is fine" rule — it is not: the defect must both leave quality unaffected and be non-misleading. Art. 51 preserves the ordinary Administrative Penalty Law routes to lighter or no punishment where the operator voluntarily removes the harm, or the breach is minor and promptly corrected.

The cross-border exception: a 1210 return is not an ordinary e-commerce return

Notice 486 of 2018 defines four participants — the cross-border e-commerce enterprise (the overseas registered company that owns the goods), the mainland-registered platform, the mainland-registered service provider, and the consumer — and allocates liability in unusual detail. The overseas enterprise bears primary responsibility for quality and safety and for consumer rights, expressly including product information disclosure, return and exchange services, a recall system for defective goods, and compensation for quality-related harm. It must entrust one mainland-registered enterprise, which registers with customs, declares truthfully and bears joint and several civil liability. The platform must run a consumer-dispute system and perform advance payment. The consumer is the taxpayer, while the platform, logistics company or customs broker withholds and pays the tax and provides a tax guarantee to customs.

That last point is what makes the return different. Under GACC Announcement 2020 No. 45, the mainland agent or its broker (the "returning enterprise") may apply to customs for a return of all or part of the goods on the original declaration list. Where it applies within 30 days of that list's release and the goods are returned in their original state within 45 days to the original customs supervision site, the original special customs supervision area or a type-B bonded logistics centre, the corresponding tax is not levied and the consumer's annual cumulative purchase amount is adjusted. Where that customs sequence is not completed, the tax and quota outcome is a separate matter from any refund the platform may make under its advance-payment duty.

30 days / 45 daysThe two customs clocks behind a cross-border retail import return under GACC Announcement 2020 No. 45 — 30 days from release of the declaration list to apply, 45 days to get the goods back in original state to the original customs site, special supervision area or type-B bonded logistics centreSource: GACC Announcement 2020 No. 45, Arts. 1–3 (gov.cn and customs.gov.cn official text)

Note also what the statutes do not say. The Consumer Protection Law, Decree No. 778, Order No. 90 and Notice 486 contain no provision that expressly resolves whether the 7-day no-reason right applies to goods cleared under 1210 or 9610. So the answer is not "imported goods are exempt" — it is set by the platform rules, by the risk notice the consumer must confirm before ordering, and by whether the seller has used the conditional-exclusion mechanism correctly. That is a design question for the listing and the returns workflow, not something to assume from the customs code. See our cross-border returns guide for the operational side, and what 1210 bonded import is for the channel itself.

The practical implication is that return economics follow the bonded footprint. Because goods must come back into a customs-supervised site, a brand with stock in a bonded zone and a defined mainland entrustee can close the loop; a brand shipping direct-mail parcels with no mainland counterparty has no one able to file the customs return. This is the same structural gap we describe in importing into China without a local company.

Two new rules from 1 February 2026

Two SAMR and Cyberspace Administration rules published on 18 December 2025 took effect on 1 February 2026, and both matter to a foreign brand using a TP partner or a livestream agency. Order No. 116 governs how platform rules are made, amended and enforced: rules or their links must be displayed continuously in a prominent homepage position and be easy to read and download, and platforms must use those rules to implement product and service quality, consumer-rights, fair-competition, credit, personal-information and data responsibilities. Order No. 117 governs livestream commerce: it defines four participant categories, sets platform duties on identity verification and registration, information reporting, training, tiered management and transaction-record retention, requires live-room operators to display product names and prices, run pre-broadcast compliance reviews and keep correction and interaction records, and prohibits false or misleading commercial promotion. Both encourage first-contact responsibility, advance payment and online dispute resolution.

For an overseas brand, these duties usually sit with the Chinese TP or live-room operator. They should be written into the operating agreement, together with who answers the 12315 complaint trail and who keeps the chat, order and after-sales records. Our cross-border customer service guide covers the record-keeping side, and advertising compliance covers the claims side.

What we deliberately do not assert

  • We do not give a single "three guarantees" (三包) term or a list of covered products. Art. 18 of Decree No. 778 sets a floor — an agreed term may not be shorter than the relevant state rules — and fixes when the clock starts and restarts; the concrete term depends on the product's applicable state rules and the contract.
  • We do not assert that cross-border retail imports are categorically covered by, or categorically exempt from, the 7-day no-reason rule. No instrument we reviewed says so. We state the statutory rules and the customs sequence and leave the channel-specific application to the platform rules and the consumer's purchase-time confirmation.
  • We do not quote return rates, return freight, compensation averages or any named enforcement case. No official source we could verify provides them for this question.
  • We do not infer what happens to tax or quota when the 30/45-day customs sequence is missed. Art. 3 of Announcement 2020 No. 45 states the consequence for returns that meet both deadlines; it does not set out an exhaustive rule for all other cases.
  • We do not claim that Notice 486's exemption from first-import licensing, registration or filing extends to consumer-protection obligations. The circular expressly assigns those obligations instead.

FAQ

Does the 7-day return apply to imported goods? For goods sold on a domestic Chinese distance-selling channel, yes — Art. 25 draws no distinction by origin. For 1210/9610 cross-border retail imports, the statutes do not expressly settle it; platform rules and purchase-time confirmation decide.

Which goods can be excluded? Four statutory classes (custom-made, perishable, unsealed digital media, newspapers/periodicals) plus three classes that are excluded only after prominent marking and the consumer's confirmation at purchase.

Who pays return freight? The operator for a quality-defect return under Art. 24; the consumer for a no-reason return under Art. 25, unless agreed otherwise.

What are the deadlines? 7 days to notify a no-reason return from the day after signing, 7 days to refund after receiving the goods back, 7 working days for a regulator to process a complaint, 60 days to complete mediation, and 30/45 days for the customs-side cross-border return.

Who is liable when the seller is overseas? Under Notice 486 of 2018, the overseas e-commerce enterprise bears primary quality and consumer-rights responsibility, its mainland entrustee bears joint and several civil liability, and the platform performs advance payment.

What are the penalties? Decree No. 778 Art. 50 allows one to five times illegal gain or up to RMB 300,000 where there is none; Order No. 90 routes unjustified refusals and late refunds to Art. 56(1)(8) of the Consumer Protection Law.

What changed in 2026? Orders No. 116 and No. 117 took effect on 1 February 2026, tightening platform-rule transparency and livestream-commerce duties.
