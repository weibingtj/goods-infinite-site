## Mega-promo enrollment is the contract that trades margin for official traffic

Mega-promo enrollment is the formal process by which a platform store commits to an event's pricing, service and compliance rules in exchange for official traffic placement during a festival window such as Double 11 or 618. It is not a calendar listing and it is not paid advertising: [Tmall's 2025 Double 11 merchant recruitment rules](https://rulechannel.taobao.com/?type=detail&ruleId=20008595&cId=1142) run enrollment as a binding application with fixed windows — 23 September to 14 October for presale goods and to 19 October for spot goods — plus obligations that attach the moment the store signs the consumer price experience agreement. For a foreign brand: a store wearing the campaign badge has pre-sold a slice of margin and accepted a month of price obligations before the first order arrives.

## The gate: store, product and service eligibility

Eligibility screening is how the platform keeps the official badge off stores that would damage the event. Three layers apply. At store level, the shop's real experience score is validated on a fixed cut-off date — 12 September for the 2025 event — so a store that passes at enrollment can still fail if the score slips by the cut-off. At product level, each enrolled item must have sold at least one unit in the preceding 90 days (new listings and defined special categories excepted) and must clear a minimum product experience score. At service level, the store must sign the campaign's consumer price experience service agreement, which carries the price protection duty described below. The platform re-validates continuously through the event: eligibility is a maintained state, not a pass at signup.

## The real admission fee is margin, not cash

The official direct discount is the centrepiece mechanic and the true price of a campaign slot. Under the 2025 rules, every enrolled store must activate the official direct discount across all products in its enrolled categories, at a platform-set minimum reduction of 1%, 2%, 5% or 15% depending on category — 15% off in most consumer categories. The merchant funds the whole discount; it is not a platform subsidy, it stacks with store coupons and cross-store vouchers, and no cap applies to how far a store may discount beyond the minimum. There is no separate enrollment fee — deposit and annual fees are store-level costs already paid — but the distinction matters when budgeting: the discount scales with campaign volume, so a store that triples its festival sales triples its discount bill. Model enrollment cost as a variable margin line, not a fixed expense.

## Price validation and the raise-then-cut ceiling

Price validation is the rule set that stops stores engineering a fictional discount. The 2025 rules compare each campaign product's price and effective coupon-inclusive price against the lowest values recorded across a validation window — 20 September to 14 November 2025, including other events inside it. Three ceilings follow. The campaign price may not exceed the window's lowest listed or coupon-inclusive price. During the pre-heating period, a product's effective price may rise no more than 20% above that floor before the event — the raise-then-cut trap, and a breach flags the listing until it is corrected. The campaign price also cannot exceed the same SKU's lowest in-store price across the window, enforcing consistency within the store. Brands that run routine September promotions therefore discover their festival price is already their floor, and the discount must come out of genuine headroom.

## The price lock: a month-long refund obligation

Price protection is the enrolled store's promise that the festival price is the season's lowest. In the 2025 rules, activity products carry mandatory price protection from the moment of payment — the final payment, for presale goods — until 23:59 on 29 November, roughly thirty days after the last sale day; if the price falls inside that window, the platform refunds the difference automatically. Non-activity products in enrolled stores carry the same automatic difference-refund service, without the front-end badge. The obligation is why festival pricing must rest on a real cost floor: a brand that prices too aggressively in wave one and then cuts in wave two pays the difference back to every earlier buyer, without negotiation.

## Service obligations that bind importers harder

Campaign service rules compress delivery and pricing commitments on top of normal store duties. Campaign products must ship free across mainland China, and — the rule specific to importers — every Tmall Global campaign product must be sold tax-included, meaning duty and consumption tax are absorbed into the listed price rather than billed at the border. ## What enrollment actually buys

Official traffic is the consideration on the platform's side of the contract. The 2025 package includes campaign badges on search results, shopping cart and product detail pages; curated venue pages organised by industry, category and theme; ecosystem-wide traffic integration; and dedicated support for stores whose experience scores qualify. Crucially, resource slots are curated, not sold: the platform assembles venue pages from eligible, competitive products, so placement follows eligibility and performance rather than a published price list. This is worth stating plainly because overseas teams often budget for a "slot fee" — the historical kongwei-fee concept belongs to livestreaming commerce, not festival venue placement. A store cannot buy the badge itself; it is granted by enrollment and withdrawn by breach.

## The regulatory counterweight: participation cannot be forced

[Article 23 of SAMR's compliance guide on network platform fee behaviour](https://www.samr.gov.cn/xw/zj/art/2025/art_c35be0a9ae514fd9b37bfd3620b678c1.html), effective 31 July 2025, is the check on enrollment's leverage asymmetry. The article lists eight prohibited practices, among them forcing merchants to take part in promotions, forcing them to purchase platform services or join campaigns on unreasonable terms, charging for basic operating data the platform itself requires, and unreasonable deposits; fee rules must be published on the platform and amended only after public comment. Enrollment is formally voluntary, but traffic allocation makes participation commercially near-compulsory in peak weeks — precisely the asymmetry the guide targets. For a foreign brand, the guide is most useful as an escalation reference when an account manager ties traffic support to unrelated purchases or imposes unpublicised charges.

## Running enrollment from a 1210 cross-border store

For imported goods, the campaign sits inside a second layer of cross-border rules. The [486 Notice on cross-border e-commerce retail imports](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm) exempts these sales from first-import licensing and registration requirements but requires the consumer risk notice before checkout, and the assortment must sit inside the positive list. Pricing must respect the [RMB 5,000 single-transaction and RMB 26,000 annual caps](https://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/201811/t20181129_3079073.htm), inside which duty is zero and VAT and consumption tax are levied at 70% of the standard rate — the tax treatment that lets imported goods price competitively inside a festival whose discounts are merchant-funded. The working sequence for a foreign brand: fix festival pricing on landed cost before the September validation window opens; confirm bonded stock covers the first-wave forecast; hand enrollment execution and score monitoring to the TP partner; and reconcile the month-long price protection exposure against inventory aging after the event.
