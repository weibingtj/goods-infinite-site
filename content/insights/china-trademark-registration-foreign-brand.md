## Why trademark strategy in China is not like the West

In most Western markets, **use** builds rights. In China, **filing** builds them. The system is first-to-file: the party that registers a mark first generally owns it, regardless of who used it first or who owns it abroad.

That single rule reshapes the playbook for any overseas brand. A name you have built for years in the US or Europe can, in China, already be registered by a stranger — who can then block your store, demand a payout, or sell the mark back to you. The fix is boring but essential: **file before you launch**.

> First-to-file means the trademark clock starts before your first sale, not after.

## The filing steps, in order

1. **Clearance search.** Before you commit a name, search CNIPA's database for identical or similar marks in your classes. This catches a blocker early.
2. **Choose classes carefully.** Register your core goods classes and the retail/marketing classes your China business touches (commonly class 35 for retail services).
3. **Prepare the application.** Provide the mark, the applicant (your company — often the HK or home entity), and the specified goods/services.
4. **File with CNIPA.** Direct national filing, or a Madrid application designating China if you want one filing across many countries.
5. **Monitor the opposition window.** Once published, watch the 3-month window; if opposed, respond through counsel.
6. **Maintain it.** Use the mark in China and renew on schedule (every 10 years) to keep it alive.

## Timeline and cost

- **Timeline:** 9–12 months for an unopposed registration. File early — the clock runs while you are still planning the launch.
- **Cost:** official fees are modest; professional search and filing through a China trademark attorney typically runs in the low thousands of USD depending on classes.
- **Madrid route:** if you also need protection in other countries, a Madrid designation of China can be efficient, but China examination still follows CNIPA rules.

## Mistakes that cost brands their name

- **Launching before filing.** The most common and most expensive error — by the time you notice a squatter, your brand is already live and exposed.
- **Filing only the home classes.** Missing class 35 (retail) or the service classes your marketing uses leaves gaps competitors exploit.
- **Assuming a home registration protects you.** It does not; trademark rights are territorial.
- **Ignoring the opposition window.** Not monitoring publication means an opposition lands unanswered.

## FAQ

**Should a foreign brand trademark its name in China before selling?** Yes — ideally before any sales, marketing, or platform onboarding, because China is first-to-file. Whoever files first owns the mark.

**How long does Chinese trademark registration take?** Usually 9–12 months if unopposed. CNIPA examines, publishes for opposition, then grants. File early.

**Can I use my home-country trademark in China?** No. A trademark is territorial; a US/EU registration gives no rights in China. You must file with CNIPA or via Madrid designation.

**Which classes should a brand register?** At minimum your core goods classes plus class 35 (retail services) and relevant service classes. A China attorney maps this to your catalog.

**What if my trademark is already registered by someone else?** Options are limited and costly: buy it back, file an invalidation if bad-faith, or rebrand. Early filing prevents all three.
