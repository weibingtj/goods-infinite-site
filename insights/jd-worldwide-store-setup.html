How to Open a JD Worldwide Store: Eligibility, Onboarding, and Fulfillment for Overseas Brands

A practical walkthrough for overseas brands launching on JD Worldwide (京东全球购): eligibility as a foreign entity, the onboarding sequence, bonded 1210 fulfillment, realistic costs and timeline, and the operational realities of running a cross-border store in China.

Published 2026-08-25 · Last updated 2026-09-04 · By Bing Wei, Operations Director

Why JD Worldwide belongs in a China entry plan

JD Worldwide (京东全球购) is the cross-border arm of JD.com, one of China's two dominant e-commerce groups, and it is purpose-built for overseas brands. Like Tmall Global it lets a foreign company sell into China through bonded import without opening a mainland entity on day one — but JD's owned logistics network and its brand promise of authenticity make it a distinct bet for brands that want speed and trust baked into the channel.

China's cross-border e-commerce market reached ¥2.63 trillion (US$363.4 billion) in 2024, up 10.8% year on year, according to the General Administration of Customs (GACC, 2025). That scale is why a JD Worldwide store is worth a place in almost any overseas brand's China entry plan — but the two marketplaces are not interchangeable, and the fulfillment model is the part most brands underestimate.

JD Worldwide is JD.com's cross-border import marketplace, used by overseas brands to reach Chinese consumers without a mainland entity

JD Worldwide is a cross-border channel: goods clear through the 1210 bonded-import model and the merchant can be an overseas company. A Hong Kong trading company such as GOODSINFINITE TRADE LIMITED can act as the merchant of record — exactly the structure many overseas brands use to avoid the cost and delay of a WFOE at launch.

The platform's edge is logistics. JD operates its own delivery network (JD Logistics), which means bonded orders can move from a free-trade zone to the consumer in 1–3 days in major cities. For a shopper deciding between two imported SKUs, "ships tomorrow" is a real conversion lever.

Eligibility is the set of requirements JD applies, used to confirm a foreign merchant can list and fulfill cross-border

The baseline requirements are similar to other cross-border marketplaces:

  • Overseas entity. A registered company outside mainland China (Hong Kong, US, EU, etc.).
  • Brand rights. Ownership or a valid authorisation for the brand you list.
  • Product compliance. Category-specific filings — for example NMPA cosmetics filing or health-food registration — where required.
  • Fulfillment. A bonded (1210) or direct-mail arrangement connected to the store.

JD has also been aggressive about provenance. Its "country pavilions" programme — over 140 national pavilions showcasing vetted brands (JD, 2024) — signals to shoppers that listed goods are genuine. Brands admitted through a pavilion or the "¥10 billion, 1,000-brands" growth plan get merchandising support, but also face stricter documentation checks.

The onboarding sequence is the step-by-step JD process, used to take a brand from application to a live storefront

  1. Entity & brand documents. Overseas business licence, brand ownership or authorisation, and the legal representative's identification.
  2. Category qualification. Submit category proofs; regulated goods need their filings (e.g. NMPA for cosmetics, SAMR for special foods).
  3. Deposit & contract. Agree the deposit, annual fee and commission, then sign the merchant agreement.
  4. Store build. Configure the flagship store, listings, pricing and brand visuals.
  5. Fulfillment link. Connect a bonded zone (1210) so orders clear per unit and ship domestically fast.
  6. Go live & operate. Launch, then run content, promotions and Mandarin customer service.

Because JD controls fulfillment, step 5 is tighter than on a pure storefront mall: your bonded inventory must be in a zone JD serves, which is where a partner with multi-city bonded capacity matters.

Fulfillment is the bonded logistics link, used to clear and deliver each order from a China free-trade zone

Under the 1210 model supervised by the General Administration of Customs (customs.gov.cn), goods are imported into a bonded zone before sale, then cleared per order and delivered domestically. The Ministry of Commerce (mofcom.gov.cn) and the customs authority periodically expand the cross-border e-commerce retail import positive list, which defines which product categories may enter under this lighter regime — a list every JD Worldwide merchant should check before committing SKUs.

Costs and timeline are the resource commitments, used to budget a JD Worldwide launch

  • Timeline: 1–3 months from complete docs to live store. Regulated categories (cosmetics, supplements, special foods) extend it with product filings.
  • Cost: a refundable deposit (often tied to projected GMV), an annual platform fee, and per-order commission that varies by category. Confirm the exact schedule during onboarding.
  • Fulfillment: bonded warehousing is a recurring cost but unlocks the delivery speed JD shoppers expect.

Operational realities are the day-to-day factors, used to avoid the mistakes that sink cross-border stores

  • Content is the store. Discovery on JD is driven by search and JD's own traffic; a bare listing underperforms. Plan ongoing content and Douyin / Xiaohongshu (RED) seeding to feed it.
  • Compliance pauses launches. Cosmetics, supplements and special foods need filings before listing — start them in parallel with onboarding.
  • The deposit is real capital. Model the refundable deposit into your China working-capital plan.
  • Service is local. Chinese shoppers expect fast, Mandarin customer service; route it before launch.
Open JD Worldwide as a foreign entity with bonded fulfillment, prove demand on JD's trusted channel, then scale into a domestic store when the data justifies it.

FAQ

Can a foreign brand open a JD Worldwide store without a China entity? Yes. JD Worldwide is cross-border; the merchant can be an overseas company with no mainland WFOE, using an overseas licence, brand proof, and bonded fulfillment.

How does JD Worldwide differ from Tmall Global? Both accept foreign merchants, but JD leans on its own logistics and emphasizes authenticity and speed, while Tmall Global runs as a merchant-operated storefront mall. Many brands list on both.

What does it cost to open a JD Worldwide store? A refundable deposit (often tied to GMV), an annual fee, and per-order commission that varies by category. Budget separately for content, operations and bonded fulfillment.

Do I need bonded warehouse fulfillment for JD Worldwide? For the 1210 model, yes — goods sit in a China bonded zone and clear per order. GOODSINFINITE runs bonded fulfillment across five cities.

How long does JD Worldwide onboarding take? Typically 1–3 months from complete docs to live, longer for regulated categories needing product filings.
