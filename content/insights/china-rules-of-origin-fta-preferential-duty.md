## Rules of origin in China are the legal test that decides which of the country's four import duty rates a shipment pays — and preferential FTA origin is governed by an entirely different instrument from the one most importers read first

Origin is one of customs' three technical determinations, alongside classification and customs value, and it is the one foreign brands most often leave to their broker. Under Art. 10 of the Tariff Law of the PRC (adopted 26 April 2024, in force 1 December 2024) China sets four import duty rates — most-favoured-nation, agreement, special preferential and general — and Art. 12 assigns them by origin: MFN for WTO members and treaty partners, agreement rates for goods originating in a party to a treaty or agreement containing tariff-preference clauses **and meeting that agreement's conditions**, special preferential rates for countries covered by a special arrangement, and general rates for everything else including goods of unknown origin. Art. 11 then states the rule that governs all of it: the applicable rate must comply with the corresponding rules of origin, with whole obtainment in one country and, for goods produced in two or more countries, the country of the last substantial transformation.

## Why origin is a pricing decision, not a paperwork step

The reason to care is arithmetic. On a consumer-goods shipment, the gap between the general rate and an agreement rate is frequently ten to twenty percentage points of customs value, and it applies to the full dutiable base before import VAT is calculated on top. Art. 13 of the Tariff Law adds a second-order effect that catches people out: where goods attracting an agreement rate also have a provisional rate, the lower of the two applies, and where the MFN rate is lower than the agreement rate and no provisional rate exists, the MFN rate applies instead. In a year when 935 tariff items carry provisional import rates below MFN, that interaction is worth modelling line by line rather than assuming the FTA is always the best answer.

## The two rulebooks — and the one most importers read by mistake

The single most common error is reading the wrong regulation. The Regulations on the Origin of Import and Export Goods (State Council Decree No. 416, published 3 September 2004, in force 1 January 2005, amended 2 March 2019) look like the general origin law, and they are — but Art. 2(2) states plainly that they **do not apply** to the determination of origin for preferential trade measures, which is instead governed by the relevant international treaty or agreement. So Decree 416 governs MFN treatment, anti-dumping and countervailing duties, safeguards, origin marking, country-specific quotas, tariff quotas, government procurement and trade statistics. Your FTA claim lives in a different instrument.

For preferential origin the general framework is the GACC Provisions on the Administration of Preferential Origin of Import and Export Goods (Decree No. 181, published 8 January 2009, in force 1 March 2009, amended by Decree No. 273 of 28 October 2024 so that it now sits under the Tariff Law). Art. 3 sets the structure: goods shipped **directly** from an agreement partner qualify for that agreement's rate if they are wholly obtained or produced there, or — if not wholly obtained — if they satisfy Arts. 5 and 6. Art. 5 then lists the four tests the individual agreement may use: tariff classification change, regional value content, manufacturing or processing operation, or any other standard the parties agree. Art. 6 is the provision that quietly creates value in regional supply chains: originating goods or materials of one agreement partner used to produce another good in a second partner are treated as originating in that second partner.

Three provisions in Decree 181 are where claims actually fail. Art. 7 confirms that minimal processing for loading, transport, storage or sale does not affect origin — but it does not make the goods originating either. Art. 10 defines "direct transport" strictly: the goods must not pass through a non-party country, and if they do, three conditions must all hold — no handling beyond what is needed to keep them in good condition, a transit stay within the period the agreement sets, and customs supervision in the transit country while temporarily stored. Art. 14 then sets the declaration package: a valid original certificate of origin or the origin declaration the agreement permits, plus the commercial invoice and transport documents, plus through-bill evidence where the goods transited.

## RCEP: the deepest set of rules, and the sharpest deadlines

RCEP is implemented in China by GACC Decree No. 255 (published 23 November 2021, in force 1 January 2022), six chapters and 44 articles. Art. 3 gives three routes to originating status — wholly obtained or produced in a party, produced exclusively from originating materials, or produced with non-originating materials but meeting the product-specific rules of origin published separately by GACC. Art. 5 lists the operations that never confer origin, including simple filtering, screening, sorting, classifying, cutting, grinding, bending, repacking, simple mixing and slaughter. Art. 8 gives a de minimis allowance: non-originating materials that fail the tariff-change test still qualify if their value does not exceed 10% of the FOB value, or — for Chapters 50 to 63 — 10% of total weight.

The procedure is where money is lost. Art. 26 requires the proof of origin, commercial invoice and full-journey transport document, and Art. 28 waives the proof altogether for consignments whose dutiable value does not exceed US$200 — but Art. 28(2) disapplies that waiver if the shipment was split to get under it. Art. 29 is the critical one: if you do not hold a valid proof at import, you must make a **supplementary declaration on originating status before customs formalities are completed** and provide a tax guarantee. Art. 32 then makes failure to do so a ground for refusing the RCEP rate, alongside goods that simply do not qualify, defective proofs, and — a real operational risk — no verification response from the exporting party within 90 days, or no reply to an on-site verification request within 30 days.

## 2026: the Africa zero-tariff measure and its own origin code

The most significant 2026 change is the extension of zero tariffs to African partners. By Tariff Commission Announcement 2026 No. 5 (28 April 2026), China applies zero tariffs, in the form of special preferential rates, to the 20 non-least-developed African countries with which it has diplomatic relations for the period 1 May 2026 to 30 April 2028; for tariff-quota products only the in-quota rate drops to zero. The 33 African least-developed countries already receive zero tariffs on 100% of tariff lines from 1 December 2024. GACC implemented the origin side as Announcement 2026 No. 54 (28 April 2026), and the practical filing instruction is specific: claims follow the GACC Announcement 2021 No. 34 procedure, and the "preferential trade agreement code" field is filled in as **30** (RCEP uses **22**).

The substantive rules in the GACC measures follow the RCEP pattern with different numbers: wholly obtained, produced exclusively from originating materials, or — where non-originating materials are used — meeting the product-specific rules or, for goods not on the PSR list, a regional value content of at least 40%. The de minimis allowance is 10% of FOB value, transit storage in a third country is capped at six months, a certificate is valid for one year, and the no-proof threshold is a dutiable value of US$1,000. Art. 19 repeats the rule that decides most disputes: supplementary declaration must happen before customs formalities are completed, and **once they are completed, tax already levied will not be adjusted**.

## The bonded-zone trap: where agreement rates are silently forfeited

This is the failure mode that matters most for anyone using bonded stock, and it is set out in one paragraph of GACC Announcement 2021 No. 34. Goods entering a special customs supervision area or bonded supervision site do not need to fill in the agreement-benefit fields on the entry record list. The importer must fill them in when the goods are sold into the domestic market. But if the goods' actual state at that point differs from their state on entry by more than the minimal processing or treatment the relevant agreement permits, **the agreement or special preferential rate must not be applied**.

In practice: goods arrive from an RCEP country with a clean claim, sit in a bonded zone, and are then repacked into retail sets, kitted with a non-originating accessory, or relabelled for the Chinese market. On the way out for domestic sale they no longer match the state the origin determination was made on, and the preference is gone — often discovered only at the point of declaration. If you intend to do anything to goods inside bond, model the origin consequence before the goods move, not after. Our own comparison of [bonded zone vs bonded warehouse](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-bonded-zone-vs-bonded-warehouse.html) sets out what each facility type permits; the two questions are linked, because the twelve permitted activity categories inside a comprehensive bonded zone include processing and remanufacturing that a plain bonded warehouse does not allow.

## Locking the answer before you ship

Two mechanisms let you convert an origin opinion into a written position. The first is the advance ruling: Art. 3(2) of the GACC Interim Measures on Advance Ruling Administration (Decree No. 236, in force 1 February 2018) expressly lists the origin or originating status of import and export goods as a ruling matter, alongside classification and customs-value elements. The applicant must be a foreign trade operator registered with customs and involved in the actual transaction, and the application goes to the competent regional customs three months before the planned import. The ruling runs for three years and can be extended on application — under GACC Announcement 2024 No. 32, the extension request is filed between 90 and 30 days before expiry.

The second is the origin pre-determination under Arts. 12 and 13 of State Council Decree 416: a written application before import, a customs decision within 150 days of receiving a complete application, and — if the goods actually imported match the determination and the origin criteria have not changed — no re-determination for three years from the decision. Note the scope difference again: this route belongs to the non-preferential system. Most importers need the advance ruling, not the pre-determination, and the two should not be confused.

## Who signs the proof — and where AEO status enters

A preferential claim rests on a document, and there are two ways to get one. The traditional route is a certificate of origin from a certifying body; under Art. 42(9) of Decree No. 255, China's certifying bodies are the regional and district customs offices and the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade and its local branches. The alternative is an origin declaration issued by an approved exporter, governed by the GACC Measures on the Administration of Approved Exporters (Decree No. 254, in force 1 January 2022) and GACC Announcement 2021 No. 105. The qualifying conditions are demanding: customs AEO advanced-certified enterprise status, demonstrated mastery of the relevant agreement's rules of origin, and a complete originating-status document management system. Approved-exporter status currently applies to the Iceland, Switzerland, Mauritius, New Zealand, Serbia and RCEP agreements.

That requirement is the link between origin work and AEO work: the same advanced certification that reduces inspection frequency is the gate to self-certifying origin on the export side. [China AEO certification and importer of record](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-aeo-certification-importer-of-record.html) covers what the certification involves.

## Records, verification and penalties

Origin claims are auditable retroactively. Art. 41 of Decree No. 255 requires exporters and approved exporters to keep originating-status records for three years from issue of the proof, and importers to keep them for three years from the completion of customs formalities; the Africa measures set the same three-year period for importers. Art. 30 lets customs verify by demanding supplementary information from the importer, the overseas exporter or the producer, or from the exporting party's certifying body, and — with the exporting party's agreement — by on-site verification abroad. On the penalty side, Art. 23 of State Council Decree 416 imposes fines of RMB 5,000 to 100,000 for obtaining an export certificate of origin with false materials or forging, altering, trading or stealing one, with confiscation of illegal gains, and Art. 24 requires origin marking that is inconsistent with the determined origin to be corrected.

## A working sequence for a first FTA claim

1. **Confirm the code and the claim together.** Classification and origin are decided on the same shipment and interact; use the [China HS code classification guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-hs-code-classification-importers.html) to settle the tariff line first, then test each candidate agreement against that line's product-specific rule.
2. **Read the right instrument.** If you are claiming an FTA rate, Decree 416 is not your rulebook — Decree 181 and the agreement-specific measures are.
3. **Test the three routes in order.** Wholly obtained, exclusively from originating materials, or product-specific rule or RVC. Gather the bill of materials evidence you would need to prove it.
4. **Check the value arithmetic where RVC applies**, using the FOB-based formula in the agreement and remembering that the [customs value](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-customs-valuation-royalties.html) and the FOB used for RVC are different calculations.
5. **Map the transport route** against the direct-transport rule before booking, not after — including any transit storage and how long it lasts.
6. **Decide where the goods will be held, and what will be done to them there**, against the Announcement 2021 No. 34 rule on post-entry processing.
7. **File the claim correctly**: agreement-benefit fields on the declaration, the proof of origin or approved-exporter declaration, invoice and full transport documents, through-bill evidence if transited.
8. **If you genuinely cannot produce the proof at import, make the supplementary declaration and post the guarantee before formalities are completed.** After that point, on the current rules, the money is gone.

## FAQ

**What are the rules of origin for importing into China?** Two systems. Non-preferential origin under State Council Decree No. 416 (whole obtainment, otherwise last substantial transformation, with tariff-classification change as the basic test) governs MFN, anti-dumping, quotas, marking and statistics — and expressly excludes preferential measures. Preferential origin under a free trade agreement is governed by GACC Decree No. 181 and the agreement's own implementing measures.

**How many FTAs does China apply agreement rates under in 2026?** The 2026 Tariff Adjustment Plan applies agreement tariff rates to goods originating in 34 trading partners under 24 free trade agreements and preferential arrangements, and grants zero-tariff treatment on 100% of tariff lines to 43 least-developed countries.

**Can I claim an FTA rate after the goods have cleared?** No. RCEP Art. 29 requires a supplementary declaration plus a tax guarantee before customs formalities are completed, and Art. 32 makes failure to do so a ground for refusal. The 2026 Africa measures state that once formalities are completed, tax already levied will not be adjusted.

**What documents do I need?** Under Decree 181 Art. 14: the valid original certificate of origin or permitted origin declaration, the commercial invoice and transport documents, plus through-bill evidence for transited goods. RCEP Art. 26 adds the full-journey transport document; consignments at or below US$200 are exempt from the proof under Art. 28.

**What is an approved exporter?** An enterprise recognised under GACC Decree No. 254 and Announcement 2021 No. 105 that may issue its own origin declarations. Conditions include AEO advanced-certified status, mastery of the agreement's origin rules and a complete originating-status document system.

**Does bonded storage affect my FTA rate?** It can. GACC Announcement 2021 No. 34 requires the agreement-benefit declaration when goods are sold domestically, and bars the agreement rate where the goods' state on exit differs from their state on entry by more than the agreement's permitted minimal processing.

**How do I get a binding origin decision in advance?** File an advance ruling on originating status under GACC Decree No. 236 Art. 3(2), three months before the planned import, with the competent regional customs; it is valid for three years and extendable.
