## What a China market entry budget actually covers

A China market entry budget is the total cost a foreign brand must fund between deciding to sell in China and receiving its first repeat order, and it divides into six layers: validation, intellectual property and legal entity, product compliance, channel entry, demand creation, and logistics with working capital. Brands that treat entry as a single "platform fee" misread the spend: the visible costs are deposits, the dangerous ones are recurring.

China's own registration charges are close to negligible. A trademark filed electronically costs RMB 270 per class and protects the mark for ten years ([CNIPA Trademark Office fee schedule](https://sbj.cnipa.gov.cn/sbj/sbsq/sfbz/)), and registering an overseas food manufacturer with customs is free. What a China budget buys is evidence and risk transfer — testing, translation, agency work, platform access, media and inventory.

## Layer 1 — Validation and market research

Market validation in China is the spend required to prove that one product at one price can win a defined consumer segment before inventory is committed, and it is the smallest layer and the one that most reliably saves money. National statistics releases, customs import records by tariff line and public platform review data are free. The three things worth paying for are an import-record subscription showing who already imports competing products and through which port, sample import and testing of the finished pack, and a short test-listing that measures real demand.

## Layer 2 — Intellectual property and the legal entity

China trademark registration is the grant of a ten-year exclusive right to a mark within one of 45 Nice classes, issued by the CNIPA Trademark Office, and it is the cheapest mandatory item in the plan: RMB 270 per class electronically for ten goods or services, plus RMB 27 per additional item. Time is the real cost: roughly seven to nine months from filing to registration for a clean application. A mark used abroad carries no priority in China, so file early, in the classes and Chinese transliteration you will sell under.

The entity is the first large fork. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise is a Chinese limited liability company owned entirely by foreign shareholders: no statutory minimum capital in most sectors since 2014 and no official fee for the licence, but declared capital must be paid in within five years under Article 47 of the revised Company Law effective 1 July 2024. Advisers in 2026 quote setup fees of roughly USD 3,000 to USD 15,000 and timelines from four to six weeks to three or four months ([MSA Asia, WFOE guide 2026](https://www.msadvisory.com/service/wholly-foreign-owned-enterprise-wfoe)). The alternative is not to incorporate at all and to work through an importer that holds the licence and the customs relationship — see our comparison of a [China market entry agency and a WFOE](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-market-entry-agency-vs-wfoe.html).

## Layer 3 — Product compliance and testing

Product compliance cost is the money spent proving to Chinese regulators that a specific product may lawfully be sold, and it is dominated by mandatory lab testing, artwork and translation rather than by government fees. The clearest example of a fee you should never pay is food facility registration.

Compliance becomes expensive only by category. Cosmetic testing of an ordinary imported product is quoted at RMB 4,000 to RMB 7,800 per formula, while special cosmetics needing human safety and efficacy testing, such as sunscreen or whitening products, typically run RMB 15,000 to RMB 38,000, so a special-cosmetics registration can exceed RMB 100,000 ([2026 cosmetics filing and testing cost guide](http://www.huayutest.com/zixun/108033.html)). Health food runs through SAMR rather than NMPA and is the longest path, covered in our [health food "blue hat" guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-health-food-registration-blue-hat.html); food needs its own dossier, described in our [GACC food registration walkthrough](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/gacc-food-registration.html). Every category also pays for a Chinese-language label, per our [labelling requirements guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-import-product-labelling-requirements.html).

## Layer 4 — Channel entry: deposits, annual fees and commission

Channel entry cost is the deposit, annual fee and sales commission a platform or retailer charges for access to its buyers, and it is the first genuinely large cash commitment in the plan — on Tmall Global it has three parts: a deposit, an annual technical service fee, and a commission on completed sales.

<div class="statcard"><span class="n">RMB 30,000–60,000</span><span class="l">Annual technical service fee on Tmall Global, in addition to a deposit of RMB 50,000 (RMB 150,000 in some store types and categories) and 2–5% commission on completed sales</span><span class="src">Source: Tmall Global merchant fee standard and rule centre, 2026 (annual fee set by top-level category; no annual-fee refund policy; first month not charged)</span></div>

Two details catch brands out: the deposit depends on store type and category — the public FAQ states RMB 50,000 as normal while the rule centre states RMB 150,000 — so confirm it in writing before modelling, and selling across categories is charged at the highest single annual-fee tier with no stacking. Commission is the cost of scaling: at 2 to 5 percent of gross merchandise value, a brand doing RMB 20 million a year pays RMB 400,000 to RMB 1 million, more than ten times the deposit.

On the import side, cross-border retail imports under the 1210 bonded model are capped at RMB 5,000 per order and RMB 26,000 per person per year, with duty temporarily zero and import VAT and consumption tax at 70 percent of the statutory rate — 9.1 percent effective for a good carrying 13 percent VAT ([GACC briefing on cross-border retail import rules](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/2026-01/13/article_2026041015241860378.html)). General trade pays duty up front plus 13 percent import VAT, but that VAT is creditable input tax, so the unrecoverable cost is duty and any consumption tax — modelled in our [landed-cost walkthrough](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-import-taxes-landed-cost.html) and compared in [1210 versus 9610 versus 0110](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/1210-vs-9610-vs-0110.html).

## Layer 5 — Demand creation and channel support

Demand-creation cost is what you spend to be found and chosen after the product is listed, and in China it is normally the largest recurring line because the platforms are marketplaces with paid discovery rather than merchandisers that buy your stock. Fund content and paid traffic from the first month; a store with no traffic plan is an expense, not a channel.

The 2026 Xiaohongshu market gives a usable scale: seeded notes are quoted at RMB 100 to RMB 1,000 from accounts under 10,000 followers, RMB 500 to RMB 8,000 from accounts of 10,000 to 100,000 followers, and from RMB 15,000 for top accounts. Paid search prices clicks at roughly RMB 1.5 to RMB 5, retainers for running an account run RMB 5,000 to RMB 30,000 a month, and prices rise 30 to 50 percent in the Double 11 and 618 windows ([2026 Xiaohongshu cost breakdown](https://www.waouwangluo.com/xiaohongshu/504.html)). Live commerce sits on a separate budget — retainers of RMB 50,000 to RMB 150,000 plus 15 to 30 percent commission, per our guide to [hiring a China livestream agency](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-livestream-agency-foreign-brands.html) — and brands not running marketing in-house use a [China social commerce agency](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-social-commerce-agency.html).

## Layer 6 — Logistics, bonded stock and working capital

Logistics and working-capital cost is the money tied up in moving, storing and financing inventory between your factory and the Chinese consumer, and it is the layer most often underestimated because freight is only one of five charges.

Storage in the 2026 bonded-warehouse market is billed by volume at roughly RMB 1.5 to RMB 5 per cubic metre per day, and some operators surcharge about 20 percent once stock passes 60 days. Inbound handling runs RMB 20 to RMB 50 per pallet, outbound order processing RMB 3 to RMB 8 per order including packaging, and customs brokerage RMB 300 to RMB 700 per declaration; deposits run from RMB 5,000 for warehousing and RMB 10,000 for duty ([2026 bonded warehouse cost breakdown](https://www.51w2c.com/details_id_12413.html)), and our [bonded warehouse cost guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/bonded-warehouse-cost-process.html) covers how port choice moves them. Working capital is the invisible half: stock typically sits 60 to 120 days before it sells, so the cash tied up in inventory usually exceeds every fee in layers one to four combined.

At an indicative RMB 7 to the dollar, the lean route lands in the region of USD 60,000 to USD 220,000 of spend in year one before inventory, and the full route well above USD 400,000. The point is the ratio, not the total: on the lean route demand creation is the largest line and government fees are almost invisible, which is why cutting the marketing budget removes the only spending that generates revenue.

## Where entry budgets most often go wrong

Three mistakes account for most of the overspend:

1. **Paying for free government registrations.** Imported-food facility registration costs nothing through the official system, and customs has warned about lookalike sites that charge for it.
2. **Filing the trademark too late.** Seven to nine months is longer than most launch plans assume, and a blocked Chinese name at month eight forces a packaging rebrand.
3. **Declaring capital you cannot fund.** Since 1 July 2024 the declared amount must be paid in within five years, so a figure chosen as a signal of commitment becomes a legal obligation.

## FAQ

**How much does it cost to enter the China market?** There is no single number, because the budget has six layers: validation and research, intellectual property and legal entity, product compliance and testing, channel entry, demand creation, and logistics with working capital. Government fees are the smallest part — a China trademark costs RMB 270 per class electronically and imported-food manufacturer registration is free of charge. For a single-category brand entering through cross-border e-commerce with a contracted importer, a realistic first-year envelope runs from roughly RMB 400,000 to RMB 1.2 million including platform deposit, testing and the first marketing programme, before inventory.

**Do I have to set up a Chinese company to sell in China?** No. Cross-border e-commerce allows an overseas or Hong Kong entity to open a Tmall Global or JD Worldwide store without a mainland company, and an import agent or distributor can act as the importer of record for general trade. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise is only necessary where you want to invoice Chinese customers in renminbi, hold your own import licence, employ staff onshore or control the customs relationship directly. If you do incorporate, the government charges no fee for the business licence, and there has been no statutory minimum registered capital in most sectors since 2014.

**Are Chinese government registration fees a major cost?** No, and they are frequently overpaid because intermediaries sell them as a service. Imported-food overseas manufacturer registration through cifer.singlewindow.cn is the only official channel and costs nothing, and customs has publicly warned that any site charging a fee is not the official system. Trademark registration is RMB 270 per class for electronic filing. The real compliance cost is mandatory lab testing, translation, label artwork and the China responsible person required for cosmetics and health food.

**What does it cost to open a store on Tmall Global?** Tmall Global's merchant fee standard is three parts: a deposit, an annual technical service fee, and a commission on completed sales. The standard deposit is RMB 50,000, rising to RMB 150,000 for some store types and categories; the annual technical service fee is RMB 30,000 or RMB 60,000 depending on the top-level category, with the highest tier applied if you sell across categories and no stacking; and commission runs 2 to 5 percent by leaf category. Tmall Global publishes no annual-fee refund policy, and the first month of a newly opened store is not charged.

**Which China market entry costs can be deferred or avoided?** Market research, trademark filing and the compliance dossier are the cheapest items and should never be deferred, because they determine whether the rest of the budget is spent correctly. What can be deferred is the onshore entity, general-trade import and full-range inventory: a brand can validate demand through a bonded cross-border store with an importer of record, prove repeat purchase on a limited SKU set, and only then fund an entity, a national listing and a scaled marketing programme.
