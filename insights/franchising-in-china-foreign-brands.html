Franchising in China: Rules for Foreign Brands

Franchising in China is not a licensing shortcut. The franchisor must be a Chinese enterprise that has already traded two direct-operated stores for more than a year before it may sell a single franchise, and the Supreme People's Court confirmed in August 2026 that contracts signed by unqualified franchisors do not hold. This guide sets out the two-store gate, the 15-day filing, the 30-day disclosure file, the statutory cooling-off right and the three routes a foreign brand can actually take.

Published 2026-11-20 · Last updated 2026-11-20 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

Franchising in China is a licence to a Chinese enterprise, never from abroad

Franchising in China, formally commercial franchising (商业特许经营), is a contractual arrangement in which an enterprise owning a registered trademark, logo, patent or proprietary know-how licenses those resources to another operator who trades under a uniform business model and pays a franchise fee. Article 3 of the Commercial Franchise Administration Regulation adds the operative restriction: the licensor must be an enterprise, and "units and individuals other than enterprises" may not act as franchisors (State Council Order 485, in force 1 May 2007). The Supreme People's Court confirmed the point in August 2026, upholding a voided contract signed by an individual business.

For a foreign brand the architecture is settled before any commercial question: the party that signs, files and carries the liability must be a mainland company — your own WFOE, a joint venture, or a Chinese master franchisee. Franchising itself is not a restricted activity, so the mainland franchisor can be wholly foreign-owned (Special Administrative Measures for Foreign Investment Access, 2024 edition). What it cannot be is a foreign company with no Chinese registration behind it.

The two-store, one-year gate sets your timeline, not your lawyer

The two-store, one-year rule requires a franchisor to own and operate at least two direct-operated outlets that have each been trading for more than a year before it may sell a single franchise. Article 7(2) is one sentence long and it is the most consequential provision in Chinese franchising. Run the arithmetic on a newly incorporated WFOE: lease two sites, fit them out, trade them for twelve months, and the earliest lawful franchise signature lands somewhere in months 18 to 24 after incorporation — before the filing clock is even started.

Franchising without the condition is not a paperwork slip. Article 24 provides for an order to correct, confiscation of illegal gains, a fine of RMB 100,000 to 500,000 and a public announcement, and it applies the same penalty range to non-enterprises acting as franchisors.

RMB 500,000Maximum fine, plus confiscation of franchise fees and a public announcement, for franchising without two direct-operated stores open for more than a yearSource: Commercial Franchise Administration Regulation (State Council Order 485), Article 24, 2007

This is why "license the brand, collect fees, let locals do the work" fails on contact with the Chinese market. The condition attaches to the franchisor, not the franchisee, so whichever entity you designate must hold the leases, the payroll and twelve months of trading records in its own name.

Filing is due 15 days after the first contract, and the register is public

Franchise filing (备案) is the registration of a franchisor and its franchise system with the commerce authority, required within 15 days of signing the first franchise contract. Article 8 splits jurisdiction: a franchisor operating inside one province files with the provincial commerce department, while one operating across provinces files with MOFCOM. The file is not a form — business licence, sample contract, operations manual, market plan, and a written commitment that the Article 7 conditions are met (State Council Order 485, Articles 8–10).

Two consequences matter commercially. The filed list is published and kept current under Article 10, so any prospective franchisee — or competitor — can verify your status in minutes; that cuts both ways when you are choosing a master franchisee. The obligation is continuing: Article 19 requires an annual report each first quarter on contracts signed the year before, fined up to RMB 10,000, or RMB 50,000 where serious. Failing to file at all starts at RMB 10,000 to 50,000 and escalates to RMB 50,000 to 100,000 with a public announcement under Article 25.

The 30-day disclosure file is the part that gets litigated

Pre-contract disclosure is the written information package a franchisor must hand a prospective franchisee at least 30 days before signature, covering twelve specified categories. Article 21 sets the clock and Article 22 sets the content, and the content is heavier than most foreign brands expect: two years of audited financial statements, all franchise-related litigation and arbitration over the last five years, any record of major illegal business conduct by the franchisor or its legal representative, and — the item that slows most negotiations — the number, location and performance assessment of existing franchisees in China (MOFCOM text of Order 485, Articles 21–22).

30 daysMinimum written disclosure period before a franchise contract may be signed, with the contract text supplied at the same timeSource: State Council Order 485, Article 21 (in force 1 May 2007)

Article 23 adds the trap. Information must be true, accurate and complete, material changes must be notified, and concealment or misrepresentation lets the franchisee rescind the contract. Penalties under Article 28 run to RMB 10,000 to 50,000 on a verified complaint, and RMB 50,000 to 100,000 where serious. In practice it is a diligence pack on your own business, prepared in Chinese and English, and it takes longer than the contract.

The cooling-off right cannot be contracted away

A cooling-off right (冷静期) is the franchisee's statutory entitlement to terminate unilaterally within a period after signature, which the contract is required to provide for. Article 12 obliges the parties to agree that window. On 12 August 2026 the Supreme People's Court published seven franchise cases and made the limit explicit: where a contract omits the clause entirely, the right still exists as a statutory right and may be exercised within a reasonable period while the licensed resources remain unused (Supreme People's Court franchise cases, August 2026). The same batch upheld termination where a franchisor's filing was revoked mid-term and the change was not disclosed — compliance status is a continuing representation, not a one-off filing.

Budget for the churn: a franchisee who signs in March and exits in May after fitting out a site is a normal first-year event, and the deposit terms are where that risk is priced.

Contract terms the regulation writes for you

A China franchise contract is a contract of prescribed content, and Article 13 fixes the minimum term at three years unless the franchisee agrees otherwise. Article 11 lists eleven mandatory subjects, from fee types and payment methods through to consumer-protection and liability allocation. Article 14 requires you to deliver an operations manual and then keep supplying guidance, technical support and training for the life of the agreement. Article 16 requires any fee collected before signature to be explained in writing, including its purpose and the conditions and method of refund. Article 17 bans earnings claims in recruitment advertising, fined at RMB 30,000 to 100,000 and up to RMB 300,000 with aggravation under Article 27.

For an imported brand the manual is the hard deliverable: a uniform system a Chinese operator can run without you on site, which forces decisions on pricing, range, service standards and supply, including how imported stock clears customs.

Three routes, and what each one actually costs

Master franchising is an arrangement in which a Chinese company takes the right to develop and sub-franchise your brand within a territory, becoming the franchisor of record for local units. It is the fastest route and the one most foreign brands choose, because the master franchisee may already satisfy the two-store condition and the filing requirement. The price is control: your royalty is negotiated with an intermediary that owns the unit economics, and if that intermediary is not itself a qualified franchisor the contracts beneath it are exposed.

Wholesale distribution triggers none of these obligations: no franchise fee, disclosure file or cooling-off period arises. For most first-entry brands the franchise question is premature by two years, and a China distributor relationship is the correct instrument until local demand is proven.

Whether it is worth it, in market numbers

Franchising is the dominant expansion format in Chinese retail and food service, and the growth is concentrated in formats that are already systemised. The China Chain Store & Franchise Association's 2026 ranking of the largest chains recorded 2025 sales of RMB 2.07 trillion across 289,700 stores — a net addition of 32,500 stores, or 12.4%, in a single year (CCFA China Chain Top 100, published 15 June 2026). Store count grew several times faster than sales, which is the arithmetic of a market where expansion is the growth engine and per-store productivity is under pressure.

289,700 storesOutlets operated by China's 100 largest chains in 2025, a net 32,500-store gain in one year — growth that comes from new doors rather than from selling more per storeSource: CCFA China Chain Top 100 (2026 release, 2025 data)

Franchising scales store count quickly in China; it does not by itself scale revenue per store, and the fee income booked in years one and two is small against the cost of the two direct stores that made the model lawful.

What this means for a foreign brand's entry plan

A China franchise plan is a sequencing decision before it is a legal one: two owned stores and twelve months of trading come before the first franchise, and the entity that clears that gate must be your mainland company. Brands that plan backwards from fee income usually discover the timeline after signing a lease. Brands that plan forwards treat the two stores as paid market research — the only lawful evidence that the model survives Chinese rents, labour costs and consumer expectations.

FAQ

Can a foreign company franchise directly into China from abroad? Not in any workable sense. Article 3 limits the franchisor to an enterprise holding trademark, logo, patent or know-how resources, and the filing duty, the two-store condition and the penalties all attach to the franchisor of record. That party is in practice a mainland company — your own WFOE or joint venture, or a Chinese master franchisee that already qualifies. An overseas parent can own the brand and license it to that mainland entity, but it cannot itself sell franchises to Chinese operators.

How long before a newly established WFOE can start franchising? Roughly 18 to 24 months. Two direct-operated outlets must each trade for more than a year before the entity may act as franchisor, and the 15-day filing window starts after the first contract. Brands needing to move faster use a master franchisee that is already qualified.

Do we have to file if we have only one franchisee? Yes. Article 8 requires filing within 15 days of the first franchise contract; the threshold is the first signature, not a volume test. File with the provincial commerce department if you operate inside one province, with MOFCOM if you operate across provinces.

Is a China franchise filing the same as registering our trademark? No. The trademark is the business resource that makes you eligible to be a franchisor; the filing registers your franchise system with the commerce authority. Article 31 refers trademark and patent licensing back to the trademark and patent laws, so both regimes run in parallel.

What happens if our Chinese master franchisee is not properly filed? Your exposure is contractual and reputational before it is regulatory. A franchisor without the two-store qualification faces RMB 100,000 to 500,000 in fines and confiscation of fees under Article 24, and the Supreme People's Court in August 2026 upheld a ruling that such a contract was void. The filed list is public under Article 10, so the status is verifiable in minutes.
