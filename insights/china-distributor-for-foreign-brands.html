Finding a China Distributor for Foreign Brands: Agent, Partner, or Local Entity

A China distributor puts your product on shelves and platforms across the mainland — but the term covers three very different models. Compare an import agent, a distribution partner, and a local entity to choose the right reach without losing control.

Published 2026-08-31 · Last updated 2026-09-04 · By Bing Wei, Operations Director

Why "find a China distributor" is the wrong question on its own

Every overseas brand eventually hears the same advice: "you need a China distributor." The problem is that "distributor" in China covers at least three structurally different arrangements — an import agent that clears and fulfills, a distribution partner that owns resale into retail, and a local entity you control outright. Picking the wrong one wastes margin, cedes brand control, or delays launch by months. This guide compares the three so you can choose reach without losing the upside. Our China market entry guide places distribution inside the full entry sequence.

A China distributor is a local counterparty that takes your product to Chinese end buyers through retail, pharmacy, or platform channels, either as an agent, a reseller, or your own entity

A China distributor (经销商/渠道商) is any local party that moves your goods from the port to the Chinese consumer — but the legal and commercial shape varies enormously. At one end, an import agent simply lands and fulfills your product while you keep the customer relationship. At the other, a distributor buys or takes title and resells into supermarkets, pharmacies, specialty retail, or platform storefronts, owning the route to the buyer. Knowing which model you are negotiating is the first step to a sane contract. The distinction matters because China's cross-border e-commerce trade reached about RMB 2.38 trillion in 2023 per the General Administration of Customs, and much of that flow is routed through exactly these intermediaries (GACC).

Model 1: The import agent (lowest commitment, fastest start)

An import agent helps you bring product INTO China without owning downstream resale. Services typically include:

  • Customs clearance and declaration (general trade 0110 or cross-border 1210).
  • Bonded warehousing in cities such as Tianjin, Shanghai, Ningbo, Guangzhou, and Qingdao.
  • 1210 bonded fulfillment for Tmall Global, JD Worldwide, or Douyin cross-border.
  • China e-commerce operations support.

The flow is foreign brand → Chinese consumer, but the agent stays behind the scenes. Our import agent vs. sourcing agent explainer clarifies the direction of flow, since the two terms are constantly confused.

Model 2: The distribution partner (real channel reach, shared control)

A distribution partner actually places product into retail and platform channels and often takes title. Choose this when you want physical or online shelf presence without building your own sales force. Trade fairs are the primary hunting ground: the Canton Fair drew over 244,000 overseas buyers at its 135th session in spring 2024, and country pavilions at CIIE (China International Import Expo) are purpose-built for exactly this matchmaking (MOFCOM). Vet partners on category experience,现有 network, and whether they can carry the required registration.

Model 3: Your own local entity (maximum control, maximum commitment)

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) lets you import, hold inventory, hire locally, and run domestic stores — full control of brand, pricing, and customer data. The cost is time and capital: registration under the PRC Company Law, a registered address, and months of setup. Our WFOE setup guide details the timeline. Most disciplined brands defer this until volume proves the market.

How to choose: a decision frame

  • Prove demand first → import agent + cross-border (1210). Lowest risk, fastest.
  • Enter retail/pharmacy → distribution partner with category reach.
  • Scale and control → WFOE once data justifies the spend.

The pattern that protects margin is to start upstream and move downstream only on evidence, rather than signing a multi-year distribution lock-in on day one.

Compliance you cannot delegate away

A distributor cannot legally sell regulated product without the approvals in place, so registration is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have:

  • Food → GACC overseas-manufacturer registration (Decree 248) (GACC).
  • Cosmetics → NMPA filing (general) or registration (special) under CSAR (NMPA).
  • Electronics / many consumer goods → CCC certification where applicable (SAMR).

Protecting your brand through the relationship

The single most overlooked step is trademark registration: China is first-to-file, so whoever registers the mark owns it. Register before any distribution or platform activity. Then use a written agreement with clear territory, pricing, and termination clauses, keep control of e-commerce accounts where possible, and retain an importer of record for compliance. Our trademark registration guide covers the filing traps that have cost brands their own name.

FAQ

What is the difference between a China distributor and an import agent? An import agent helps you bring product INTO China (customs, bonded warehousing, 1210 fulfillment, e-commerce operations) but does not usually own downstream resale. A distributor buys or takes title and resells into retail, pharmacy, or platform channels, owning the route to the end buyer.

How do foreign brands find a China distributor? Common routes are industry trade fairs (the Canton Fair drew over 244,000 overseas buyers at its 135th session in spring 2024), chambers of commerce and country desks, platform partner directories, and vetted import-agent networks. Verify track record, category experience, and registration capacity.

Should a foreign brand use a distributor or set up its own local entity? A distributor is faster and needs less capital, at the cost of margin and control. A WFOE gives full control but takes months and committed spend. Most brands prove demand through an agent or distributor first, then form a WFOE when volume justifies it.

Do I need product registration before appointing a China distributor? For regulated categories, yes. Food needs GACC registration, cosmetics need NMPA filing/registration, and many goods need CCC. A distributor cannot legally sell regulated product without these in place.

How should a foreign brand protect its brand with a China distributor? Register your trademark in China first (first-to-file rule), use a clear distribution agreement with territory and termination clauses, keep control of e-commerce accounts where possible, and retain an importer of record for compliance.
