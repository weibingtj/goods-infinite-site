## China import freight is the inbound leg that moves a foreign brand's goods from the factory gate to a Chinese bonded port or airport before customs clearance

Most mode advice is written for exporters leaving China, not brands arriving. An inbound plan must answer two linked questions: which mode, and which bonded city the goods will stock. The two interact — a cheap ocean rate into the wrong port adds a costly truck leg. This guide compares the three modes with 2025 official data; see [China Bonded Zone vs Bonded Warehouse](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-bonded-zone-vs-bonded-warehouse.html) for what happens after arrival.

## Sea freight is the default mode for consumer-goods brands entering China, moving the bulk of bonded stock through deep-water container ports

Ocean wins on cost per unit for anything heavy, bulky or shelf-stable. Tianjin, the North China gateway, handled 24.02 million TEU in 2025, up 3.2 percent and ranked eighth globally, alongside 595 million tonnes of total cargo and some 150 shipping routes, according to the [Tianjin Municipal Development and Reform Commission](https://fzgg.tj.gov.cn/xxfb/xwxx/202605/t20260518_7301085.html).

<div class="statcard"><span class="n">24.02 million TEU</span><span class="l">Tianjin Port container throughput in 2025, up 3.2 percent year on year and ranked eighth in the world, serving North China across about 150 shipping routes</span><span class="src">Source: Tianjin Municipal Development and Reform Commission, May 2026 (fzgg.tj.gov.cn)</span></div>

For an importer that means weekly sailings from major origins, mature cold-chain handling, and bonded terminals where goods enter customs supervision inside the port area. The trade-offs are long lead times — market practice of 35 to 45 days from North Europe — schedule variability, and working capital at sea.

## Rail freight on the China-Europe network is the mid-speed option for European-origin goods too slow for ocean lead times but too heavy for air rates

The mode is now scheduled infrastructure. China State Railway Group reports annual China-Europe freight trains grew from 1,702 in 2016 to [20,022 trains in 2025](http://www.china-railway.com.cn/xwzx/ywsl/202607/t20260728_158534.html), a 10.8-fold increase, while broader China-Europe and Central Asia services ran 34,000 trains carrying 3.17 million TEU in 2025, up 9.8 and 7.6 percent.

<div class="statcard"><span class="n">20,022 trains</span><span class="l">China-Europe freight trains operated in 2025, up from 1,702 in 2016 — a 10.8-fold increase in ten years, with tariffs now more than 40 percent below the launch-period level</span><span class="src">Source: China State Railway Group Co., Ltd., June 2026 (china-railway.com.cn)</span></div>

Three facts make rail relevant to importers. Trains carried USD 67.7 billion of goods in 2025, officially including European agricultural products, chemicals and consumer goods inbound to China. The return-to-departure ratio has exceeded 85 percent for a long unbroken run, so westbound capacity is a scheduled, loaded service. And tariffs are down more than 40 percent from the launch period, closing part of the gap to ocean.

## Air freight is the premium mode for launches, stockout recovery and short shelf-life goods — a narrow pipe, not a bulk lane

Air capacity into China is real but small. Tianjin airport handled 133,000 tonnes of cargo and mail in 2025, down 3.1 percent, according to the [Tianjin Municipal Transport Commission](https://jtys.tj.gov.cn/ZWGK6002/JTTJ4958/TJBG216/202602/t20260202_7237408.html) — less in a year, in tonnes, than the port moves in hours, which is why per-kilo air rates sit in a different universe from ocean rates.

<div class="statcard"><span class="n">133,000 tonnes</span><span class="l">Air cargo and mail handled at Tianjin airport in 2025, down 3.1 percent year on year — air is a capacity-constrained premium lane, not a bulk alternative</span><span class="src">Source: Tianjin Municipal Transport Commission, 2025 transport indicators, February 2026 (jtys.tj.gov.cn)</span></div>

Used surgically, air earns its premium: a launch with a fixed date, a top-up rescuing a fast mover before a livestream slot, or perishables where every extra day is a quality risk. As a default lane it destroys the unit economics of most consumer categories.

The arithmetic: divide the faster mode's premium by the days it saves and compare with the SKU's daily contribution on sale. If contribution exceeds the daily cost of speed, the faster mode pays for itself; if not, the slower mode wins even when the rate gap looks trivial.

## Mode choice and the 1210 bonded model are separate decisions: any inbound mode can feed bonded stock, but the goods and the warehouse must qualify

Importers sometimes assume bonded e-commerce stock must arrive by sea. It does not. Under the [six-ministry circular Shang Cai Fa 486 of 2018](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm), the 1210 model is goods shipped in bulk into a customs special supervision area, then cleared order by order — the circular regulates who may sell, what and where, not the transport document. Rail- and air-arrived stock enters on the same terms.

Two checks bind. The warehouse must be a lawful bonded location — a special supervision area or type-B centre. Perishables carry their own regime: under [GACC Order No. 249](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/2021-04/16/article_2025121221035693974.html), in force 1 January 2022, port inspection of chilled and frozen food covers core temperature, ambient temperature and temperature-control equipment — see [China Cold-Chain Warehousing](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-cold-chain-warehousing.html).

## Intermodal legs decide the real landed cost: the cheapest ocean rate can be undone by inland haulage

A rate per TEU is not a landed cost — terminal handling, customs entry and bonded trucking attach at both ends. The [National Railway Administration reported](https://www.peoplerail.com/rail/show-2958-567136-1.html) rail-water intermodal transport of 17.38 million TEU in 2025, up 15.1 percent, and a New Western Land-Sea Corridor volume of 1.42 million TEU, up 47.5 percent.

<div class="statcard"><span class="n">17.38 million TEU</span><span class="l">China's rail-water intermodal freight volume in 2025, up 15.1 percent year on year, with the New Western Land-Sea Corridor adding 1.42 million TEU, up 47.5 percent</span><span class="src">Source: National Railway Administration, 2026 work conference data on 2025 (peoplerail.com)</span></div>

Quote the whole chain — origin haulage, main leg, destination handling into the bonded warehouse — on one liability basis where possible. Tianjin's sea-rail "one bill of lading" services into central and western China are a worked example of the port internalising exactly this problem.

## A practical sequencing for a first-time entrant: bond by ocean, measure, then buy speed where the data says so

Ship the first batch by ocean into the bonded city closest to your demand — [China Market Entry by Region](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-market-entry-regional-demand.html) maps where that is — with enough stock to measure sell-through, sized against the quota caps in [Cross-Border E-Commerce Stock Planning](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/cross-border-ecommerce-stock-planning-china.html). Then let the data buy speed: fast movers get rail top-ups, the launch-critical few fly. Inverting it — air on a speculative first shipment, or a year of slow stock behind fast-mode premiums — burns the budget.

## FAQ

**How long does sea freight from Europe to North China take?** Market practice is roughly 35 to 45 days port to port from North Europe to Tianjin, plus inland legs, against 15 to 20 days for rail and 3 to 8 days door to door for air.

**Is China-Europe rail freight cheaper than sea freight?** No. Rail sits between ocean and air on price and transit. Tariffs are more than 40 percent below the 2016 launch level, and trains carried USD 67.7 billion of goods in 2025, including European consumer goods inbound to China.

**Can 1210 cross-border e-commerce stock arrive by rail or air?** Yes. The 1210 model governs supervision at the bonded warehouse, not the inbound mode. Stock may arrive by any lawful mode provided the goods are on the positive list and the warehouse is a customs special supervision area or type-B bonded logistics centre.

**When is air freight into China worth the premium?** For launches with fixed dates, stockout recovery on fast movers, and short shelf-life or temperature-sensitive goods. Air runs at several multiples of the ocean rate per kilo in market practice — a shipment-level tool, not a default lane.

**Which Chinese port should I route my goods through?** Match the port to the bonded warehouse city: Tianjin for the north, Shanghai and Ningbo for the Yangtze Delta, Guangzhou for the Greater Bay Area, Qingdao for Shandong. 2025 customs data on cross-border retail import destinations: Guangdong 14.8 percent, Jiangsu 8.6, Zhejiang 8.5, Shanghai 6.8, Shandong 5.6.
