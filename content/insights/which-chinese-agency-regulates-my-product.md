## Definition: "which agency regulates my product" means two different questions

Most importers ask it as one question, but it is really two:

1. **Who approves my product before it can be sold?** (pre-market registration, certification, licensing)
2. **Who checks it at the border, and who polices it once it is on shelves?** (border control vs. market supervision)

Answering both is the difference between a smooth clearance and a container sitting at the port. The Chinese system splits these across **seven authorities** — and knowing which one owns your category saves weeks of misdirected effort.

> The single most common mistake: assuming "customs" is the only gate. For regulated products, the registration authority (NMPA, SAMR, CNCA) decides admissibility long before GACC ever sees the shipment.

## The seven agencies that matter for importers

| Agency | Full name | Owns | Checks at |
|--------|-----------|------|-----------|
| **GACC** | General Administration of Customs | Import/export declarations, tariffs, border inspection & quarantine, overseas manufacturer registration (Decree 248/280) | **Border** |
| **NMPA** | National Medical Products Administration | Registration/filing of drugs, medical devices, cosmetics | Pre-market + post-market |
| **SAMR** | State Administration for Market Regulation | Market supervision, recalls, advertising/pricing, health food & infant formula registration, CCC enforcement | **Market** |
| **CNCA** | Certification & Accreditation Administration | Compulsory CCC certification, overseas factory certification (dairy/meat/aquatic) | Pre-market + border |
| **MOFCOM** | Ministry of Commerce | Import licences, quotas, certificates of origin, automatic import permits | Border paperwork |
| **MOA** | Ministry of Agriculture | Primary agricultural products, animal & plant quarantine, feed, pet food, GMO safety | Border + source |
| **NHC** | National Health Commission | National food-safety GB standards (the standards themselves) | Standard-setting |

Two more show up on specific products: **CNIPA** (National Intellectual Property Administration) for trademarks and patents, and provincial/municipal **Market Regulation Bureaus** that run the day-to-day retail inspection.

## Border vs. market: who touches your goods when

- **At the port**, GACC inspects and quarantines, verifies your declaration and HS code, confirms overseas-manufacturer registration (for food), and collects duty/VAT. If a product needs CCC or an import licence, GACC also confirms those certificates at clearance.
- **In the market**, SAMR runs sampling, recalls non-compliant products, and polices advertising and pricing. NMPA continues to supervise registered drugs, devices and cosmetics after launch.
- **Pre-market**, the approval authority (NMPA for devices/cosmetics, SAMR for health food, CNCA for CCC) issues the certificate that makes the product admissible at all.

A product can pass customs and still be pulled from shelves weeks later by SAMR. Plan for both gates, not just the first.

## Find your agency in three steps

**Step 1 — Name the product category.**
Food, cosmetics, medical device, drug, electronics/appliance, toy, health-food supplement, or machinery? Category decides everything downstream.

**Step 2 — Check for pre-market registration or certification.**
- Drugs, medical devices (Class II/III), special-use cosmetics → **NMPA** registration/filing.
- Health food (blue-hat), infant formula, foods for special medical purpose → **SAMR** registration.
- CCC-catalogue products (electronics, toys, many appliances, some auto parts) → **CNCA** certification.
- Food (most categories, plus 17 high-risk under Decree 280) → overseas manufacturer **GACC** registration.

**Step 3 — Confirm border paperwork.**
- MOFCOM licence/quota if the item is controlled.
- MOA certificate for agricultural, animal or feed products.
- GACC declaration + overseas-registration proof at the port.

If you cannot name a registration or certification step in Step 2, the product is likely general goods — GACC alone usually clears it, provided labels and HS code are correct.

## Category quick-map

| Your product | Primary authority | Also involved |
|--------------|-------------------|---------------|
| General food, beverages, snacks | GACC (border + overseas factory reg) | NHC/SAMR (standards), MOA (agri), CNCA (dairy/meat/aquatic) |
| Health food / supplements (blue-hat) | SAMR | GACC, NHC standards |
| Cosmetics & personal care | NMPA | GACC (border), SAMR (market) |
| Medical devices (II/III) | NMPA | GACC, CNCA (if EMC/radio) |
| Pharmaceuticals | NMPA | GACC, MOFCOM (if controlled) |
| Electronics, appliances, toys, auto parts | CNCA (CCC) | GACC, SAMR |
| Agricultural / pet food / GMO | MOA | GACC, NHC |

The pattern is consistent: **NMPA owns the product register, CNCA owns the certificate, SAMR owns the market, GACC owns the border.** Map your product to those four verbs and you have your answer.

## Common traps

- **"My EU/US approval covers China."** It does not. China applies its own GB standards and NMPA/CNCA regimes. A cosmetic legal in the EU may still need NMPA filing and a China responsible person.
- **"Customs will tell me if something's wrong."** GACC checks at the port, but admissibility is decided by the registration authority beforehand. An unregistered medical device is stopped regardless of paperwork.
- **"CCC is only for big factories."** If the HS code falls in the CCC catalogue, the individual importer is liable at clearance — no certificate, no release.

## FAQ

**Which Chinese agency approves imported cosmetics?** The National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). General cosmetics use a simplified filing; special-use cosmetics need NMPA registration, plus a China responsible person and Chinese-language labels.

**Is the border agency the same as the market regulator?** No. GACC inspects and clears at the port; SAMR supervises the product in the market (sampling, recalls, advertising). NMPA owns pre-market registration of drugs, devices and cosmetics.

**Do I need CCC, and who issues it?** Only for products on China's compulsory certification catalogue (electronics, many appliances, toys, some auto parts). CNCA administers CCC; goods without it are stopped at the border.

**Who registers my overseas food factory?** GACC, under Decree 248/280. Most food manufacturers — and the 17 high-risk categories under Decree 280 — must register before exporting to China.

**Who sets the standards my product must meet?** The National Health Commission (NHC) sets food-safety and many GB standards; SAMR enforces them; CNCA sets certification rules; GACC enforces at import. Chinese national standards apply, not EU/US equivalents.
