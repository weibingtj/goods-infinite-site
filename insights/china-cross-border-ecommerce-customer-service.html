China Cross-Border E-Commerce Customer Service SLAs

Customer service on a Chinese marketplace storefront is not a satisfaction function, it is a deadline system: a seven-day no-reason return right, a seven-working-day regulator acceptance clock with forty-five or sixty days of mediation behind it, a fifteen-working-day window on personal-information requests, three years of retained chat records, and a platform response score that books an unanswered message as ten minutes. This guide sets out what each clock requires of the roster, the scripts and the escalation path, compares in-house, TP and BPO staffing models, and lists the metrics that show whether a desk is compliant rather than merely fast.

Published 2026-10-26 · Last updated 2026-10-30 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

China cross-border e-commerce customer service is a compliance desk, not a satisfaction desk

China cross-border e-commerce customer service is the function that answers Chinese shoppers on marketplace channels, resolves after-sales disputes and produces the records that a platform or a regulator can later inspect, used to keep a storefront trading without penalties. English-language org charts file it under support. In China the desk is judged on deadlines it does not set, so stop measuring it as a satisfaction centre.

The scale of after-sales exposure is documented. In 2024 market-regulation authorities across China received 18.625 million consumer complaints, and after-sales service was the largest category at 4.403 million, or 23.6 percent — top for the second consecutive year, ahead of quality at 19.5 percent (SAMR, 2024 consumer complaint data). Complaints about "smart customer service" reached 6,969 cases, up 56.3 percent year on year, for answers that missed the question and no route to a human.

Five clocks run against every conversation

A service clock is a deadline set by a statute, a regulation or a platform rule that starts when a consumer acts, used to determine how quickly a desk must respond before the cost changes.

The seven-day return right cannot be opted out by default

A no-reason return window is a statutory right of a consumer buying at a distance to cancel within seven days of receipt without giving cause, used to set the first decision an after-sales agent must be empowered to make. Article 25 of the Consumer Rights Protection Law creates it for goods sold by network, television, telephone or mail order, excluding fresh and perishable goods, made-to-order items, digital downloads and periodicals (Consumer Rights Protection Law, 2013 amendment).

The 2024 implementing regulation closed that room. Article 19 forbids expanding the excluded categories unilaterally, requires prominent marking and the consumer's confirmation at the point of purchase, prohibits treating exclusion as a default, and confirms that opening packaging to inspect goods does not defeat the right (State Council Order No. 778, in force 1 July 2024). Refunds are made at the price actually paid, in full.

On cross-border bonded orders the legal right is not the binding constraint; the customs return procedure is. The application must be filed within 30 days, the goods must arrive within 45 days, and they must be fit for resale. A returned unit re-enters bonded stock or becomes a destruction case, so the policy has to be scripted around the customs calendar.

A 12315 filing starts a clock that outlasts the chat

A 12315 complaint is a consumer dispute filed on China's national market-regulation platform, used by a bureau with jurisdiction to compel a documented answer from the seller. Jurisdiction follows the registered address: a complaint against a platform operator goes to the district bureau where the platform is registered; against a merchant, to its actual place of business or the platform operator's address (SAMR Order No. 20, Art. 12 and 14).

The bureau must decide within seven working days whether to accept the case. Mediation then runs to a hard stop: it is terminated if no agreement is reached within 45 working days of acceptance, and the 2024 regulation sets a 60-day outer limit for completing mediation. In 2024 authorities handled 24.094 million complaints and reports, up 7.2 percent, and recovered RMB 5.16 billion.

Operationally, somebody in China must answer a phone on a Chinese working day, pull the order, chat log and return record, and decide whether to settle. A brand whose only escalation path is an email alias in Europe loses on timing, and the loss is published: 13.264 million complaint entries across 5.34 million companies are already public.

Platform scoring books silence as ten minutes

A first-response score is the platform's measure of how long a shopper waits for a human reply, used to rank a storefront and to decide how much traffic it receives. Douyin's service rules set the average response time standard at under 90 seconds, count only human sessions whose first message arrives between 08:00 and 22:59, and — the decisive clause — record a turn as ten minutes whenever the consumer sends a message and the merchant does not reply (Douyin E-commerce, Feige service rules).

90 secondsCeiling on a storefront's average human response time on Douyin, scored only between 08:00 and 22:59 — and an unanswered message is booked as ten minutesSource: Douyin E-commerce, Feige service use and management rules (current revision)

The arithmetic forces the roster. A desk that answers in fifteen seconds all day but leaves the queue unattended from 23:00 to 09:00 does not score fifteen seconds; it scores whatever the backlog drags the mean towards. Fifteen hours of coverage in two shifts with a weekend rotation is the minimum that keeps the average inside the ceiling. An out-of-hours Chinese acknowledgement does not excuse the turn, but it reduces abandoned conversations.

Data-rights requests carry a fifteen-working-day deadline

A personal-information request is a consumer demand to see, copy, correct, restrict or delete the data a merchant holds about them, used as the trigger for a statutory response deadline that most service scripts never mention. Under Article 23 of the Network Data Security Regulation, a data handler must provide a convenient route for structured queries, must not restrict reasonable requests by time or location, and must handle and answer requests to copy, correct, delete, withdraw consent or close an account within fifteen working days (State Council Order No. 790, in force 1 January 2025).

15 working daysStatutory window to complete and answer a consumer's request to copy, correct or delete personal data, or to close an accountSource: State Council Order No. 790, Network Data Security Regulation, Art. 23 (in force 1 January 2025)

Such a request arrives in chat dressed as something else: a shopper asking why the store still has her phone number, or asking to cancel an account. It must be recognised, logged and routed, not answered with an improvised promise. Alongside it sits a three-year retention duty on transaction, logistics, return and after-sales records, held from completion (SAMR Order No. 37, Art. 31). The two are not contradictory — keep the commercial record, correct the personal detail on request — but assign them to different owners, or an agent will resolve the conflict by guessing.

Three staffing models, and the stage each one fits

A China service model is the arrangement by which a foreign brand puts Mandarin-speaking agents on a Chinese marketplace channel, used to trade without first building a mainland payroll. Four routes exist, and they differ less on cost than on who holds the accountability.

In-house through a mainland entity gives control of scripts and escalation but needs a registered company, a payroll and hiring time before the first order. A platform partner runs the desk inside store operations: fastest to go live, hardest to keep queue discipline visible. A BPO sells dedicated seats, quoted by Chinese providers at mid four figures of renminbi per seat-month for a 7x8 position in 2026, and absorbs overnight and campaign peaks. The fourth route runs no Chinese entity at all: a Hong Kong importer of record contracts a licensed service partner that answers as provider of record, which is how a European skincare brand reached first sale in nine weeks.

Whichever route is chosen, the accountable party for a regulator complaint and a data request stays the merchant of record — name that person in writing before the first campaign.

Script rules that keep agents out of advertising trouble

A service script is the approved set of phrases an agent may use in a customer conversation, used to keep answers consistent and legally defensible. In China the script is also an advertising document: Article 9 of the Advertising Law bans superlatives such as "national-level" and "best"; Article 11 requires a cited figure to carry its source and validity period; Article 28 treats a material omission as false advertising (Advertising Law, as amended).

Four rules cover most of the risk. Agents may not describe a cosmetic or food product as treating or preventing disease. They may not promise a refund timetable the customs window cannot meet, because the promise is enforceable once made. They may not collect ID numbers or health details in chat. And they may not coach a consumer on how to declare an order — the declaration is the consumer's.

The metrics that show whether a desk is compliant

A service metric is a number reviewed on a fixed cycle to decide staffing and scripting changes, used to keep platform scores and regulator exposure inside their limits. Speed is the easy half.

  • First responses inside 90 seconds, by hour of day, so the overnight gap is visible rather than averaged away.
  • Return requests approved on first contact — a proxy for whether agents hold the authority the law assumes.
  • Days from approval to refund, measured against the 30/45-day customs window.
  • 12315 cases per 10,000 orders, and median days to close, which is the only honest measure of regulatory exposure.
  • Data-request cycle time against the fifteen-working-day limit, reported even when volume is zero.
  • Repeat contact within seven days on the same order, the source of those smart-customer-service complaints.

FAQ

Do we need Mandarin-speaking customer service before we launch in China? Yes, from the first order. Platform scoring is measured on human replies inside a fixed daily window, so a storefront that cannot answer in Chinese loses traffic allocation before it has revenue to protect. Three statutory clocks also start on the consumer's action rather than yours: a no-reason return request, a 12315 complaint and a request to delete personal data. The minimum viable desk is two Mandarin agents covering 09:00 to 23:00 China time with a written escalation path for regulator and data cases.

Does the seven-day no-reason return right apply to cross-border bonded orders? The right is written into national consumer law for distance selling, and the 2024 implementing regulation forbids expanding the excluded categories or treating exclusion as a default. The binding constraint on bonded orders is the customs return procedure: application within 30 days, arrival within 45 days, goods fit for resale. A returned unit re-enters bonded stock or becomes a destruction case, so the policy should be scripted around the customs calendar and stated on the product page.

Can a foreign brand outsource China customer service instead of hiring? Yes, and most do in the first year. A Tmall Global partner can run the desk inside store operations, and Chinese BPO providers sell dedicated seats, commonly quoted in the mid four figures of renminbi per seat-month for a 7x8 position in 2026. Accountability cannot be outsourced: the merchant of record remains the party named in a regulator complaint and obliged to answer a data request, so keep a named internal owner for escalations.

What service hours should a China storefront cover? At least 08:00 to 23:00 China time, seven days a week — the window Douyin's rules use for scoring average response time. Only human sessions starting inside that window count, and an unanswered message is booked as ten minutes, which makes overnight silence disproportionately expensive. A two-shift roster with a weekend rotation is the practical minimum, plus an out-of-hours acknowledgement in Chinese.

What happens if a consumer complains to 12315 about our store? The case goes to the bureau with jurisdiction over the seller's actual place of business or the platform operator's registered address, which must decide within seven working days whether to accept it. Mediation is terminated if no agreement is reached within 45 working days of acceptance, with a 60-day outer limit under the 2024 regulation. You receive a call or a platform notice and have days, not weeks, to produce the order record, chat log and return history.
