Baidu Search Marketing for Foreign Brands in China

Baidu is where Chinese buyers verify a foreign brand before they trust it — and where a trademark squatter or a daigou listing fills the page if you do not. This guide covers what buyers actually search, the paid and organic layers of Baidu search marketing, the compliance rules that apply, and how the AI-reshaped results page changes the plan.

Published 2026-11-16 · Last updated 2026-11-20 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

Baidu search marketing is how a foreign brand controls what Chinese buyers find when they verify it

Baidu search marketing is the practice of managing what appears when someone searches your brand or category on Baidu — paid listings, organic results and the overall shape of the page — and for a foreign brand entering China its first job is verification, not traffic. A Chinese consumer who discovers your product on Xiaohongshu or in a livestream will almost always check the brand name on Baidu before paying: is there an official site, a real trademark, consistent product information, or only reseller listings of uncertain origin? Distributors and procurement teams run the same check before they reply to your email. If that page is unmanaged, it fills itself — with daigou posts, unauthorized resellers, copycat brands and stale or wrong translations of your name. Search marketing on Baidu is therefore less an acquisition channel and more an integrity layer underneath every other channel you invest in.

Baidu still matters because it is the last engine standing in the trust check

Baidu remains China's default search engine, and it carries that role partly by default: Google has offered no mainland search service since 2010. The honest 2026 picture is more complicated than dominance, though. China counted 782.06 million search engine users at December 2025, 69.5% of netizens — down from 877.82 million a year earlier, a fall of roughly 96 million that the official report attributes to intelligent AI search products drawing users away (CNNIC, 57th Statistical Report, 2026). The same report counted 602 million users of generative AI products. Two things follow for a foreign brand. First, the audience that still types queries into a search engine is large — larger than the population of most continents — and skewing toward deliberate, higher-consideration checks. Second, the check itself is migrating: part of what used to be a search is now a question asked of an AI assistant, which is why the plan below treats the answer layer as seriously as the results page.

What Chinese buyers actually type, and what fills the page if you do not

Brand-verification searches are the searches a foreign brand must win, because they happen at the moment of maximum purchase intent and carry the whole funnel's credibility. The queries follow patterns: your brand name plus "official website" (官网), "is it genuine" (是正品吗), "how is it" (怎么样), and the brand name in a transliterated or translated Chinese form you may not have chosen yourself. That last point is the quiet trap — if you have never published an official Chinese name and registered it as a trademark, the market assigns you one, and squatters register it first (our China trademark registration guide covers the sequence). An unmanaged brand page typically shows e-commerce resellers of unknown authenticity, third-party reviews of your products that may predate your entry, and at worst a counterfeit operation using your imagery. Every downstream partner — the KOL deciding whether to feature you, the distributor assessing risk, the consumer at checkout — reads that same page.

Baidu SEM: the paid layer and the compliance rules that come with it

Baidu search advertising is a keyword-auction system in which advertisers bid to appear against queries, and it is the fastest way to place an official, controlled result at the top of your brand page. The core buys are category keyword ads and brand-term protection — bidding on your own name so an official listing outranks whatever else occupies it. Baidu does not publish a rate card, so all pricing is agency-quote territory and should be labelled as such in your budgeting; get competing quotes and defined reporting metrics before committing. Whatever you run must clear Chinese advertising law. The Advertising Law bans absolute superlatives such as "national-level", "best" or "most advanced" (Article 9) and requires any cited data in an ad to carry its source and validity period (Article 11) (Advertising Law, via MOFCOM policy database). The Measures on Internet Advertising Administration add that paid promotion must be identifiable as advertising, that testimonial or review content carrying a purchase link must be clearly labelled "advertisement", and that advertisers keep advertising records for at least three years (SAMR Order No. 72, 2023). Our China social media advertising compliance guide walks the full rule set.

The results page itself is going AI-native, and your content has to survive that

Baidu's own business results show the search surface is being rebuilt around AI, which changes what "ranking" even means. Baidu's 2025 annual results reported total revenue of RMB 129.08 billion, down 3%, with traditional search-and-feed advertising declining and AI-native marketing services revenue reaching RMB 9.8 billion, up 301% year on year (Baidu FY2025 results, via Eastmoney, Feb 2026). Baidu App monthly active users stood at 679 million at December 2025 (Baidu FY2025 results, via Sina Finance, Feb 2026). In practice this means a growing share of Baidu results pages open with an AI-generated answer assembled from sources the model can retrieve. For a foreign brand, that is a generative-engine-optimization problem in Chinese: write definition-style sentences, publish them on surfaces Baidu retrieves well — Zhihu answers being the classic example, as covered in our Zhihu marketing guide — and keep names, claims and figures consistent across every page, because the model synthesizes rather than ranks.

Baidu SEO: what ranks when you do not pay

Baidu organic ranking rewards mainland-hosted, ICP-licensed, Chinese-language content, plus the ecosystem platforms Baidu indexes deeply. A fully controlled option is your own Chinese-language site hosted in mainland China under an ICP filing — achievable once you have a mainland entity, and part of why entity choice matters earlier than brands expect. Before that stage, the practical organic layer is borrowed authority: Zhihu answers and Weibo posts that Baidu indexes well, official brand accounts on those platforms, and e-commerce storefront pages that rank for brand queries. This is why the platform matrix matters even for a brand that never runs a Baidu ad — the Weibo guide explains how public opinion and indexed posts shape your search page whether or not you advertise there. What does not work: an English-only global site with no Chinese pages, which effectively guarantees the brand-verification search returns other people's content.

A first-90-days checklist for the brand search page

The efficient sequence for a new entrant is audit, register, occupy, publish, monitor — in that order. First, audit: search your brand name, its plausible Chinese translations and your top category terms, and record what occupies the page today. Second, register: file the China trademark for the brand and its official Chinese name before any marketing spend, because both ad-platform brand protection and takedown requests depend on it. Third, occupy: bid on your own brand term through a certified agency so an official result sits above whatever the page holds. Fourth, publish: put three to five authoritative Chinese-language pages live — official brand story, product facts with sourced figures, an FAQ — on surfaces Baidu indexes well. Fifth, monitor monthly, because reseller listings and copycats return. Budget realistically: brand-term defense is cheap relative to category keywords, and it protects the conversion of every other channel you run.
