Importing Into China Without a Local Company: The Importer-of-Record Path

You do not need a mainland WFOE to start selling in China. But you do need a licensed importer of record. Here is the legal baseline, the four structures that work without a local entity, and how a Hong Kong trading arm fits in.

Published 2026-09-21 · Last updated 2026-09-21 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

Definition: what "importing without a local company" actually means

"Importing without a local company" does not mean shipping containers into China with no importer. It means: you, the overseas brand, hold no mainland legal entity, yet a compliant importer of record still clears your goods through Chinese customs. The local-importer function is outsourced, not eliminated.

This distinction matters because Chinese customs will not release cargo without a declared importer that carries legal and tax liability. The question is never "entity or no entity?" — it is "whose entity is on the declaration, and what do they take responsibility for?"

The legal baseline: who can be the importer of record in China

China's framework is strict and well-defined:

  • The importer is the taxpayer. Under the Customs Law of the PRC, the consignee of imported goods is the person liable for customs duties (Art. 54).
  • The importer must be registered with customs. Article 11 requires that importers and customs brokers be lawfully registered with the customs authority; unregistered parties may not conduct customs business.
  • "Importer of record" = a mainland-registered enterprise. The entity named on the import declaration must be a company incorporated within the PRC customs territory with import/export operating rights and the correct business-licence scope for the product category (GACC Announcement No. 113 of 2022 sets out the registration conditions for importers/exporters).
  • A Hong Kong company is a separate tariff-zone entity. A Hong Kong-incorporated company is not a "person within the PRC customs territory" and therefore cannot register with mainland customs or file the declaration in its own name. Customs lawyers are explicit on this point.
The practical consequence: a foreign brand without a mainland entity cannot be the importer of record itself. It must use a mainland-licensed entity — a third-party IOR, a bonded-zone/cross-border operator, or a distributor — as the declared importer.

Path 1 — Third-party Importer of Record (general trade, 0110)

A mainland company with import/export rights and the right licence scope acts as your importer of record. It:

  • Files the import declaration and pays duty + 13% import VAT (9% on some staples) on your behalf.
  • Holds or obtains the product-specific approvals (CCC, NMPA, GACC registration, etc.).
  • Carries customs-compliance liability for the declaration; you remain responsible for product accuracy.

This is the route when goods move under general trade or when a product is regulated and needs a local licence holder. Cost is typically a percentage of CIF or a per-shipment fee — far below the USD 5,000–50,000+ and 3–9 months needed to set up your own entity.

Path 2 — 1210 bonded cross-border (the leanest test)

The 1210 model is where most overseas brands should start, and it needs no WFOE:

  1. Your overseas factory ships to a mainland bonded zone; your Hong Kong trading arm is the overseas shipper and title holder.
  2. Goods sit in bond — no mainland duty/VAT paid yet.
  3. A Chinese consumer orders on Tmall Global / JD Worldwide / Douyin cross-border.
  4. The order triggers per-unit clearance, tax and domestic delivery (1–3 days).

The bonded-zone operator or platform files as the declarant. You get a China-selling capability without forming a mainland company for the first market test. See our guide on what 1210 bonded import is.

Path 3 — Hong Kong trading entity + China import agent

A Hong Kong-incorporated company (e.g. GOODSINFINITE TRADE LIMITED) is a near-ideal holding and trading vehicle: free-port status, no VAT/GST, free currency convertibility, common-law contract certainty. It:

  • Signs supply/distribution contracts in USD/HKD/EUR with your overseas parent or factory.
  • Settles cross-border payments cleanly, keeping the brand-owning layer outside mainland regulatory scope.
  • Works with a mainland import agent that files as importer of record.

This gives you a near-local experience with far less entity overhead. Details in using a Hong Kong entity to import into China.

Path 4 — Local distributor (cede control)

Simplest commercially: the distributor is the importer, so goods land on shelf fastest. The trade-off is total loss of price control, brand story and first-party customer data. Acceptable as a supplement, not a strategy.

Compliance you must clear first — on every path

Outsourcing the importer does not outsource product compliance. Before any structure moves cargo, the product itself must be admissible:

  • Food & agricultural products — overseas manufacturer registration with GACC (Decree 248/280 regimes, including the 17 high-risk categories under Decree 280).
  • Cosmetics & personal care — NMPA filing/registration (with the 2025–2026 animal-testing exemptions and "China-first" filing changes).
  • Medical devices — NMPA registration (Class II/III require a registration certificate).
  • Electronics & telecom — CCC certification; SRRC/NAL for wireless and network equipment.
  • Chinese-language labels conforming to GB standards, and correct HS classification.

See our China import compliance overview and the product-registration guide for the full checklist. A qualified importer of record verifies these at origin, not at the port.

Risk & liability — get the allocation right

  • The importer of record bears declaration liability to the General Administration of Customs: accurate HS coding, duty/VAT payment, record-keeping for audit.
  • You bear product accuracy — composition, claims, origin.
  • Never let your freight forwarder be the IOR. Unless it holds a mainland entity with import rights and the right licence scope, it cannot legally file. An incorrect declaration means holds, demurrage, or seizure — liability lands on whoever customs can identify.
  • DDP does not self-fulfil. A DDP seller with no mainland entity and no IOR partner has taken on an obligation it cannot legally meet; the goods arrive with no compliant importer to clear them.

When you DO need a mainland entity

A WFOE (or local distributor contract) becomes necessary when you want:

  • A domestic Tmall/JD store (not the cross-border version).
  • General-trade (0110) distribution into offline or wholesale channels.
  • To hold licences for regulated categories requiring a local licence holder.
  • To employ local staff, sign local contracts, or establish a permanent commercial presence.

Until then, the importer-of-record path lets you import, sell to real customers, and prove demand before committing to the fixed cost of a permanent local structure. FAQ

Can I import into China without a Chinese company? Yes — but not in your own name. Customs requires the importer of record to be a mainland-registered enterprise, so a foreign brand uses a third-party IOR, a 1210 bonded structure, or a distributor as the declared importer.

Can a Hong Kong company be the importer of record? Not directly. A HK company is a separate tariff-zone entity and cannot file with mainland customs. It acts as overseas shipper, title holder and FX layer; a mainland-licensed entity files as importer of record — especially under 1210.

What is the cheapest way without a WFOE? A 1210 cross-border bonded test through a Hong Kong trading arm: pay duty/VAT only on units sold, avoid WFOE setup cost. A third-party IOR is the alternative for general-trade shipments.

When do I need a mainland company? For a domestic Tmall/JD store, general-trade distribution, or regulated categories needing a local licence holder.

Is a freight forwarder-as-IOR safe? No. Without a mainland entity with import rights and the right licence scope, a forwarder cannot legally file; an incorrect declaration creates holds and shifts liability onto you.
