China Customs Valuation: How Royalties, Assists and Related-Party Prices Set Your Import Tax Base

China customs valuation decides the number that import duty, import VAT and consumption tax are charged on — and it is frequently higher than the price on the invoice. This guide explains the additions and deductions that adjust the tax base, the two tests that make a royalty dutiable, how a special relationship shifts the burden of proof onto the importer, the 30-day royalty declaration clock, and why voluntary disclosure is usually the cheapest way out of a past under-declaration.

Published 2026-09-30 · Last updated 2026-10-02 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

Your import tax base in China is not the price on the invoice

China customs valuation is the process by which the General Administration of Customs determines the value on which import duty, import VAT and consumption tax are levied on imported goods, and it routinely produces a number higher than the one on the commercial invoice. Since the Tariff Law took effect on 1 December 2024 the concept carries a new name — the taxable price, or 计税价格 — and its rules sit in Articles 24 to 28 of the Tariff Law and in the GACC Measures for Determining the Taxable Price of Imported and Exported Goods, Order No. 213 of 2013 as amended by Order No. 273 of 2024.

Where the importer buys from a related overseas seller, or pays a trademark or know-how royalty, the base becomes a position customs can challenge for three years.

3 yearsHow far back customs can recover under-collected duty where the importer's own non-compliance caused it, charged at 0.05 percent per day in late fees — about 18 percent a year, running from the date of release rather than the date of discoverySource: Tariff Law of the People's Republic of China, effective 1 December 2024, Articles 46 and 47

The base starts with transaction value, then additions and deductions

Transaction value is the price actually paid or payable for goods sold for export to China, adjusted by the additions and deductions the law mandates, and it is the compulsory starting point for every valuation. Article 24 of the Tariff Law adds freight and insurance up to the place of arrival, so a CIF price is effectively complete while FOB and CFR prices are not. Only where the transaction value fails the statutory conditions does customs move to five alternative methods — identical goods, similar goods, deductive value, computed value and reasonable means — after a formal price consultation.

A royalty is dutiable on two tests, not on what the contract calls it

A royalty is dutiable in China only where it is related to the imported goods and its payment is a condition of their sale into China, so the two must be tested separately. Article 25 of the Tariff Law brings into the base royalties the buyer must pay, directly or indirectly, to the seller or a related party as a condition of the sale, and the Measures define when each limb is met.

The relationship test in Article 13 recognises four categories. A patent or know-how royalty is related where the goods contain it, are produced by it, or were specially designed to implement it. A trademark royalty is related where the goods bear the mark, can be sold once it is attached, or can be sold after light processing with the mark attached; copyright and distribution or sales-rights royalties cover goods containing software, text, music or images, and goods sellable directly or after light processing. Light processing means dilution, mixing, sorting, simple assembly, repackaging or comparable operations — not a change in physical or chemical character. The condition test in Article 14 is met where the buyer cannot obtain the goods, or cannot obtain them on the agreed terms, without paying the royalty.

Applied together, the tests surprise brands. A Chinese pharmaceutical importer paying a trademark royalty on finished medicines — where the imported goods were raw material and the mark was attached only to the finished product — falls outside the base, while the process know-how fee on the same contract fell inside it, because the know-how could only be implemented using that imported raw material (Tianjin municipal trade bureau, worked customs case). The same reasoning separates an engine from a car: buy branded engines and assemble cars in China, and the engine royalty is dutiable while the car trademark royalty is not.

A special relationship does not disqualify your price — it moves the burden onto you

A special relationship in Chinese customs valuation is a defined family, control or ownership link between buyer and seller that places the transaction value under scrutiny rather than automatically replacing it. Article 16 of the Measures lists eight situations, including family ties, officer or director overlap, common control by a third party, and ownership or control of five percent or more of the other party's publicly issued voting shares; a sole agency, distributorship or concession arrangement qualifies where it meets any of those tests.

The consequence is procedural, not fatal. Article 8 permits the transaction value of related parties provided the relationship did not influence the price, and Article 17 sets out how that is proved: if the declared price is close to sales of identical or similar goods to unrelated buyers in China, or to a deductive or computed value, the relationship is deemed not to have affected it.

No numerical tolerance for "close" exists in the rules, which turns an intercompany price into a position needing evidence — a comparables file, a transfer-pricing study, or an advance ruling. Where the defence cannot be made, customs rejects the transaction value and works down the Article 6 hierarchy, which is how a related-party import ends up taxed on the Chinese resale price.

A royalty paid after import has its own 30-day clock

Subsequent royalty declaration is the procedure for paying import tax on a dutiable royalty not settled when the goods were imported, and it runs on a separate timeline. Under GACC Announcement No. 58 of 2019, the importer must answer the royalty confirmation field on every declaration — exports, processing trade and bonded goods other than those sold domestically are exempt — and where a dutiable royalty was already paid at import, the amount goes in the incidental charges field rather than the total price.

Where it has not been paid, the royalty must be declared within 30 days of each payment, using a dedicated form and supervision code 9500 for subsequent royalty taxation, with the commodity description and code copied from the original import declaration. Failing to complete the confirmation field at all attracts a late fee of 0.05 percent per day from release until the violation is discovered.

Enforcement is routine. A Ningbo company importing throttle sensors, motors and iron cores from Indonesia and Thailand between 2021 and 2024, paying a technology transfer fee and variable technical royalties to its Japanese parent, was ordered to pay the undeclared duties (China Customs journal, 2025). Two Sichuan cases show the range of outcomes: 27 consignments where the confirmation field was left blank drew a fine of about RMB 30,000 for affecting customs supervision order, while royalties paid without the 30-day declaration drew RMB 100,000 for affecting tax collection (Sichuan digital trade portal, customs guidance). The procedural omission is cheaper than the fiscal one, and neither is as cheap as disclosure.

Voluntary disclosure is the cheapest exit from a past under-declaration

Voluntary disclosure is a Chinese customs compliance mechanism under which an enterprise that reports its own violation before customs finds it can avoid administrative penalty and have late fees reduced or waived. The instrument in force is the GACC announcement on handling voluntary disclosure, published 28 September 2025 and effective from 11 October 2025 to 30 June 2027, which replaced the 2023 announcement.

No penalty applies where the disclosure is made within six months of the violation, or between six and twenty-four months where the underpaid tax is no more than 30 percent of the tax payable or no more than RMB 1 million. And a disclosure resulting in a warning or a fine of up to RMB 1 million is not entered in the enterprise's customs credit record — a distinction that matters to any brand watching its importer of record's rating.

Customs has published worked illustrations. A company that had under-declared royalties on imports made between January and May, with roughly RMB 1.3 million of tax underpaid against about RMB 5 million paid on those consignments, was confirmed as penalty-free because the shortfall was around 20 percent — inside the 30 percent threshold (GACC Customs Policy Express, January 2026).

Certainty before you import: the valuation advance ruling

An advance ruling is a binding written decision customs issues before the goods are imported, covering the valuation elements or the valuation method to be applied, and it is available to foreign brands as well as Chinese importers. Article 10 of the Measures for the Administration of the Collection of Import and Export Duties, GACC Order No. 272, effective 1 December 2024, confirms that customs may rule in advance on the elements or method of determining the taxable price, on classification and on origin. A ruling is issued within 60 days of acceptance and remains valid for three years. The same regulation allows a supplementary declaration to be requested by the importer.

For a brand with a substantial royalty stream or an intercompany price above the transfer-pricing radar, an advance ruling converts the largest single uncertainty in the cost model into a fixed number. It must be applied for at least three months before the first import.

What this means for a foreign brand's structure

Customs valuation is the one part of a China import model where the price is not simply what the supplier invoices. Every structure that crosses a border between related entities, or pays separately for a brand or a technology, creates a base customs can test — and the tests run on documents that exist only if they were created deliberately: the licence agreement, the assist apportionment, the comparables file behind an intercompany price, and the confirmation field on every declaration.

FAQ

How is the customs value of imported goods determined in China? By the transaction value first — the price actually paid or payable when the goods are sold for export to China — adjusted for the statutory additions and deductions. Since 1 December 2024 the concept is called the taxable price (计税价格), governed by Articles 24 to 28 of the Tariff Law and the GACC Measures. Only where the transaction value fails the statutory conditions does customs move through the five alternative methods, from identical goods to a reasonable-means valuation.

Are royalties always added to the customs value in China? No. A royalty is dutiable only where it relates to the imported goods and its payment is a condition of their sale into China. The rules define four categories of relationship — patents and technical know-how, trademarks, copyright, and distribution or sales rights — and treat payment as a condition where the buyer cannot obtain the goods, or cannot obtain them on the agreed contract terms, without paying it.

Can an importer still use the invoice price when buyer and seller are related? Yes, if it can show the relationship did not affect the price. Eight situations create a special relationship, including a five percent equity link and common control, but the rules provide a defence: where the price is close to sales of identical or similar goods to unrelated buyers in China, or to a deductive or computed value, the relationship is deemed not to have influenced it. If no benchmark can be met, the transaction value is rejected.

What happens when a royalty is paid after the goods have been imported? It has its own declaration window. Where a dutiable royalty was not paid at import, the importer must declare it within 30 days of each payment under GACC Announcement No. 58 of 2019, using supervision code 9500, and pays at the rate and exchange rate in force on the date customs accepts the royalty declaration rather than the original import date.

What is voluntary disclosure in Chinese customs, and what does it cost? A mechanism allowing an enterprise that reports its own violation before customs finds it to avoid administrative penalty and have late fees reduced or waived. Under the announcement in force since 11 October 2025, no penalty applies to a disclosure made within six months, or between six months and two years where the underpaid tax is no more than 30 percent of the tax payable or no more than RMB 1 million. A resulting fine of up to RMB 1 million is not recorded in the enterprise's customs credit file.
