## Why an import business needs a China entity — and why the WFOE is the default

If you only want to test demand, you do **not** need a Chinese legal entity on day one. Cross-border e-commerce (1210) lets an overseas brand sell into China through a bonded zone, with a Hong Kong importer or a licensed China import agent acting as the record, and no WFOE required.

But at some point the model tips: you want to hold domestic inventory, open a local Tmall or JD store, hire your own team, or invoice customers in RMB. That is when a **Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE)** becomes the right structure. It is the standard vehicle for foreign businesses that actually operate — not just trade across the border — inside mainland China.

> A WFOE is an *upgrade* from the light-touch cross-border model, not a prerequisite. Enter light, then add the entity when the numbers say "scale."

For an import or distribution business, the Representative Office is a dead end — it cannot invoice or trade. The Joint Venture adds a partner and shared control, which most brands avoid unless a licence or local network demands it. The **WFOE** is the clean, fully-controlled default.

## The WFOE registration sequence

The order matters; doing step 2 before step 1 sends you back to the queue.

1. **Name pre-approval.** Reserve a Chinese and English company name via the SAMR (State Administration for Market Regulation) system. Have 3–5 alternatives ready.
2. **Articles of association &amp; lease.** Define business scope (critically, include "import and export" and your product categories), and secure a registered-address lease.
3. **Business licence.** File with the local Market Regulation Bureau; receive the unified social credit code.
4. **Company chops (seals).** Engrave the official seal, legal-rep seal, finance seal and contract seal — these are how a company "signs" in China.
5. **Bank account &amp; capital.** Open a corporate RMB account and register your registered capital.
6. **Tax &amp; customs registration.** Register with tax authorities, and with customs if you will import directly.
7. **Licences (if applicable).** Food, cosmetics (NMPA), medical or other regulated categories need extra filings before you can trade them.

## Realistic timeline and cost

- **Timeline:** 4–8 weeks end-to-end for a standard WFOE, assuming documents are clean. Regulated products add weeks for the extra licences.
- **Set-up cost:** one-time registration (agent, notarisation, registered address, filings) commonly lands in the **low thousands of USD**.
- **Running cost:** the real money is substance — a registered office, at least minimal local staff, accounting and annual audit. Many brands start with a virtual office plus an import agent, and add a WFOE only when volume justifies it.

## Mistakes that delay market entry

- **Wrong business scope.** Forgetting "import and export" or your product category means you cannot clear customs or list the goods — fix it later and you re-file.
- **Leasing before name approval.** Sign a lease tied to a name that then fails pre-approval, and you restart.
- **Underestimating the chop process.** Trying to operate without the proper seals stalls banking and contracts.
- **Assuming cross-border and WFOE compliance are the same.** They are not; scaling to a domestic store triggers the full regime.
