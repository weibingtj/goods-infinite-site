## Why product registration decides whether you can sell at all

Most Western brands are used to a launch sequence where you build a listing, ship stock, and start selling. China does not always work that way. A large slice of consumer categories — from cosmetics and food to electrical goods and medical devices — cannot legally clear customs or appear on a shelf until a Chinese authority has pre-approved the product. For a foreign brand, "product registration" is therefore not a paperwork afterthought; it is the gate that determines whether your goods can enter the country at all. This roadmap shows which authority owns your category, how the registration-versus-filing split works, and how long the clock really runs.

## China product registration is the pre-market approval a foreign brand must obtain from a Chinese authority before its goods can be legally imported, marketed, or sold

China product registration (产品注册) is the mandatory pre-market approval issued by a designated Chinese regulator that authorizes a specific foreign product to be imported and sold in mainland China. It is distinct from customs clearance: clearance moves a shipment across the border, while registration confers the legal right for that product to be on the market in the first place. The single most common mistake foreign brands make is to confuse the two — they book freight and platform onboarding, then discover at the port that the product was never authorized to be sold. Registration is typically held by the overseas manufacturer as registrant of record, but in practice it is lodged and maintained through a China importer or qualified agent.

## Which authority owns your product category

Chinese product oversight is split by product type, and each category sits with a different regulator — so the first job is identifying who owns yours:

- **NMPA (National Medical Products Administration)** owns cosmetics, drugs, and medical devices. Under the Cosmetic Supervision and Administration Regulation (CSAR), special cosmetics need registration while ordinary cosmetics need only filing.
- **GACC (General Administration of Customs)** owns imported food through overseas-manufacturer registration (Decree 248) for the 18 higher-risk food categories — covered in detail in our [GACC food registration guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/gacc-food-registration.html).
- **SAMR / CNCA** own the CCC mark for about 17 categories of electrical, electronic, vehicle, and children's products that must carry compulsory certification before sale.
- **MIIT / SRRC** own radio-type approval (型号核准) for any device with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or cellular radio — a frequent surprise for consumer-electronics and IoT brands.
- Additional regimes exist for veterinary drugs, pesticides, and a handful of other controlled goods.

If your portfolio spans categories, expect to file with two or three of these bodies in parallel rather than one.

## Registration versus filing: the two speeds of market entry

Registration (注册) is a full pre-market review and approval where the regulator assesses safety, formula, and claims before granting market access — the slow lane. Filing (备案) is a lighter notification where the responsible party submits the dossier and can proceed once it is accepted — the fast lane. The same product family can sit in both: under CSAR, a sunscreen is a *special* cosmetic requiring NMPA registration, while a standard moisturizer is *ordinary* and only needs filing ([NMPA](https://www.nmpa.gov.cn)). Knowing which lane each SKU occupies is what turns a 12-month plan into a 6-week one, because filing-track products can go to market almost as soon as the dossier is complete.

## Timelines and validity you should plan around

Registration clocks vary enormously by authority and product risk:

- **NMPA special cosmetics:** typically 8–12 months including required safety and efficacy testing ([NMPA](https://www.nmpa.gov.cn), CSAR framework).
- **Ordinary cosmetics filing:** often weeks once the dossier and China responsible person are in place.
- **GACC 18-category food:** a few weeks to several months depending on category and home-country endorsement.
- **CCC certification:** commonly 1–3 months for testing and factory inspection ([SAMR](https://www.samr.gov.cn)).
- **SRRC radio approval:** roughly 4–8 weeks for model testing.

Registrations also carry validity and renewal obligations, and can be suspended if post-market surveillance finds non-compliance. Build the clock in at the planning stage and run it in parallel with your [Tmall Global or JD Worldwide store setup](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/tmall-global-store-setup.html), never after it.

## Common registration mistakes

- **Treating filing as registration.** Assuming a fast-lane filing covers a product that actually needs full registration leads to a refused shipment.
- **Starting registration after onboarding.** Because special-category clocks run 8–12 months, registration must start before — not after — platform and marketing work.
- **Assuming one approval covers the whole range.** A device can need both CCC and an NMPA medical-device filing; map every SKU separately.
- **Skipping label and claims review.** Chinese labelling and advertising rules are strict; fix them before production, not at the border.

## FAQ

**Do all products need registration before entering China?** No. China gates specific high-risk or regulated categories behind pre-market registration or compulsory certification, while most general merchandise can be imported and sold once normal customs and labelling rules are met. The decisive question is which category your SKU falls into.

**Can a foreign brand complete registration without a China entity?** Often yes, but with help. The overseas manufacturer is usually the registrant of record and the application is lodged through a China importer or qualified agent, while a local import-record-filed entity is still required to lodge shipments and share accountability.

**How long does NMPA cosmetic registration take under CSAR?** Special cosmetics typically take 8–12 months including testing; ordinary cosmetics only require filing and can clear in weeks once the dossier and responsible-person requirements are met.

**What is the difference between CCC and NMPA registration?** CCC is a SAMR/CNCA safety-certification mark for about 17 hardware categories; NMPA registration governs cosmetics, drugs and medical devices at the product-and-claims level. A device can need both.
