## Why cosmetics is the category everyone wants — and the one to plan carefully

China is the world's second-largest beauty market, and demand for imported skincare, makeup and fragrance keeps growing. But cosmetics is also one of the most **compliance-sensitive** categories to bring in: the rules sit inside a dedicated law, the *Cosmetics Supervision and Administration Regulation* (CSAR, 化妆品监督管理条例), enforced by the **National Medical Products Administration (NMPA)**.

The good news: you do **not** have to clear the full NMPA regime on day one. Most overseas beauty brands should enter through **cross-border e-commerce (1210)** first, prove demand with low risk, then upgrade certification as volume justifies it. This guide explains exactly where the lines are.

## The regulatory map: CSAR and NMPA

Under CSAR (effective 1 January 2021), every cosmetic imported or sold in China is split into two buckets:

1. **General cosmetics (普通化妆品)** — everything that is not "special": most skincare, makeup, fragrance, haircare (non-functional), cleansers.
2. **Special cosmetics (特殊化妆品)** — products with specific functional claims: hair dye, perming, spot-removal and whitening, sun protection, and anti-hair-loss.

The bucket decides your paperwork.

A mandatory piece for **both** routes: an overseas manufacturer must appoint a **domestic responsible person (境内责任人)** in China — to lodge the filing/registration, handle adverse-event monitoring and act as the local point of contact. That responsible person is usually your importer, a subsidiary, or a qualified agent.

## Cross-border (1210) vs. general trade — the compliance gap

This is the single most important nuance for a beauty brand:

- **Cross-border e-commerce (1210).** Most cosmetics enter under the cross-border **positive list** with **simplified oversight** — they are not held to the full NMPA filing/registration and Chinese-label regime that general trade demands. You stock into a bonded zone, clear per order, pay tax only when a unit sells, and can go live in weeks.
- **General trade (0110) / domestic store.** The moment you want a **domestic Tmall or JD store**, full general-trade rules apply: NMPA filing or registration, a China responsible person, full Chinese labelling, and substantiated claims.

> The smart sequence for a beauty brand: enter via 1210 to validate demand with light compliance, then invest in full NMPA filing/registration only when the sales data says "scale."

## The animal-testing question (yes, there is good news)

Since **1 May 2021**, imported **general cosmetics** can be exempt from mandatory animal testing — *if* they meet conditions such as a complete safety assessment report and a GMP certificate from the home country. That is what finally let "cruelty-free" imported beauty reach Chinese consumers through cross-border channels.

**Special-use cosmetics** (e.g. sunscreen, whitening) generally still require animal testing under current rules. If your brand's cruelty-free positioning is central, plan the product mix and channel accordingly, and confirm per SKU with a licensed broker.

## Label and claim rules that trip brands up

Whether general trade or cross-border, claims are policed tightly:

- **Efficacy claims must be substantiated.** You need evidence behind claims like "anti-ageing" or "brightening" — vague or unproven claims draw scrutiny.
- **Medical/therapeutic claims are off-limits.** Terms like "药妆" (cosmeceutical), "医美" (medical beauty) and disease-treatment language are prohibited for cosmetics.
- **Ingredient disclosure** is required; banned or restricted ingredients under Chinese rules must be removed before import.
- **Chinese-language information** is mandatory for general-trade sales; get the label reviewed *before* you ship, because one wrong claim can mean a border rejection.

## A realistic timeline for a beauty launch

- **1210 cross-border test:** ~4–8 weeks to first sale once product and platform onboarding are ready (light compliance).
- **NMPA filing (general cosmetics):** typically weeks to ~2–3 months after the dossier and responsible person are in place.
- **NMPA registration (special cosmetics):** often 6–12 months — budget this early if sunscreen/whitening is core to your line.

## Common mistakes beauty brands make

- **Shipping a full container before confirming the category path** — a special-use product silently needs registration, not filing.
- ** Assuming cross-border and general trade are the same compliance set** — they are not; scaling to a domestic store triggers the full regime.
- **Using prohibited claim language** ("药妆", medical claims) on packaging or in livestream scripts — this is enforced on-platform too.
- **Skipping the China marketing layer** — discovery for beauty happens on Douyin and RedNote (Xiaohongshu), not Google.

## FAQ

**Do I need NMPA approval to sell cosmetics in China?** For cross-border (1210) retail, most cosmetics enter under a simplified positive-list regime without full filing. For general trade or a domestic store, general cosmetics need NMPA filing and special cosmetics need registration, plus a China responsible person.

**What is the difference between NMPA filing and registration?** Filing applies to general cosmetics and is a notification once the dossier and responsible person are ready. Registration applies to special-use cosmetics and is a substantive NMPA review with longer lead time.

**Can imported cosmetics skip animal testing?** Imported general cosmetics can be exempt from mandatory animal testing since May 2021 if conditions (safety assessment, GMP certificate, etc.) are met. Special-use cosmetics generally still require it. Confirm per product.

**What is the fastest way for a beauty brand to enter China?** A 1210 cross-border test via a Hong Kong entity or import agent — lighter compliance, no WFOE, tax paid per order. Upgrade to full NMPA filing and a domestic store only after demand is proven.
