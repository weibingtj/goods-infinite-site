## Why Tmall Global is the default first store for overseas brands

For a foreign brand entering China, **Tmall Global** is the lowest-friction storefront with real scale. It is a *cross-border* channel: you sell to Chinese consumers while the goods clear through 1210 bonded import, and you can do it as an overseas entity — no China WFOE required on day one.

That makes it the natural first step before a full domestic store, which would trigger the heavier general-trade regime (China entity, NMPA/filing for regulated goods, full Chinese labelling).

> Tmall Global lets you prove demand with a foreign entity and bonded fulfillment — then upgrade to a domestic store only when the data says scale.

## The onboarding sequence

1. **Brand &amp; entity docs.** Overseas business licence, brand ownership or authorisation, and the legal representative's identification.
2. **Category qualification.** Submit product-category proofs; regulated goods need their filings (e.g. NMPA for cosmetics).
3. **Deposit &amp; contract.** Agree the deposit, annual fee and commission; sign the merchant agreement.
4. **Store build.** Set up the flagship store, listings, pricing and brand visuals.
5. **Fulfillment link.** Connect a bonded zone (1210) so orders clear per unit and ship domestically fast.
6. **Go live &amp; operate.** Launch, then run content, promotions and customer service.

## Timeline and cost

- **Timeline:** 1–3 months from complete docs to live store. Regulated categories (cosmetics, supplements) extend it with product filings.
- **Cost:** a refundable deposit (often tied to projected GMV), an annual platform fee, and per-order commission. Confirm the exact schedule during onboarding, and budget separately for content and operation.
- **Fulfillment:** bonded warehousing is a recurring cost but unlocks 1–3 day delivery, which Chinese shoppers expect.

## Operational realities brands underestimate

- **Content is the store.** Discovery on Tmall is driven by search and live-commerce; a bare listing underperforms. Plan for ongoing content and Douyin/RedNote seeding.
- **Compliance pauses launches.** Cosmetics and supplements need filings before listing; start them in parallel with onboarding.
- **Deposit is real capital.** The refundable deposit ties up cash; model it into your China working-capital plan.
- **Customer service is local.** Chinese shoppers expect fast, Mandarin service; route it before launch.
