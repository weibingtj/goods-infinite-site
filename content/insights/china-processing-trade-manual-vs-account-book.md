faq:
  - q: What is processing trade in China, in legal terms?
    a: Art. 3 of GACC Order No. 219 (the Customs Measures for the Supervision of Processing Trade Goods) defines it as the business of importing all or part of the raw and auxiliary materials, parts, components and packaging materials, processing or assembling them, and re-exporting the finished products. It covers two structures, lailiao jiagong and jinliao jiagong, and nothing else. The distinguishing feature is not the factory and not the product — it is the obligation to re-export. Once the finished goods stay in China, the regime converts into a taxable import.
  - q: What is the difference between a processing trade manual and an account book?
    a: Both are bonded ledgers, and both are filed electronically through the single window. A manual (手册) is established per contract or order and is closed by a report-and-write-off cycle; its validity follows the contract but in principle does not exceed one year, extendable to a maximum of two (GACC Announcement No. 56 of 2016). An account book (账册) is established per enterprise under networked supervision and runs on a write-off cycle instead of a contract, which is why larger manufacturers with many short orders use it. Art. 5 of Announcement No. 80 of 2021 adds a third layer, the enterprise group model, in which group members may each hold a manual or account book, or one company may hold it for the whole group.
  - q: What exactly is danhao, and why does it matter commercially?
    a: Danhao is the quantity of imported materials consumed in producing one unit of finished product under normal processing conditions, and under Art. 3 of GACC Order No. 155 it decomposes into net consumption plus process loss. It is the number customs uses to write off bonded materials, so it converts directly into money: declare it too low and you have undeclared bonded material at write-off, declare it too high and you have over-declared loss. Where a published consumption standard exists, a declaration above the standard is written off at the standard's ceiling — the excess material is then treated as non-bonded and must be taxed through ordinary trade.
  - q: Can processing trade materials be sold inside China?
    a: Yes, but the tax deferral reverses. Art. 33 of Order 219 provides that where bonded imported materials or finished goods are sold domestically, customs levies duty on the bonded imported materials and additionally charges deferred-payment interest. Art. 36 of the Tariff Law states the same rule at the level of statute. Where the materials are subject to import licence restrictions, the licence must also be produced. Domestic sale is a lawful exit route; selling before completing the procedure is not.
  - q: How is the duty calculated when processing trade goods are sold domestically?
    a: On the materials, not on the finished product, and on the import price, not the domestic selling price. Under Art. 4 of Order No. 211 (retitled and amended by Order No. 273 of 2024 as the Measures for Determining the Taxable Price of Bonded Goods Sold Domestically), materials imported under jinliao jiagong are valued on the original import transaction price, or on a weighted average of the contract or write-off cycle where batches cannot be matched one-to-one. Under Art. 5, lailiao jiagong materials are valued on the price of identical or similar bonded goods imported at or about the time the domestic-sale tax is paid. By-products and scrap are valued on the domestic selling price (Art. 6).
  - q: Is there a faster way to handle domestic sales than declaring each shipment?
    a: Yes — consolidated domestic-sale tax payment. A qualifying enterprise registers for the consolidated procedure, keeps a dispatch record for goods released into the domestic market, and then declares and pays the tax after the quarter ends. The window is fifteen days after the end of the quarter, capped by the validity of the manual or the write-off deadline of the account book, and it cannot cross a calendar year. The trade-off is that the enterprise carries the exposure in the meantime, which is why the procedure is not open to enterprises under investigation, with overdue manuals, or under a rectification order.
  - q: Did the guarantee requirement for restricted-category processing trade change recently?
    a: Yes. The ten-department Opinion on Raising the Level of Processing Trade (Shang Mao Fa 2023 No. 308) suspended guarantee measures for restricted-category processing trade until the end of 2025. That suspension expired on 31 December 2025, and from 1 January 2026 the guarantee is applied again: advanced certified enterprises post nothing, enterprises in the eastern region other than advanced certified and discredited ones post 50% of the duty and import VAT payable on the restricted materials, enterprises where both operator and processor sit in the central, western or north-eastern regions post nothing, and discredited enterprises post 100%.

## Processing trade in China is a deferral, not an exemption — and it is policed by a ledger, not by a licence

Every importer eventually asks the same question in a different accent: *if I ship components into China, have them assembled, and sell the result outside China, do I pay Chinese import duty on the components?* The answer is no — but the mechanism is narrower and more administrative than most summaries suggest, and it is enforced through a document rather than a permission.

[Art. 3 of GACC Order No. 219](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/attachDir/2026/01/2026010615052227060.pdf), the Customs Measures for the Supervision of Processing Trade Goods (2014, amended five times, most recently by Order No. 262 of 2023), defines processing trade as importing all or part of the raw and auxiliary materials, parts, components and packaging materials, processing or assembling them, and **re-exporting the finished products**. Art. 42 then fixes the two permitted structures. In *lailiao jiagong* the overseas enterprise supplies the materials, the Chinese operator pays no foreign exchange for them, is paid a processing fee, and the overseas enterprise sells the output. In *jinliao jiagong* the Chinese operator buys the materials itself and sells the finished goods abroad. Everything else — who owns the inventory, who invoices whom, where the margin sits — follows from which of those two you are in.

The consequence is blunt. Processing trade is a regime for goods that leave. [Art. 5 of the same Order](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/attachDir/2026/01/2026010615052227060.pdf) makes write-off conditional on the quantity actually processed and re-exported, and [Art. 36 of the Tariff Law](https://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/202404/t20240426_3933677.htm), in force since 1 December 2024, states the rule at statute level: bonded goods re-exported are free of duty; bonded goods not re-exported and converted to domestic sale are dutiable, and processing trade materials or finished goods sold domestically additionally attract deferred-payment interest.

## Manual or account book: same ledger, different unit of supervision

Practitioners talk about "the manual" and "the account book" as if they were rival systems. They are the same bonded ledger filed through the same system, differing in what the unit of supervision is. [China Customs describes three models](http://chinacustoms.gmcmonline.com/202407/c/19938.shtml) — by contract, by enterprise, and by enterprise group — and each maps to a different way of holding the ledger.

The group model is the one that changed most recently, and it is worth knowing because it is the closest thing in the regime to supply-chain flexibility. [GACC Announcement No. 80 of 2021](http://chinacustoms.gmcmonline.com/202407/c/19938.shtml), effective 15 October 2021, allowed bonded materials to move between group members, allowed bonded goods to be stored in any member's filed premises, removed the filing step for intra-group outsourcing, removed the guarantee for full-process intra-group outsourcing, and allowed non-priced equipment still under supervision to be transferred between members. The original condition was that the lead enterprise hold advanced certified status; a 2024 package of sixteen measures widened that, so that in information technology, artificial intelligence, biopharmaceuticals, new energy, new materials and major equipment manufacturing, an enterprise that is not on the discredited list may act as lead enterprise.

## What the manual actually gives you — and what it takes

Two provisions do most of the commercial work, and they point in opposite directions.

[Art. 4 of Order 219](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/attachDir/2026/01/2026010615052227060.pdf) provides that, unless the state provides otherwise, where imported processing trade materials are subject to import restrictive rules, **the operator is exempt from submitting the import licence**. That is a genuine and under-appreciated benefit: the raw material can enter without the licence the same material would need under ordinary trade. The second half of the article reverses it — where the *finished product* is subject to export restrictive rules, the operator must obtain the export licence, and customs verifies it electronically.

<div class="statcard"><span class="n">5 working days · 3 working days · 3 years</span><span class="l">Manual establishment must be completed within five working days of an acceptable declaration; any outsourcing of processing must be filed within three working days of dispatch; and the establishment and write-off documents must be retained for three years from the date the write-off is closed</span><span class="src">Source: GACC Order No. 219, Arts. 13, 24 and 39</span></div>

What it takes is discipline, in five specific places. Materials must be kept separately from non-processing-trade goods and stored at premises filed with customs — changing the premises requires prior notice and a filing amendment (Art. 10). Bonded materials may be swapped with each other or with non-bonded materials only with customs approval, only within the same enterprise, and only on the principles of identical variety, identical specification, identical quantity and no profit — and *lailiao jiagong* bonded materials may not be swapped at all (Art. 27). Where the process requires non-bonded materials, the proportion, variety, specification, model and quantity must be declared in advance, and customs deducts them from the total consumption at write-off (Art. 28). Outsourcing is permitted but the consignee may not resell the goods or sub-outsource them, and outsourcing of the entire process requires a guarantee equal to the duty payable (Art. 24). And a manual cannot be opened at all in five situations, including prohibited materials or products, products the state forbids processing in China, materials unsuitable for bonded supervision, and where the operator has an expired manual it never reported for write-off (Art. 16).

## Unit consumption is where the money is

If one number decides whether a processing trade operation survives an audit, it is *danhao*. [Art. 3 of GACC Order No. 155](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/attachDir/2026/01/2026010614432375899.doc), the Customs Measures for the Administration of Unit Consumption in Processing Trade (2007, amended three times), defines it as the quantity of materials consumed in producing one unit of finished product under normal processing conditions, and decomposes it into **net consumption plus process loss**. Art. 4 requires it to be filed at the manual establishment stage; Art. 14 requires it to be declared truthfully before the finished goods are exported, transferred by deep processing, or sold domestically.

The cap is where operations get caught. Art. 10 of the same Order states that published consumption standards apply to processing trade enterprises **outside** special customs supervision areas and bonded supervision sites, and do not apply to enterprises inside them. Art. 11 then sets the consequence: a declared figure within the standard is used to write off the bonded materials, but a declared figure **above** the standard is written off at the standard's maximum ceiling — or, for export-dutiable finished goods, at its minimum floor. Art. 12 covers the many products with no published standard: declare the actual figure, and customs writes off on the actual figure. The practical effect is that inventing a generous loss rate does not create bonded material; it creates material that must be taxed through ordinary trade.

Equally, not everything a factory calls loss is process loss. The customs service guide for unit consumption lists six categories expressly **excluded** from process loss: loss caused by sudden power, water or gas failure or other human causes; loss caused by loss of or damage to bonded materials, semi-finished or finished goods; loss from force majeure; increased material use caused by quality or specification non-conformity with the contract; loss arising from non-bonded materials used in process batching; and consumption of consumable materials during processing. Those categories are exactly the ones a factory's cost sheet tends to blend in.

## The domestic-sale bill: materials, original price, plus interest

Selling inside China is lawful, and it is expensive in a specific, computable way. [Art. 33 of Order 219](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/attachDir/2026/01/2026010615052227060.pdf) requires customs to levy duty on the bonded imported materials and to charge deferred-payment interest, and to require the import licence where the materials are subject to import restrictions. [Art. 36 of the Tariff Law](https://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/202404/t20240426_3933677.htm) is the statutory basis.

The valuation rule is counter-intuitive and worth stating plainly: you are taxed on the **materials**, at the **import** price, not on the finished product at the domestic selling price. [Order No. 211](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/attachDir/2026/04/2026041014055087021.pdf), retitled by Order No. 273 of 2024 as the Measures for Determining the Taxable Price of Bonded Goods Sold Domestically, sets the ladder in Arts. 4 to 7.

Interest is charged on top, and its window is unusually long. The detailed computation sits in GACC Announcement No. 14 of 2009 on the collection and refund of deferred-payment interest on domestic sales of bonded processing trade goods: interest runs from the date the **first batch of materials under the contract** was imported until the date customs issues the duty payment certificate, at the rate published by the GACC. For a contract that ran for eleven months before the goods were sold inside China, that is eleven months of interest on the full duty amount, not interest from the date of the sale.

That consolidated procedure exists because the default rule is the opposite one — pay first, then sell — and the mismatch between a factory's shipping rhythm and customs declarations is real. Registration for it is refused where the enterprise is under investigation for smuggling or a regulatory violation that is not concluded, holds an overdue manual, or is inside a rectification period ordered for poor management. And the statutory consequences of getting the sequence wrong are not administrative: Art. 41 of Order 219 routes unapproved domestic sales to the Customs Law and the implementing regulations on customs administrative penalties.

## What came back on 1 January 2026

Processing trade goods are classified into prohibited, restricted and permitted categories, and the restricted category carries a guarantee. The ten-department [Opinion on Raising the Level of Processing Trade](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202312/content_6923425.htm) (Shang Mao Fa 2023 No. 308, 25 December 2023) suspended guarantee measures for restricted-category processing trade "until 2025", directed a timely reduction of the prohibited-category list, and cut the supervision period for non-priced processing trade equipment from five years to three. That suspension expired on 31 December 2025.

From 1 January 2026 the guarantee applies again, and the structure is a credit-and-geography matrix rather than a flat rate. [China Customs' own explanation](http://chinacustoms.gmcmonline.com/202601/c/21994.shtml) sets it out: the restricted-category list runs to 451 tariff codes (95 export-restricted, 356 import-restricted), of which 81 codes — all import-restricted — are on the "actual transfer" footing that requires a guarantee.

The eastern region is defined as Beijing, Tianjin, Shanghai, Liaoning, Hebei, Shandong, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian and Guangdong. The guarantee is posted at the same time as the manual or account book is established, must be topped up if an amendment raises the exposure, may be given as a cash deposit or a bank or non-bank financial institution guarantee letter, and is released after the finished goods are exported and the write-off is closed. The instrument does not apply inside special customs supervision areas such as export processing zones and bonded zones, nor to deep-processing transfers of restricted goods within China. This is the same architecture as the enterprise-level duty guarantee used elsewhere in the customs code, which is covered in our [guide to China customs duty guarantees](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-customs-duty-guarantee.html).

## What this means if you are a foreign brand using a Chinese factory

Three consequences matter more than the mechanics.

First, **the ledger holder is a Chinese entity, not you.** A manual or account book is established by a customs-registered operator and processor. A foreign brand cannot hold one. In practice your contract manufacturer holds it, and your role is defined by which structure you are in: as *lailiao jiagong* you are the overseas enterprise supplying materials and buying back output against a processing fee; as *jinliao jiagong* you are simply selling materials to a Chinese buyer who then exports finished goods. The tax outcome is similar; the ownership, foreign-exchange and accounting consequences are not.

Second, **the customs status of your product does not decide this** — the flow of goods does. Whether your category is restricted, whether it is subject to a tariff-rate quota, and how it is classified all still apply, and they apply at the point of domestic sale rather than import. Our notes on [HS classification](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-hs-code-classification-importers.html) and on [tariff-rate quotas](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-tariff-rate-quota-in-quota-out-of-quota.html) are the relevant companion reading, because a quota code that cannot be imported under ordinary trade also cannot be quietly converted through a bonded ledger.

Third, **processing trade and bonded warehousing are different answers to adjacent questions.** Processing trade assumes transformation and re-export. A [bonded warehouse or comprehensive bonded zone](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-bonded-zone-vs-bonded-warehouse.html) assumes storage and deferral without a manufacturing obligation. If your plan is to import, hold, and then decide between export and domestic sale, the warehouse is the right instrument; if the plan is to import materials, manufacture and export, it is the manual. Brands selling directly to Chinese consumers through 1210 have a third route entirely, described in [what 1210 bonded import actually is](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/what-is-1210-bonded-import.html), and one that does not involve a processing trade ledger at all.

## What we deliberately do not assert

- We do not state the current deferred-payment interest rate. The GACC publishes it and adjusts it; the rate in force on the date the duty payment certificate is issued is the one that applies, and quoting a historical figure would mislead.
- We do not give a cost for establishing a manual or an account book, or a total elapsed time for a write-off in a specific port. The statutory clocks are five working days for establishment and thirty for write-off; the rest depends on the file.
- We do not assert that the prohibited-category list has been reduced to a specific current number of codes. The 308 Opinion directs timely reduction and the list is adjusted by joint announcement, so the applicable version should be checked against the announcement in force.
- We do not treat processing trade as available to a foreign company without a Chinese operating entity in the chain. The operator and processor are customs-registered Chinese enterprises; a brand outside China participates as counterparty, not as ledger holder.
- We do not assert that a bonded ledger can cure a missing import licence at the point of domestic sale. Art. 33 of Order 219 requires the licence to be produced when the materials are subject to import restrictions.

## FAQ

**What is processing trade in China, in legal terms?** Art. 3 of GACC Order No. 219 defines it as importing all or part of the raw and auxiliary materials, parts, components and packaging materials, processing or assembling them, and re-exporting the finished products, in either the *lailiao jiagong* or *jinliao jiagong* structure. The defining obligation is re-export.

**Manual or account book — which applies to me?** A manual is opened per contract and closed per contract; its validity follows the contract but in principle does not exceed one year, extendable to two. An account book is opened per enterprise under networked supervision and runs on a write-off cycle, with an annual declaration where the cycle exceeds a year. Manufacturers with many short orders generally use the account book.

**What is danhao and why does it decide the bill?** It is the quantity of imported material consumed per unit of finished product under normal conditions, comprising net consumption plus process loss (Order 155, Art. 3). Customs writes off bonded materials against it. Where a published standard exists, a declaration above the standard is written off at the ceiling, and the excess material must be taxed through ordinary trade.

**Can I sell processing trade goods inside China?** Yes. Art. 33 of Order 219 requires duty on the bonded imported materials plus deferred-payment interest, and the import licence where applicable; Art. 36 of the Tariff Law is the statutory basis. Duty is assessed on the materials at the import price, not on the finished product at the domestic selling price.

**How long does the interest run?** From the date the first batch of materials under the relevant contract was imported to the date customs issues the duty payment certificate, at the rate published by the GACC (GACC Announcement No. 14 of 2009). It is not calculated from the date of the domestic sale.

**Is there a consolidated domestic-sale procedure?** Yes — register, release goods with a dispatch record, then declare and pay within fifteen days after the quarter ends, capped by the manual's validity or the account book's write-off deadline, and never across a calendar year. It is refused to enterprises under investigation, with overdue manuals, or inside a rectification period.

**What changed for restricted-category goods on 1 January 2026?** The suspension under Shang Mao Fa 2023 No. 308 expired on 31 December 2025. From 1 January 2026 a guarantee applies again: nothing for advanced certified enterprises and for operators and processors both in the central, western or north-eastern regions; 50% of duty and import VAT for other eastern-region enterprises; 100% for discredited enterprises.
