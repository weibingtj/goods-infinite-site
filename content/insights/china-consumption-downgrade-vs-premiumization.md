## The China consumption downgrade debate is a question about direction, not collapse

**The "China consumption downgrade" is the argument that Chinese households are systematically trading down — choosing cheaper products, discount channels and own-label goods — and the debate for any foreign brand is whether that describes the market you are entering.** The 2025 data gives a sharper answer than either camp: China is not downgrading or upgrading uniformly. It is running both at once. Volume is migrating to formats and channels that price efficiently, while specific premium categories keep growing fast enough to embarrass the pessimists. For a brand planning entry, the useful question is not "is China downgrading?" but "which half of this two-speed market does my category live in, and what price position does that half reward?" This article reads the official 2025 numbers and turns them into that decision.

## The format signal: brand-exclusive shelves are the only ones shrinking

**The retail-format data is where the downgrade shows up first, because it measures where Chinese consumers chose to shop, not what they say about the economy.** Across above-designated-size retailers in 2025, convenience stores grew 5.5% and supermarkets 4.3%, specialty stores 2.6%, department stores 0.1% — and brand-exclusive stores shrank 0.6%, the only negative line ([NBS, December 2025 retail release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260120_1962354.html)). Read together, the shelf where a brand pays for its own image is the one losing volume, while high-frequency, price-transparent formats gain. This is a channel migration more than a quality migration: the same household buying a cheaper ticket more often in a convenience store is counted as growth, and the flagship store down the street is counted as decline.

## The saving margin: income outgrew spending, and the difference went to services

**A savings margin is the gap between income growth and consumption growth, and in 2025 China's households widened it deliberately.** Per-capita disposable income reached RMB 43,377, up 5.0%, while per-capita consumption expenditure rose 4.4% to RMB 29,476 — so households saved the widening, a precautionary pattern rather than distress ([NBS, 2025 income and expenditure release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260120_1962356.html)). Two details inside the spending matter more for brands. First, services took 46.1% of consumption spending, so the fastest reallocation is from goods to experiences — travel, dining, fitness. Second, the Engel coefficient (the food share of spending) held at 29.3%, meaning food demand is stable, not cut. Consumers are not spending less; they are auditing goods spending harder and letting services, not SKUs, absorb the growth.

## Where the volume went: channels, not cheaper products

**The downgrade in volume terms is a channel shift — money moved to formats that price well — before it ever became a product-quality shift.** Online physical-goods retail reached RMB 13.09 trillion in 2025, 26.1% of total retail sales of RMB 50.12 trillion ([NBS, December 2025 retail release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260120_1962354.html)). Combined with the convenience-store gain and the flat department-store line, the pattern is consistent: Chinese consumers did not stop buying branded goods; they moved the purchase to wherever price discovery is easiest. For a foreign brand this reframes the threat. The competitor to your premium price is rarely a cheaper rival product — it is the price-transparent channel that lets the buyer compare you against one.

## The premium half of the barbell is intact and measurable

**A two-speed market means the premium half keeps growing, and the 2025 category data identifies exactly which shelves compounded.** Retail sales above designated size grew 20.9% for communications equipment, 15.7% for sports and recreational goods, 14.6% for furniture and 11.0% for household appliances, against 5.1% for cosmetics and 3.2% for clothing ([NBS, December 2025 retail release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260120_1962354.html)). These are not bargain categories — they are performance, experience and self-investment purchases. The premium that survives the downgrade is premium that proves itself: measurable function, visible results, credentials a buyer can check. What is not surviving is premium that asks to be taken on image alone, which is precisely what the shrinking brand-exclusive shelf used to sell.

## Imported goods sit on both ends of the barbell at once

**China's cross-border import mix in 2025 proves that imported demand has one foot in each speed of the market.** Cross-border e-commerce imports reached a record RMB 570.2 billion, up 2.7 percent, and all of it was consumer goods ([GACC statistics department, June 2026](http://tjs.customs.gov.cn/tjs/2026-06/16/article_2026061616113844507.html)). The mix is a barbell in itself: beauty, perfume and daily personal care took 27.2% and food and fresh produce 23.9% — replenishment categories where an imported staple earns repeat purchase at a controlled price — while health products lost 1.7 percentage points of share to 13.7%. In the first half of 2026 customs counted 140 million Chinese consumers buying through cross-border platforms ([State Council Information Office, July 2026](https://english.scio.gov.cn/pressroom/2026-07/22/content_118612088.html)). Demand is not evaporating; it is being rationed by category discipline.

## What the two-speed market means for a foreign brand's price position

**A price position in today's China has to be chosen, because the two speeds of the market reward opposite business models and punish the middle.** The value barbell works with the channel shift: bonded cross-border pricing is structurally lower than general-trade retail, so a replenishment SKU — food, daily care, household staples — can be priced for volume and ride the 1210 route without a local entity. The proof-of-premium barbell works only where the imported label is a trust signal — infant formula, beauty, supplements, spirits — and where the product substance survives scrutiny. The squeezed middle is the undifferentiated mid-priced good sold through brand-exclusive retail: it pays premium-channel costs for value-channel demand. ## Test which side of the barbell you are on before you commit

**The cheapest way to learn which speed of the market your brand wins in is a bonded pilot judged at full price.** Assumptions about premium are exactly what pilots exist to test: run the SKU through a 1210 bonded lane for a full season, hold the price, and watch sell-through and repeat purchase rate rather than headline volume ([the commitment-ladder method](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-market-entry-test-before-commit.html)). Correct for the sample's skew — cross-border buyers concentrate in wealthier coastal provinces, so a Shanghai price read is not a national one. If the pilot shows you winning on replenishment value, reprice for the value barbell before scaling; if repeat rate holds at premium, invest in the proof of premium. Decide with data before the general-trade commitment makes the decision expensive.
