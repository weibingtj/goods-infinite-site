China Pet Market Entry Roadmap for Foreign Brands: Registration, Channels and Timing

A category-specific China market entry roadmap for foreign pet food, supplement and pet-care brands: the GACC and MARA registration gates, the general-trade versus 1210 question, tariffs, and the channels where imported pet products actually sell.

Published 2026-09-07 · Last updated 2026-09-09 · By Bing Wei, Operations Director

Why pet needs its own China market entry roadmap

A China market entry roadmap for pet brands is a staged launch plan for pet food, supplements and pet-care products in mainland China, sequenced around import controls that most consumer categories never face. A handbag or cosmetic brand entering China deals with one regulator; a pet-food brand deals with two agencies, three access gates and a quarantine permit before the first bag of kibble can be sold. Generic entry advice misleads pet brands exactly where money is lost: formulations locked before access is checked, production scheduled before certificates exist, and channel plans built on cross-border shortcuts that pet food cannot actually use.

The China pet market is food-led, urban and structurally open to imported brands

The China pet market is the measured spending of China's urban dog and cat owners — approximately RMB 312.6 billion in 2025 — and its structure favours exactly the categories where Western brands are strongest. Dogs contributed RMB 160.6 billion and cats RMB 152.0 billion in 2025, cat spending grew faster (5.2% versus 3.2% for dogs), and the population reached 126 million pets — 72.9 million cats and 53.4 million dogs (Petdata 2026 White Paper). Three facts matter to an entrant:

  • Food is the category. Pet food holds a 53.7 percent share of total spend, with growth concentrated in premium dry, functional treats and fresh-style nutrition; average annual spend is RMB 3,006 per dog and RMB 2,085 per cat and rising (2026 China Pet Industry White Paper).
  • Buyers are young and platform-native. Owners born in the 1990s are 42.7 percent of the market and Gen Z another 26.3 percent — a demographic that researches on Xiaohongshu, buys on Tmall and JD and follows Douyin live commerce.
  • Imported brands still hold premium credibility, positioned and priced above domestic mass-market lines — precisely the slot premiumisation rewards.

Imported pet food is regulated as animal feed, and three gates control access

Access control for imported pet food is a three-layer system run by the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) at the border and the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs (MARA) at the point of sale, and every layer must clear before a formula can be legally sold in China. Because pet food is classified as animal feed rather than human food, the familiar food-import playbooks do not apply.

Gate 1 — country and product access (GACC). China publishes a dynamic list of countries and regions permitted to export feed, and pet food may come only from countries on it. Ingredient restrictions apply too: since March 2023, for example, GACC has suspended US-produced pet food containing beef, mutton, rabbit or related animal-derived ingredients. Check the approved feed country and product list before planning a formula.

Gate 2 — factory registration with GACC. Every manufacturing plant must be registered on GACC's register of overseas pet-food manufacturers, applied for through the exporting country's competent authority and valid for five years. Unregistered plants cannot ship to China, and approval runs on government calendars rather than commercial ones. Pet food is treated as a high-risk feed, and the revised Feed and Feed Additive Import and Export Inspection and Quarantine Supervision Measures (GACC Order No. 283, effective 1 November 2026) adds a formal I-to-IV product risk-grading system.

Gate 3 — MARA product import registration. Under the Measures for the Administration of Import Registration of Feed and Feed Additives (MARA Order No. 2 of 2014) and the pet-feed rules in MARA Announcement No. 20 of 2018, each complete-food and supplement-premix formula needs a MARA import registration certificate before it can be sold in China. The certificate is valid for five years, must be renewed no later than six months before expiry, and is filed through a China-based agent; since January 2026 MARA issues it as an electronic licence.

Two further steps gate the physical shipment. Most pet food requires an advance quarantine permit, obtained before the trade contract is signed — canned dog and cat food (HS 2309.1010) is the exception, exempted by GACC Announcement No. 51 of 2018. At the border, the importer must present the exporting country's official veterinary health certificate and certificate of origin, and the packaging must carry a Chinese-language label compliant with the pet-feed labelling rules issued under the 2018 measures.

Pet food is feed first and e-commerce second — the cross-border (1210) caveat

The 1210 bonded-warehouse model is a China retail-import mechanism in which goods are pre-positioned in a supervised customs zone and released against individual consumer orders, and for pet food it is available only under conditions most new entrants will not meet on day one. Retail-packed dog and cat food does appear on China's cross-border retail import list, but with a restrictive note — bonded (1210) mode only, and only for products that have already obtained quarantine market access (MOFCOM cross-border retail import policy). The access and registration obligations are the same as general trade, which is why the reliable route for a food brand is general trade through a licensed importer, with 1210 reserved for later optimisation once approvals exist. Pet supplies face no such gate and can use the standard cross-border routes described in our guide to the 1210 model.

The duty arithmetic is worth modelling before pricing. Retail-packed dog and cat food (HS 2309.1010 and 2309.1090) carries an MFN duty of 15 percent, reduced to a 10 percent temporary rate under the Tariff Commission's 2026 adjustment plan (effective 1 January 2026), plus 9 percent import VAT at the feed rate and no consumption tax (Tariff Commission of the State Council; State Taxation Administration). Two variations move the number materially: formulas produced in Australia or New Zealand are commonly charged zero duty under China's free-trade agreements, while US-origin goods can incur additional retaliatory duties. Our guide to do you need a China import licence places pet food inside the wider licensing picture.

The realistic pet-brand timeline: 6–12 months to a legal first shipment

A realistic China entry timeline for a new pet brand is six to twelve months, because MARA certificates, GACC factory registration and quarantine access each run on government calendars that no amount of marketing spend can shorten. The sequence that protects the calendar:

  • Month 0–1 — trademark and formula. China is first-to-file; register the Latin and Chinese marks before any factory, agent or platform conversation, and lock the formula — every subsequent approval names it. See our trademark registration guide.
  • Month 1–2 — check the three gates. Confirm the country is on GACC's approved list, verify or start the factory registration through your national authority, and appoint the China-based agent for the MARA filing. Most brands lose a quarter discovering a restriction only after production planning.
  • Month 2–5 — MARA filing and label build. File the import registration for each formula and commission the Chinese label against the pet-feed labelling rules in parallel.
  • Month 5–8 — permit, shipment, clearance. Obtain the advance quarantine permit for non-canned products, ship with the official health certificate, and clear through the importer of record under general trade.
  • Month 6–12 — channel build. Open the platform flagship store, seed on Xiaohongshu and Douyin once stock is legally in-country, then begin distributor conversations for offline retail.

Channels that actually sell imported pet food in China

A distribution channel in the China pet market is any route through which a registered imported product reaches an owner — platform flagship stores, social-commerce seeding and offline premium retail — and imported brands typically run all three in sequence once approvals exist. Online, the Tmall or JD flagship builds the trust-led base while Douyin live commerce converts discovery into volume, and Xiaohongshu is where young owners form brand preference before purchase. Offline, imported premium brands matter in high-end pet retail and the trade-show circuit such as Asia Pet Fair, where distribution relationships are formed — a route covered in depth in our guide to finding a China distributor.

The principle underneath this roadmap is the same one that governs every regulated category: start the slow government work the day the entry decision is made, sell nothing until every certificate exists, and let approvals — not the marketing plan — set the launch date. FAQ

Does a foreign pet brand need a China entity to import pet food? No. China's registration rules require an overseas pet-food maker to appoint a China-based agent or a China branch office to file the MARA import registration, and customs clearance needs a licensed importer of record in China — but neither step forces you to incorporate a WFOE. A Hong Kong trading entity plus a mainland import agent, the structure most first-time brands use, satisfies both obligations.

How long does it take to start selling imported pet food in China? Budget six to twelve months from a standing start. If your factory is not yet on the GACC register of overseas pet-food manufacturers, that approval runs through your national authority to GACC and takes months on its own; the MARA import registration certificate for each complete-food and supplement-premix formula typically adds several more months. Brands whose factory is already registered and whose formula matches an existing approval can compress the timeline substantially.

Can imported pet food be sold through the 1210 bonded cross-border channel? Retail-packed dog and cat food appears in the cross-border e-commerce retail import list, but with a restrictive note: bonded (1210) mode only, and only for products that have already obtained quarantine market access. Because the same GACC access and MARA registration still apply, most imported pet-food brands in practice enter through general trade, and many platforms have restricted bonded pet-food listings. Pet supplies such as toys, grooming and litter are not feed and face no such gate.

What import duty and VAT apply to pet food in China? Retail-packed dog and cat food (HS 2309.1010 canned and 2309.1090 other) carries an MFN duty of 15 percent, reduced to a 10 percent temporary rate under China's annual tariff adjustment plans, plus 9 percent import VAT, and no consumption tax. Formulas manufactured in Australia or New Zealand are commonly charged 0 percent duty under China's free-trade agreements. US-origin pet food can face additional retaliatory duties on top of the base rate.

What is the most common mistake foreign pet brands make entering China? Booking production and freight before the three access gates are cleared. Shipping a container before the MARA import registration certificate exists, before the factory is on the GACC register, or without the advance quarantine permit for non-canned products invites detention or return at the border — and none of these can be fixed after the goods are in transit. The second most common error is appointing a national distributor before the trademark is filed and the registration is done.
