Importing Chemicals Into China: MEE Registration, Hazardous Chemical Rules and What 'China REACH' Actually Requires

There is no single 'China REACH' certificate. Importing a chemical into China runs four or five parallel approval tracks — MEE new-substance registration, hazardous chemical registration, customs inspection, toxic chemical release, precursor licensing — and each one has its own clock, its own applicant rules and its own penalty.

Published 2026-10-26 · Last updated 2026-10-28 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

Definition: what "China REACH" actually means

"China REACH" is not a Chinese legal term. It is the informal name importers give to China's new chemical substance regime — the Measures for the Environmental Management Registration of New Chemical Substances (新化学物质环境管理登记办法, Ministry of Ecology and Environment Order No. 12), published 29 April 2020 and in force 1 January 2021, which replaced the 2010 rules (former MEP Order No. 7).

Calling it REACH is useful shorthand but dangerous if taken literally, because EU REACH is one regulation with one dossier and one deadline, while China's system is a set of parallel tracks that a single shipment can trip at the same time. A chemical consignment arriving at a Chinese port may need:

  1. MEE new-substance registration if the substance is not on the Inventory of Existing Chemical Substances in China (IECSC) — plus a Chinese agent if you apply from abroad.
  2. Hazardous chemical registration if it appears in the national Hazardous Chemicals Catalogue — plus a business licence if you sell it.
  3. Customs inspection with a Chinese-language GHS label and Chinese safety data sheet.
  4. A toxic chemical release notice (放行通知单) if it is on the Catalogue of Toxic Chemicals Strictly Restricted for Import and Export in China.
  5. A MOFCOM import licence if it is a listed drug precursor.

The five tracks have different applicants, different clocks, different competent authorities and different penalties. This article walks through each one with the article numbers, so you can check your own substance against the official text rather than against a consultant's slide.

Step 1 — Check the IECSC first, because it decides everything else

Under Art. 3 of MEE Order No. 12, a "new chemical substance" is simply a chemical substance not listed on the IECSC. If your substance is on the inventory, it is managed as an existing substance and no new-substance registration is required. There is an important trap: some inventory entries carry a new-use environmental management notation, and using such a substance for an industrial use other than the permitted one puts it back into the "new" category (Art. 3, second paragraph).

The IECSC is maintained and published by MEE. Its 2013 base edition (Announcement No. 1 of 2013) contained 45,612 substances, and it is supplemented in batches — most recently by MEE Announcement No. 40 of 2026 (4 August 2026), which added 4 qualifying substances and 482 already-registered new chemical substances.

Two exclusions matter commercially (Art. 2):

  • Finished products are out. Medicines, pesticides, veterinary drugs, cosmetics, food, food additives, feed, feed additives and fertilisers are not covered — but a new chemical substance used as a raw material or intermediate for those products is covered. If you supply an intermediate to a Chinese cosmetic or food manufacturer, you are in scope even though the finished product is not.
  • Re-export through a bonded zone is out. Substances imported and stored in a special customs supervision zone and exported in full without any processing are excluded. The moment they are processed, the exclusion is lost.

One inclusion catches people out: articles designed to intentionally release a new chemical substance during normal use are in scope for the substance they contain.

10 t · 1 t · < 1 tThe three tonnage bands that decide which MEE track you are on: 10 tonnes or more per year produced or imported requires regular registration (常规登记); 1 tonne or more but under 10 tonnes requires simplified registration (简易登记); under 1 tonne — or a polymer whose monomer or reactant content does not exceed 2%, or a low-concern polymer — requires filing (备案). A substance with a regular registration certificate is added to the IECSC five years after first registration.Source: MEE Order No. 12, Arts. 10 and 44

Three practical points that change planning:

  1. Ecotoxicology must use Chinese test organisms. Art. 15 and Art. 16 both require ecotoxicology test reports to include data generated with test organisms of China under the relevant standards. Data packages built only for EU or US submission usually need top-up testing. Domestic labs must hold testing-institution qualification and, for health/eco toxicology, comply with GLP; overseas labs must meet internationally accepted GLP requirements (Art. 14).
  2. Certificate contents are narrow, and they bind you. Art. 26 lists what the certificate states: type, applicant and agent, identifiers, the applied use, the applied tonnage, activity type and risk control measures. Exceeding the tonnage, switching from import to production, changing the use or changing the risk control measures all require a fresh application (Art. 29). A successful trial order therefore does not authorise commercial volume.
  3. Filing does not entitle you to be listed on the IECSC. Only regular registration leads to inclusion, five years after first registration (Art. 44); simplified and filed substances are expressly not listed.

The post-registration obligations nobody budgets for

Registration is not the end. Under Chapter IV of MEE Order No. 12:

  • Downstream information transfer (Art. 38): certificate or filing receipt number, applied use, hazard characteristics and risk control measures, and environmental management requirements must be passed to downstream users, who may demand them.
  • Records (Art. 39): regular and simplified registration materials and activity records must be kept at least 10 years; filing materials and activity records at least 3 years.
  • Public disclosure (Art. 40): holders of regular registrations must publish implementation of risk control measures via their own website or another publicly accessible channel.
  • First activity report (Art. 41): within 60 days of first production, or of first import and transfer to a processor using the substance.
  • Annual report (Art. 41): where the certificate requires it, by 30 April each year from the year after registration.
  • New hazard reporting (Art. 42): any newly identified environmental or health hazard or risk must be reported, and MEE may then vary or withdraw the certificate.

Step 3 — If it is on the Hazardous Chemicals Catalogue, a second regulator appears

The environmental track and the safety track are independent. A substance can be an existing substance on the IECSC (no MEE registration) and still be a listed hazardous chemical (full safety regime). The governing instrument is the Regulation on the Safety Management of Hazardous Chemicals (State Council Order No. 591, in force 1 December 2011, amended by State Council Order No. 645 of 2013), which defines a hazardous chemical by its properties — toxic, corrosive, explosive, flammable, oxidising — and delegates the catalogue to the competent authority.

Order No. 53's review chain is fully timed: the provincial registration office makes a preliminary review within 3 working days, reviews materials within 20 working days, and the national registration centre completes its review within 15 working days (Art. 13).

A caution on the documents. Arts. 14 and 31 of Order No. 53 list the foreign-trade operator registration form (对外贸易经营者备案登记表) among the documents an importer must produce. That registration was abolished on 30 December 2022, when the NPC Standing Committee deleted Art. 9 of the Foreign Trade Law. The 2012 rule has not been re-issued to match, so confirm with the provincial registration office which substitute documents it accepts before you assemble the file.

Enforcement is real and it is published. In a case released by the Shanghai Municipal Emergency Management Bureau in January 2025, a trading company in Changning District was found on inspection to have imported six catalogue chemicals, including UV-curing adhesive and a nano dispersion, without completing registration. It was fined RMB 20,000 on 9 July 2024 under Art. 78(1)(12) of State Council Order No. 591.

Step 4 — Customs inspection: the Chinese label and the Chinese SDS

Even with every certificate in hand, the shipment is inspected at the border. GACC Announcement No. 129 of 2020 (issued 18 December 2020, in force 10 January 2021, replacing former AQSIQ Announcement No. 30 of 2012) sets out what customs checks for chemicals on the current national catalogue.

At declaration, the consignee or its agent must enter the hazard class, packing group (except for bulk), UN number and UN packaging mark (except for bulk), and provide:

  1. a Declaration of Conformity for Importing Hazardous Chemical Enterprises (prescribed form, Annex 1);
  2. for products requiring inhibitors or stabilisers, a statement of the actual inhibitor or stabiliser added and the quantity;
  3. samples of the Chinese hazardous-property label and the Chinese safety data sheet.

Customs then inspects whether the main components, physical and chemical characteristics and hazard class conform to the applicable requirements, whether the package carries a Chinese label and is accompanied by a Chinese SDS, and whether the packaging type, mark, category, specification, unit weight and condition conform (Announcement No. 129, Sections 5–6). Where the chemical is used as a food or food additive, food safety rules also apply.

The standards behind those labels are the GHS implementation standards — the GB 30000 series on classification and labelling of chemicals and GB 15258 on the preparation of chemical safety labels, with SDS format standards including GB/T 17519-2013. Chinese-language labels and SDS are not a translation afterthought; they are a customs inspection item, which means a labelling defect stops the container, not the marketing launch.

Step 5 — Two lists that can stop the shipment outright

Toxic chemicals strictly restricted for import and export

China implements the Stockholm, Minamata and Rotterdam conventions plus its own new-pollutants agenda through the Catalogue of Toxic Chemicals Strictly Restricted for Import and Export in China, published in October 2023 by MEE, MOFCOM and GACC, replacing the 2020 edition. The current catalogue covers nine categories including PFOS and its salts, mercury, tetra-methyl and tetra-ethyl lead, polychlorinated terphenyls, tributyltin compounds, short-chain chlorinated paraffins, decabromodiphenyl ether, and PFOA and its salts and related compounds.

Anyone importing or exporting a listed toxic chemical must apply to MEE for a Toxic Chemical Import/Export Environmental Management Release Notice (有毒化学品进(出)口环境管理放行通知单) and present that notice to customs to clear the goods. There is no discretionary alternative route.

Overlapping this, the Key Controlled New Pollutants List (2023 edition) (MEE and five other departments, Order No. 28, in force 1 March 2023) sets per-substance control measures. For PFOS, for example, the listed measures required a release notice for any import or export and then prohibited import and export from 1 January 2024. If your substance appears on either list, the commercial question is usually not "how do we register it" but "is it still importable at all".

Drug precursors

The Regulation on the Administration of Precursor Chemicals (State Council Order No. 445, in force 1 November 2005, thrice amended) applies a classified licensing system to production, trading, purchase, transport and import/export. Import requires:

  • an import licence from MOFCOM or a commerce department it delegates, issued after review within 20 days (Arts. 26 and 27);
  • an international verification procedure for substances on the verification catalogue, with verification time excluded from the licensing period (Art. 29);
  • presentation of the licence to customs, which clears the goods against it (Art. 30).

Two provisions catch importers by surprise. First, moving precursors from abroad into a bonded zone, export processing zone or other special customs supervision area is treated as import and needs the licence; movements within China into or between those zones do not (Art. 30, paragraphs 2 and 3). Second, Art. 5 makes the regime cumulative: where a precursor is also a drug or a hazardous chemical, the drug and hazardous chemical rules apply on top. Import and export of precursors outside the licensing system carries criminal liability.

The fines are not the real risk for most importers. The 1-year and 3-year application bans are, because they freeze your entire China pipeline across every substance in your portfolio, not just the one that failed.

The eight-step sequence we run before a chemical shipment

  1. Fix the substance identity — CAS number, composition, and whether the product is a substance, a mixture or an article that intentionally releases.
  2. Screen the IECSC — on the inventory (existing) or off it (new)? If on it, check for a new-use management notation on your intended use.
  3. Screen the Hazardous Chemicals Catalogue (2015 edition) — if listed, plan hazardous chemical registration before first import, and a business licence if you will sell domestically.
  4. Screen the restricted toxic chemicals catalogue and the new pollutants list — if listed, apply for the MEE release notice, or confirm whether the substance is already prohibited.
  5. Screen the precursor schedules — if listed, build the MOFCOM import licence and international verification into the shipment timeline.
  6. Choose the MEE track by real forecast tonnage — regular, simplified or filing — and generate the Chinese-organism ecotoxicology data early, because that is the long pole.
  7. Appoint the Chinese agent and sign the agency contract — MEE requires the applicant and agent to jointly perform obligations; the contract must cover replacement of the agent and run for the life of the certificate.
  8. Prepare the Chinese GHS label and Chinese SDS before shipment — plus the Declaration of Conformity, the UN number and packaging marks for the declaration, and confirm the receiving warehouse is licensed for the hazard class.

Where a bonded model does and does not fit

Bonded warehousing is a genuine advantage for non-hazardous industrial chemicals, raw materials and formulated products: you can land stock, hold it under customs supervision, and release it into the domestic market as orders arrive, deferring duty and VAT until release. We operate 6,000㎡ of bonded warehousing and 8,000㎡ of temperature- and humidity-controlled warehousing through our network, alongside 300+ enterprises served and our own AEO advanced certification plus 15 partner AEO facilities, with fulfilment across five Chinese cities.

It is equally important to say where it does not fit. A listed hazardous chemical cannot sit in a conventional bonded warehouse. Art. 24 of State Council Order No. 591 requires storage in a dedicated warehouse, dedicated site or dedicated storage room under a designated person, with additional safety facilities, and a business licence is required for warehousing operations. For those products the correct structure is a licensed dangerous-goods warehouse, a qualified domestic carrier and an emergency response arrangement — and the classification has to be settled before the vessel sails.

What we deliberately do not assert

This article stays with what the official text says. Four things commonly claimed in vendor and law-firm content we will not repeat:

  1. There is no official fee schedule or fixed calendar duration for MEE registration, so we give none. The statute fixes technical review and decision periods, not total elapsed time; the variable is testing.
  2. We do not claim an equivalence or mutual recognition between EU REACH and MEE Order No. 12. Art. 14 permits overseas test institutions that meet internationally accepted GLP requirements to generate health and eco toxicology data, which reduces duplication, but it is not recognition of a foreign registration.
  3. We do not state a customs clearance time for chemical imports. Clearance depends on declaration mode, inspection selection and enterprise credit grade; no single statutory figure exists.
  4. We do not advise whether a specific substance is or is not on a catalogue. That is a substance-by-substance determination against the current published lists and MEE's technical identification service, not a general conclusion.
