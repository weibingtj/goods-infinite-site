## WFOE exit is one liquidation and a queue of deregistrations, and the law now writes the timetable

**A WFOE exit is the lawful closing of a wholly foreign-owned Chinese company: a dissolution trigger, a statutory liquidation, and parallel cancellations of the tax, customs and market-regulator registrations, ending with the company being struck from the company register.** Nothing about it is fast, and since the 2023 revision of the Company Law took effect on 1 July 2024, nothing about it is optional either — the timetable is written into the statute itself ([Company Law full text, effective 1 July 2024](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202312/content_6923395.htm)).

Exit starts with a dissolution cause under Article 229: expiry of the business term, a shareholders' resolution, merger or division, revocation of the business licence, or a court order — publicised on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System within ten days. The other registrations then follow in a fixed order, because each authority wants proof the previous one is clear: tax before customs, customs before the final company deregistration.

## Directors are the liquidation obligors, and the duty is personal

**Liquidation is the statutory winding-down process in which a liquidation group collects assets, settles claims and distributes what remains, and under Article 232 the company's directors are personally its liquidation obligors.** Once a dissolution cause appears, the directors have fifteen days to form the liquidation group. This is the single sharpest change the 2023 revision brought to exits: the obligation attaches to named individuals, not to the company as an abstraction.

Article 238 backs it with money: members owe fiduciary and diligence duties, negligence that loses the company money is compensable, and losses caused to creditors by intent or gross negligence are personally compensable too. A board that lets a wound-down subsidiary sit unliquidated carries that exposure the entire time.

## The creditor clock runs in fixed days, and no one gets paid during it

**Creditor notification is the liquidation stage in which every known creditor is individually told of the winding-down and given a window to file claims, and Article 235 fixes its length.** The liquidation group notifies known creditors within ten days of formation and announces to unknown ones on the publicity system or in a newspaper for sixty days. A creditor notified directly has thirty days to file; one reached only by announcement has forty-five. During the claim window the group may not pay anyone.

Article 235 also forces the register: claims must be itemised, evidenced and logged by the group, which is why accounts in good order shorten the exit while sloppy ones stretch it.

## The asset waterfall clears wages and tax before shareholders see anything

**The liquidation waterfall is the statutory payment order applied once assets are inventoried, and Article 236 writes it in one line: liquidation costs, then employee wages, social insurance and statutory compensation, then taxes owed, then ordinary debts, and only the remainder to shareholders.** The liquidation plan needs confirmation by the shareholders' meeting or the court before execution, the company may conduct no business unrelated to the liquidation, and no shareholder distribution is permitted before the waterfall is complete.

Article 237 closes the trapdoor: if assets cannot cover the debts, the group must apply to the court for bankruptcy and hand the file to a court-appointed administrator. A WFOE that is exit-ready at entry — modest receivables, no processing-trade residue, tax filings current — exits through Article 236; one that is not exits through bankruptcy.

## Tax deregistration comes first, and it is where exits stall

**Tax deregistration (清税) is the cancellation of a company's taxpayer status, and the Implementation Rules of the Tax Collection and Administration Law require it before the market-regulator deregistration.** Article 15 directs a company dissolving to apply for tax deregistration before applying for company deregistration, and Article 16 sets the price of admission: settle all taxes due, late-payment surcharges and fines, and surrender invoices and tax registration certificates ([Implementation Rules, Articles 15–16](https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/chinatax/n810214/c102374/c102377/c102378/c3575330/content.html)).

In practice this is the slowest gate: the tax authority reviews filings, invoices and intercompany balances before issuing the clearance, and an entity claiming VAT refunds or running transfer prices should expect the file to be examined line by line. Exits stall here more often than at any other desk.

## Trading entities must close customs before leaving

**Customs filing cancellation is the removal of a company from the customs register of declaration entities, required under Article 10 of the customs filing rules when the company dissolves, is deregistered or loses its licence.** A customs filing is now valid indefinitely, so it does not lapse on its own — it must be actively cancelled ([GACC Order 252, effective 1 January 2022](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/2024-01/05/article_2025121420391589986.html)).

Article 11 sets the condition: all outstanding customs procedures must be completed before the filing can be cancelled. For a trading WFOE that means bonded inventory disposed of, processing-trade manuals closed and duties settled, and any bonded goods that are unsaleable and cannot be re-exported handled through the destruction channel — since April 2025 the procedure is GACC Announcement 2025 No. 79, with video records kept for at least three years.

## The final deregistration has a 30-day deadline, and there is a shortcut

**Company deregistration is the striking of the entity from the company register, which ends its legal personality, and Article 32 of the Market Entity Registration Regulation sets the deadline: apply within thirty days of the liquidation ending.** The liquidation report must be confirmed by the shareholders and filed, branches deregistered first, and the licence surrendered ([State Council Order 746, in force 1 March 2022](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-08/24/content_5632964.htm)).

<div class="statcard"><span class="n">30 days</span><span class="l">Window to apply for final company deregistration once the liquidation is complete, with branches deregistered first</span><span class="src">Source: Market Entity Registration Regulation (State Council Order 746), Article 32, in force 1 March 2022</span></div>

Companies with no debts — or all debts cleared — can skip the full liquidation. Article 240 of the Company Law and Article 33 of the Regulation allow simple deregistration on a written commitment from all shareholders, announced publicly for at least twenty days, with the application due within twenty days after. The commitment is not a formality: if it proves false, shareholders are jointly liable for debts arising before the deregistration.

## Walking away leaves the liability attached to your shareholders

**Involuntary deregistration is the registration authority's power to strike a defunct company itself, and Article 241 applies it to a company whose licence was revoked after three years without a deregistration application, following a public notice of at least sixty days.** The Article states the consequence plainly: the original shareholders and the liquidation obligors are unaffected in their liability. A licence revoked for cause also fires the Article 232 clock, with directors' personal exposure running while the shell sits dormant.

That is the argument for pricing the exit at entry. Capital paid into a WFOE returns only through dividends, an equity transfer or liquidation, and the liquidation door has statutory minimums at every step. ## FAQ

**How long does it take to liquidate and deregister a WFOE in China?** There is no fixed statutory total, but the minimum clocks alone are heavy: ten days to publicise the dissolution cause, fifteen days to form the liquidation group, at least sixty days of creditor announcement plus a 30- or 45-day claim window, and tax clearance before anything else moves. A clean WFOE typically completes in around six to twelve months; disputes, audits or bonded inventory extend it.

**Can a foreign parent just abandon its Chinese WFOE and walk away?** It can walk away, but the liability does not stay behind. Article 241 allows involuntary deregistration of a revoked-licence company only after three years of inaction and a 60-day public notice, and shareholders' and liquidation obligors' liability is expressly unaffected. Directors who miss the fifteen-day liquidation-group deadline compensate the company and creditors for losses.

**When can a WFOE use the simple deregistration procedure?** Only when it never incurred debts or has cleared them all, with a written commitment from all shareholders. The announcement runs at least twenty days on the publicity system, then the application is due within twenty days. If the commitment proves false, shareholders are jointly liable for pre-deregistration debts, and companies on the abnormal-operations list cannot use the procedure at all.

**What happens to remaining inventory when a WFOE closes?** It must be cleared before the customs filing can be cancelled. Bonded stock can be re-exported, transferred to another bonded entity, sold domestically with taxes paid, or destroyed under GACC Announcement 2025 No. 79 with video records kept for at least three years. Processing-trade manuals must be closed and duties settled first.

**Who gets paid first in a WFOE liquidation?** Company Law Article 236 fixes the order: liquidation costs, then employee wages, social insurance and statutory compensation, then taxes owed, then ordinary debts, and only the remainder to shareholders. No distribution to shareholders is permitted before the waterfall completes, and creditors may not be paid during the claim window.
