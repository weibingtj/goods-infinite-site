## Why GACC registration is the first gate for imported food

China is one of the world's largest food-importing markets, and appetite for imported snacks, dairy, wine, and specialty foods keeps climbing. But food is handled differently from cosmetics or general merchandise: before a single carton reaches a Chinese port, the factory that made it usually has to be pre-approved by Chinese Customs. This guide explains exactly when that approval is required, how the system works, and how to plan around it.

## GACC registration is the pre-clearance that lets an overseas food factory export to China

GACC registration (境外生产企业注册) is the mandatory pre-clearance administered by the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) that authorizes an overseas manufacturer to export a covered food category to mainland China. Under GACC Decree 248 (《进口食品境外生产企业注册管理规定》), which took effect on 1 January 2022, overseas manufacturers of the 18 higher-risk categories of imported food must be registered before their products can clear Chinese customs ([GACC](https://www.customs.gov.cn)).

The reform consolidated a patchwork of rules into one framework and shifted the burden onto the overseas manufacturer rather than the importer alone. If your plant is not on the GACC register, your goods will be refused at the border — regardless of how strong demand is on the Chinese side.

## Two tracks: registration vs. information filing

The GACC system splits imported food into two compliance tracks — a registration track for high-risk categories and an information-filing track for the rest — so the regulatory burden matches the risk profile rather than applying a blanket rule. Products on the registration track need a full GACC review and approval of the manufacturing site. Products on the information-filing track rely on the China importer recording the manufacturer's details and a self-declaration of compliance with Chinese food-safety standards ([SAMR](https://www.samr.gov.cn)).

This two-track design is the single most important thing for a new brand to understand: "Do I need GACC registration?" is the wrong question. The right question is "Which track does my product fall into?"

## Which products fall under the 18 categories

The 18 registered categories are the higher-risk imported foods — such as meat and poultry, aquatic products, dairy, bird's nest, edible oils, grains, canned food, health food, infant formula and food additives — that GACC screens before export. A representative set includes:

- Meat and meat products, poultry, and aquatic animals and their products
- Dairy products and bird's nest (燕窝)
- Edible vegetable oils, grains, canned goods, and aquatic plants
- Fruit, vegetables, nuts and seeds
- Health food (保健食品), foods for special medical purpose (FSMP), and infant formula
- Food additives

If your SKU sits in one of these, registration is non-negotiable. If it does not, your China importer can usually complete the lighter information-filing path. When in doubt, confirm the classification with a licensed broker before you ship, because mis-categorisation is a common cause of border rejections.

## The documents you actually need

A GACC registration dossier is the package of documents — legal entity proof, a HACCP or equivalent quality-management system, and product-category specifics — that proves an overseas plant meets Chinese food-safety equivalence. The core documents typically include:

- The manufacturer's business licence and legal entity proof
- A HACCP plan or equivalent food-safety management certificate
- A recommendation or attestation from the home-country competent authority (many categories require the national food-safety agency to endorse the application)
- Product specifications, labels, and a declaration of compliance with Chinese standards
- For category-specific items (e.g. health food), additional dossiers on function claims and safety

Applications are lodged through the GACC "Import Food Enterprises Registration System" (进口食品境外生产企业注册管理系统), almost always via the China importer or a qualified agent acting on the manufacturer's behalf.

## Timelines, validity and renewal

GACC registration validity is generally tied to the manufacturer's export licence and is subject to annual or category-specific renewal, so planning the clock in is critical. Many registrations complete in a few weeks once the dossier and the home-country recommendation are in place; first-time or complex categories can take several months. Registrations can also be suspended or revoked if a plant fails follow-up inspections or is linked to a food-safety incident — ongoing compliance, not just the initial approval, keeps you on the list.

## Where the China importer fits

A China import record-filed entity (进口商备案) is the local party that lodges shipments, holds the importer qualification, and is jointly accountable for food safety with the overseas manufacturer. Under the 1210 cross-border model, this role can be filled by a Hong Kong or mainland importer of record — such as GOODSINFINITE TRADE LIMITED — so a foreign brand does not need to set up a mainland company on day one. The importer's responsibilities include:

- Holding the import record-filing with GACC
- Matching each shipment to a valid manufacturer registration
- Managing labelling review and the required Chinese-language information
- Handling customs declaration, tax, and traceability records

## Common mistakes food brands make

- **Assuming all food is treated equally.** Treating a high-risk category as a simple filing leads to a refused shipment at the port.
- **Starting registration after onboarding.** Because registration can take months, it must run in parallel with Tmall Global or JD Worldwide setup, not after it.
- **Skipping the home-country endorsement.** Many categories require your national food-safety authority to recommend the plant; brands that omit it stall.
- **Ignoring label review.** Chinese labelling rules (ingredients, nutrition, claims) are strict; fix the label before production, not at the border.

## FAQ

**Do all imported foods need GACC registration?** No. GACC Decree 248 requires overseas-manufacturer registration only for the 18 higher-risk categories of imported food. Everything else is handled through a lighter importer information-filing path.

**What is the difference between registration and information filing?** Registration is a GACC review and approval of the overseas manufacturer before export, required for the 18 high-risk categories. Information filing is the lighter path for remaining foods, where the China importer records the manufacturer's details in the GACC system.

**How long does GACC registration take?** Timelines vary by category and country. Many registrations complete in a few weeks once the dossier and the home-country recommendation are in place; complex or first-time categories can take several months.

**Can a foreign brand register itself, or does the importer do it?** The overseas manufacturer is the registrant of record, but the registration is lodged through the China importer or a qualified agent via the GACC Import Food Enterprises Registration System. A registered China import entity is also required to lodge shipments.
