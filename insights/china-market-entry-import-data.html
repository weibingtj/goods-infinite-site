China Market Entry: What Import Data Really Shows

China publishes its import record monthly, by eight-digit code and origin country, for free — and it is the only market-size figure in the country that no agency has an incentive to inflate. This guide shows how to read it before commissioning research: the 8,972-line tariff problem, why cross-border e-commerce is 3.1% of the import bill rather than the market, why import value is not retail value, and a four-week sizing sequence that produces a go/no-go range first.

Published 2026-11-27 · Last updated 2026-11-27 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

The import record is the one market-size number in China nobody can argue with

Customs import statistics are the declared value and quantity of goods crossing a border, compiled by the importing administration from the declarations filed at the port, and they are the cheapest credible way to size a category before committing a research budget. China publishes its version monthly and in full, at eight-digit commodity code by country of origin by destination province, and it is free. In 2025 China imported RMB 18.48 trillion of goods, up 0.5% on the year and its 17th consecutive year as the world's second-largest import market (GACC 2025 annual data). Every market-size number a foreign brand is ever quoted sits inside that figure somewhere.

The attraction is that the customs record is a census, not a sample. Nothing is extrapolated, nothing is projected forward, and no agency has an incentive in the answer.

RMB 18.48 trillionChina's total goods imports in 2025, up 0.5% year on year, the 17th consecutive year as the world's second-largest import marketSource: General Administration of Customs of China, annual trade release, January 2026

What the database contains, and the one field it never contains

A customs statistical record is an aggregated declaration, published by commodity code, country of origin, destination province, trade mode and enterprise type. The trade-mode split is the most under-used dimension: general trade, processing trade and cross-border e-commerce retail (the 1210 bonded and 9610 direct-purchase modes) are separated, which tells you whether a category is sold into China or merely assembled there. The enterprise-type split separates state-owned, foreign-invested and private importers, which tells you whether foreign brands already dominate the pool.

What the record never contains is an importer's name. GACC publishes aggregates and does not release the company on the declaration, so the "China customs data with importer contact details" that data vendors sell is not customs data at all — it is usually bill-of-lading material from other jurisdictions or an assembled directory. Budget customs data for sizing the pool and trade fairs plus channel checks for finding the buyers; the two jobs are not interchangeable.

Your product is hiding inside one of 8,972 tariff lines

A tariff line is the eight-digit code under which a product is declared, and the 2026 Chinese tariff contains 8,972 of them, of which 935 carry a temporary import rate below the MFN level (2026 tariff adjustment plan, effective 1 January 2026). Declarations are actually filed at ten digits: the extra two are national sub-headings GACC creates wherever a good needs a different rate, a licence or its own statistical identity.

So the six-digit level — the one that is internationally comparable and therefore the one quoted in every consultancy report — is almost always far broader than your product. Eight digits is the Chinese-specific sizing unit; ten digits is what your broker will file. Pull all three, and pick the one that matches what you actually ship. Reading the wrong line does not only give you the wrong market size, it also mis-signals your duty and your licensing route (HS code classification).

Value and volume answer two different questions

Import value is the CIF value assessed by customs at the border, while import volume is the quantity declared in the supplementary unit, so the two series diverge whenever world prices move. A consumer brand looking at a flat value line is very often looking at a price effect rather than collapsing demand. In 2025 total import value grew just 0.5% in RMB terms, yet machinery and electronics imports reached RMB 7.41 trillion, up 5.7%, and agricultural imports ran close to RMB 1.5 trillion (GACC). Falling commodity prices took the headline down while underlying demand rose.

Currency is the second distortion. The same year's import bill was USD 2.58 trillion at the annual average rate used by customs, so a USD series compared against a 2024 USD series can flip sign purely on translation. Take the RMB series and state your conversion rate.

Origin mix is the closest thing to competitive share

Origin-country share is the proportion of a commodity code's import value supplied by each exporting country, and it is the only competitive metric the customs record gives away for free. For a brand that already sells in other markets, the comparison is diagnostic: if your country supplies 4% of Chinese imports in a category while supplying 20% of world exports in the same category, that gap is displaceable share — either nobody has bothered to sell into China, or something regulatory is blocking it, and both are worth knowing before you commit.

In 2025 China's imports from more than 130 countries and regions grew, seven more than in 2024, so the aggregate hides a fairly churned base (GACC). A category where the origin mix has shifted in the last three years is a category where entry is still possible.

Cross-border e-commerce is 3.1% of the import bill, not the market

Cross-border e-commerce retail import is the channel in which goods are sold to Chinese consumers before duty is paid on arrival, cleared under the 1210 bonded or 9610 direct-purchase modes, and in 2025 it carried RMB 570.2 billion of goods — up 2.7%, and 3.1% of China's total goods imports (GACC cross-border e-commerce statistics). Every one of those goods was a consumer product: beauty, fragrance and personal care 27.2%, food and fresh produce 23.9%.

Read that twice, because it inverts a very common assumption. A beauty brand that sizes its China opportunity from Tmall Global sell-through is looking at roughly one-thirtieth of the imports in its category. The rest arrives as general trade, with a Chinese label, a registration file and a distributor's margin already inside the shelf price. Cross-border is the beachhead; it is not the pool.

Import value is not retail value, and the gap is the margin stack

Landed cost is the customs value plus duty, import VAT and any consumption tax, plus freight and clearance; retail price is what the shelf asks once distributor, platform and store margins are added on top. For imported consumer goods in China the multiple between the two is routinely two to three times, and it is not a hidden number — it is the sum of published rates: MFN duty, 13% VAT, 15% consumption tax on high-end cosmetics, plus channel take. Dividing RMB 18.48 trillion of imports by RMB 50.12 trillion of retail sales is a category error, because the first is measured at the port and the second at the till (landed cost breakdown).

The workable rule is to size the import pool in customs value, size the retail pool in retail value, and express penetration as a ratio only after both are on the same basis.

Triangulate with three datasets, one question each

Triangulation is the practice of answering one sizing question with at least two independent official datasets and reconciling the difference rather than averaging it. Chinese public data answers three different questions well, and each is bad at the others.

QuestionDatasetPublished by
Is my category imported, from where, and how fast is it growing?Eight-digit import value and quantity by originGACC monthly statistics
How much do Chinese consumers spend on the category overall?Retail sales by category, above designated size and totalNational Bureau of Statistics
Which channel carries it, and how much is online?Online retail value and share of retail salesNBS monthly release

NBS recorded RMB 50.12 trillion of retail sales in 2025 — 501,202 亿元 in the official release, up 3.7% — with physical goods sold online at 26.1% of the total (NBS retail release, 19 January 2026). Set that against the import pool and you get a defensible import-penetration rate per category, which is the number a board actually asks for.

A four-week sequence you can run before paying for research

A pre-research sizing sprint is a fixed sequence of public-data pulls that produces a go/no-go range before any agency is commissioned. Run it in this order and the research brief writes itself.

  • Week 1 — pull the record. Three years of eight-digit import value and quantity for your candidate codes, by origin country, in RMB.
  • Week 2 — cross it with consumption. Match against NBS category growth and the online share. A category where imports outgrow total retail is one where imported supply is taking share: that is the opening.
  • Week 3 — confirm the code. Test the ten-digit classification with a broker, because that is the line your shipments will be filed under and the line your competitors' are.
  • Week 4 — price the pool. Put a landed-cost model behind the figure so the output is a gross-margin pool, not a headline.

If the pool survives those four weeks, a research budget is well spent. If it does not, you have just saved it.

FAQ

Where can I get official China import data by product? The General Administration of Customs publishes it free, monthly, at eight-digit code by origin country and destination province. The headline release lands between the 7th and 14th of the following month, followed by the full monthly report. Summary tables need no registration; the queryable database asks for a free account. Third-party resellers mostly repackage the same numbers with a larger margin and a smaller denominator.

Does China publish the names of importers? No. GACC publishes aggregates by commodity code, origin, province, trade mode and enterprise type, and does not release the company on a declaration. Datasets sold as "China customs data with importer contacts" are therefore not customs data — usually bill-of-lading scrapes from other jurisdictions or assembled directories. Size the pool with customs data; identify buyers with trade fairs, associations and channel checks.

Why is my category's import value flat while volumes grow? Because value is assessed CIF value and moves with world prices. In 2025 total import value grew only 0.5% in RMB while machinery and electronics imports reached RMB 7.41 trillion, up 5.7%; commodity price falls dragged the headline. Pull the value and quantity series together — a flat value line in a falling-price year is usually growth.

Is cross-border e-commerce a good proxy for China market size? No. In 2025 cross-border retail imports were RMB 570.2 billion, 3.1% of the RMB 18.48 trillion import bill, even though all of it was consumer goods. Around 97% of imported consumer goods reach China through general trade, with Chinese labelling, registration and distributor margin already inside the price. Treat cross-border as a beachhead and size the pool separately.

How many tariff lines does China have, and does it matter? The 2026 tariff contains 8,972 eight-digit lines, 935 of them with a temporary rate below MFN. Declarations are filed at ten digits, where GACC adds national sub-headings for goods needing a different rate, licence or statistical identity. Six digits is comparable but too broad; eight is the practical sizing unit; ten is what your broker files.
