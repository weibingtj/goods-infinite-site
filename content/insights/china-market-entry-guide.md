## Why entering China is different

China is not one market you "export into" — it is a distinct retail, logistics and regulatory environment with its own platforms (Tmall, JD, Douyin, RedNote), its own payment rails (WeChat Pay, Alipay) and its own customs codes. The single biggest mistake foreign brands make is treating it like the EU or US and shipping a container.

This guide lays out the realistic paths, a decision framework, and the documents you will actually need — so you can pick the route that fits your risk profile.

## The four paths into China

1. **Cross-border e-commerce (1210 bonded).** Stock into a China bonded zone, clear per order. Lowest upfront tax and entity risk; ideal for a first test.
2. **General trade (0110) + domestic store.** Import the full shipment, pay duty/VAT up front, sell through a domestic Tmall/JD store. Needs a local entity.
3. **Import agent / Hong Kong entity (hybrid).** A Hong Kong trading company or licensed agent acts as importer of record; you keep control without a WFOE.
4. **Local distribution partner.** Hand off to a China distributor. Fastest to shelf, but you lose pricing control and customer data.

## Path 1 — Cross-border e-commerce (1210): fastest, lowest risk

This is where most overseas brands should start. Goods sit in a bonded zone in Tianjin, Shanghai, Ningbo, Guangzhou or Qingdao; you list on a cross-border platform; tax is paid only when a unit sells. Delivery is 1–3 days domestically.

You do **not** need a WFOE to begin. A Hong Kong trading entity or a licensed import agent can be the importer of record. See our guides on the [1210 model](what-is-1210-bonded-import.html) and [using a Hong Kong entity](hong-kong-entity-china-import.html).

## Path 2 — General trade + domestic store

When you want a **domestic** Tmall or JD store, or broad offline/wholesale distribution, you move to general trade (code 0110): full shipment cleared up front, full duty and VAT paid, product registered under a local licence holder. This usually means forming a WFOE or appointing a local distributor.

## Path 3 — Hong Kong entity / import agent (hybrid)

A Hong Kong-incorporated trading company (e.g. GOODSINFINITE TRADE LIMITED) imports under bond and serves as importer of record, while bonded fulfilment and last-mile are handled in mainland China. You get a near-local experience with far less entity overhead. This is the route we specialise in.

## Path 4 — Local distributor

Simplest commercially, but you cede control of price, brand story and first-party data. Best when you want presence without building any capability — and acceptable as a supplement, not a strategy.

## Documents you will actually need

- Product compliance evidence: CCC, NMPA filing, food/health licence as applicable (see our [compliance guide](china-import-compliance-ccc-nmpa.html)).
- Chinese-language labels and ingredient/manual translations.
- Commercial documents: contract, invoice, packing list, bill of lading.
- Importer of record details (your HK entity or agent).
- Platform onboarding materials for Tmall Global / JD Worldwide / Douyin.

## Common mistakes

- **Shipping a full container before verifying compliance** — categories like cosmetics, supplements and infant formula need licences first.
- **Assuming one model fits everything** — cross-border and general trade solve different problems.
- **Skipping the China marketing layer** — discovery happens on Douyin, RedNote and WeChat, not Google.

## FAQ

**What is the cheapest way to test the China market?** A 1210 cross-border test through a Hong Kong entity or import agent — you pay tax only on units that sell, and avoid WFOE setup cost.

**Do I really not need a WFOE?** For cross-border retail, no. A WFOE becomes necessary for a domestic store, general-trade distribution, or regulated categories requiring a local licence holder.

**How long does it take?** A compliant 1210 launch is typically 4–8 weeks; a full WFOE plus domestic store is 3–6 months plus licensing.
