How Long Does Customs Clearance Take in China? (2026 Timelines)

There is no single fixed number for China customs clearance time — it depends on your declaration mode, whether goods are inspected, and your AEO status. Here are the official benchmarks and the mechanisms that actually move the clock.

Published 2026-10-05 · Last updated 2026-10-09 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

Definition: what "customs clearance time in China" actually measures

"Customs clearance time" in China is not a single statutory number. It is the elapsed time between the moment your declaration is accepted by the General Administration of Customs (GACC) and the moment goods are released — and it is shaped by three variables the law lets vary: your declaration mode, whether your goods are selected for inspection, and your enterprise's credit status (AEO).

The legally fixed part is the declaration deadline (not the clearance speed): importers must declare within 14 days of the transport's entry declaration, or pay a demurrage fee (滞报金). The clearance speed itself is a function of how compliant and well-prepared your filing is.

The official baseline: clearance is fast for compliant goods

China has deliberately compressed clearance time through its Smart Customs (智慧海关) programme. At the State Council Information Office briefing on 13 January 2025, GACC stated that for low-risk, compliant goods, the process from declaration to release is handled fully online and is essentially "second-level" clearance (读秒通关) — i.e. near-instant once the system accepts the declaration.

秒级For low-risk, compliant goods under Smart Customs, GACC states release from declaration to release is essentially "second-level" (near-instant) when filed fully electronicallySource: State Council Information Office briefing, 13 January 2025 (China.org.cn)

This is the honest headline: if your paperwork is clean, your goods are not inspected, and your declaration is accepted automatically, clearance is the same day — often within minutes. The variability everyone worries about comes from the inspection and manual-review steps, not from a slow baseline.

The three variables that move the clock

1. Declaration mode — two-step declaration (两步申报)

China's two-step declaration is the single biggest structural lever on clearance time. Formalised in GACC Decree No. 277 (Measures for the Declaration and Management of Import and Export Goods) and refined by the 2025 optimisation pilot (piloted from 6 May 2025 at 9 regional customs authorities across 11 sites), it works like this:

  • Step 1 — summary declaration (概要申报): the importer files a reduced set of fields. If the goods are not subject to tax, licence, or inspection holds, they can be taken away from the port (提离) immediately after summary acceptance.
  • Step 2 — complete declaration (完整申报): the remaining fields are lodged within 14 days of the transport's entry declaration.
14 daysStatutory window to lodge the complete declaration after the transport's entry declaration under two-step declaration — miss it and a demurrage fee (滞报金) appliesSource: GACC Decree No. 277 (Measures for Declaration and Management of Import and Export Goods); gov.cn policy interpretation, April 2025

A related mechanism, advance declaration (提前申报), lets importers file before goods physically arrive — for imports, after departure and before arrival; for exports, within seven days before arrival at the customs supervision zone. Both move the clerical work ahead of the cargo so release is not blocked at the gate.

2. Inspection selection (查验) — the real time variable

When goods are picked for inspection, time becomes genuinely variable. Risk-based selection depends on the product category: food, cosmetics, medical devices, and electronics carry higher inspection probability than general merchandise. China is compressing this too — GACC's remote inspection (远程查检 / "千里眼") reform cuts single-inspection job time by about 90% versus on-site inspection, and vacuum-packaged high-tech goods get a dedicated non-intrusive inspection mode that cuts clearance time by about 80%.

Practical read: you cannot eliminate inspection risk, but you can shrink its impact by getting product compliance right before arrival — see our China customs clearance process guide.

3. AEO status — the credit-tier shortcut

An AEO (Authorised Economic Operator, 高级认证企业) is the highest credit tier in China's customs system, and it is the most reliable way to keep clearance fast and predictable.

<20%An AEO enterprise's average inspection rate is below 20% of the rate applied to ordinary enterprises, plus priority handling and a dedicated coordinatorSource: GACC AEO management measures (Decree No. 251 and implementing notices)

AEO benefits are codified in GACC's management measures and include: priority clearance, a substantially lower inspection rate, the ability to apply for bond waivers, reduced audit frequency, and — in emergencies — the ability to request clearance outside normal working hours with as little as 8 hours' notice. China currently has AEO mutual recognition with 52 countries and regions, so the status also speeds you through partner customs abroad.

| Scenario | Typical release | Why | |----------|----------------|-----| | Low-risk goods, clean e-declaration, no inspection | Same day, often minutes | Smart Customs auto-accepts; "second-level" release | | Two-step summary declaration, non-held goods | Goods leave port before full declaration | Summary acceptance triggers 提离 | | Goods selected for inspection | Variable; remote inspection cuts job time ~90% | Risk-based 查验 + modernised inspection | | Manual review (wrong HS / value / missing licence) | Hours to days | Declaration bounced for correction | | Regulated product, compliance not done upstream | Blocked until fixed | GACC/NMPA/CCC registration must clear first |

The lesson: the clock is mostly in your control. Inspection and manual review are the only real unknowns; everything else is preparation.

FAQ

How long does customs clearance take in China? There is no single statutory duration. For low-risk, fully-compliant goods under Smart Customs, GACC states release from declaration to release is essentially "second-level" (near-instant) when filed electronically. Declarations not selected for inspection typically clear the day they are accepted; time only becomes variable when goods are inspected or the declaration needs manual review.

What is two-step declaration and does it speed up clearance? Two-step declaration (两步申报) lets you lodge a summary declaration first; compliant goods can be taken away from the port before the full declaration is finished. The complete declaration must be lodged within 14 days of the transport's entry declaration, or a late-declaration fee applies. It is formalised in GACC Decree No. 277 and refined by the 2025 optimisation pilot.

Do AEO-certified companies clear customs faster? Yes. AEO (高级认证企业) is China Customs' top credit tier: its average inspection rate is below 20% of ordinary enterprises, with priority handling, a dedicated coordinator, and mutual recognition across 52 countries and regions.

What makes clearance slow? Inspection selection (查验), incorrect or incomplete declarations (wrong HS code, value mismatch, missing licences), and upstream product-compliance gaps (GACC/NMPA/CCC not done before arrival). Fixing these at origin is what saves time.

How long do I have to declare imported goods? Within 14 days of the transport's entry declaration; under two-step declaration this window applies to the complete declaration after an initial summary. Late declaration incurs a daily demurrage fee from the 15th day.
