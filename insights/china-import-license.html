Do You Need a China Import License Before Selling? A Foreign Brand Checklist

Most overseas consumer brands do not need a generic China import licence — compliance runs through product registration instead. This checklist separates the licence myth from the real approvals (GACC/NMPA/SAMR) and explains the 1210 cross-border shortcut.

Published 2026-08-27 · Last updated 2026-09-09 · By Bing Wei, Operations Director

The licence question that confuses every first-time exporter

Search "do I need a China import license" and you will find a fog of half-answers. The confusion is understandable: China does run an import-licence system, but it rarely applies to the branded consumer products most overseas businesses want to sell. This checklist clears the fog and tells you what you actually need — and what you do not.

China's import licence system is a narrow control, not a blanket rule

China's import licence system is the MOFCOM-administered control that restricts entry of a narrow list of goods — it is not a requirement attached to every shipment crossing the border. The annual MOFCOM catalogue of licence-administered goods covers items such as certain grains, sugar, cotton, fertilizers, timber, and selected industrial inputs — not the化妆品, supplements, snacks, or apparel that make up most cross-border retail (MOFCOM).

1210the Customs bonded-import code that lets overseas brands sell into China without a local entity or per-shipment licenceSource: General Administration of Customs cross-border e-commerce supervision rules

If your product is a normal branded consumer good, the honest answer to "do I need an import licence?" is almost always no. The real question is whether your category needs a product approval — a different mechanism entirely.

Product registration is the gate most brands actually face

Product registration is the category-specific approval — lodged with GACC, NMPA, or SAMR — that most regulated brands must clear instead of a generic import licence. The agency depends on what you sell:

  • Food → GACC overseas-manufacturer registration (Decree 248) for the 18 high-risk categories; lighter information filing for the rest.
  • Cosmetics → NMPA filing (general) or registration (special-use) under CSAR.
  • Medical devices / health food → NMPA or SAMR approvals as applicable.

These approvals are the substantive compliance work. Treat "licence" and "registration" as different words for different gates, and you will plan correctly.

The 1210 cross-border model removes the per-shipment burden

The 1210 bonded model is a Customs regime where goods are pre-stocked in a China free-trade zone and cleared per order, deferring tax until a unit actually sells. For most consumer goods sold this way, the per-shipment import-licence formalities that general trade would require are simplified, because the goods move under a dedicated cross-border e-commerce framework rather than the full general-trade (0110) regime (GACC).

That is why a foreign brand can run a Tmall Global or JD Worldwide store as an overseas entity: the platform and the importer of record absorb the clearance mechanics, and you avoid both a mainland company and the heavier general-trade licence layer.

An import record-filed entity is the local anchor you still need

An import record-filed entity (进口商备案) is the local party qualified to lodge declarations and hold food-safety and customs accountability — even when no licence is required. Under 1210, this role can be filled by a Hong Kong or mainland importer of record such as GOODSINFINITE TRADE LIMITED, so you do not need a WFOE on day one. The entity:

  • Holds the import record-filing with GACC
  • Matches each shipment to valid product approvals
  • Manages declaration, tax, and traceability
  • Acts as the accountable party for compliance

A practical pre-launch checklist

  • Step 1 — Classify your product. Is it on the MOFCOM licence catalogue? For most branded consumer goods, no.
  • Step 2 — Identify the approval agency. Food → GACC; cosmetics → NMPA; health/special foods → SAMR/NMPA.
  • Step 3 — Decide the channel. 1210 cross-border for light compliance and no local entity; general trade for a domestic store.
  • Step 4 — Appoint an importer of record. A qualified China entity or a Hong Kong importer such as GOODSINFINITE.
  • Step 5 — Pre-clear labels and claims. Review against Chinese rules before production to avoid border rejection.

Common mistakes brands make

  • Assuming "licence" and "registration" are the same. They are not; chasing a licence you do not need wastes weeks.
  • Believing 1210 means zero compliance. You still need product registration where the category demands it.
  • Shipping before the importer is filed. Without a record-filed entity, nothing clears — licence or not.

FAQ

Do most foreign brands need a China import licence? Usually not. China's import-licence system (administered by MOFCOM) covers a narrow catalogue of goods such as certain agricultural commodities, energy and chemicals — not ordinary branded consumer goods.

What replaces the import licence for regulated products? Category-specific approvals: GACC registration for imported food manufacturers, NMPA filing/registration for cosmetics and medical devices, and SAMR oversight for health food and special-dietary foods.

Does the 1210 cross-border model remove the licence requirement? For most consumer goods sold via 1210 bonded import, yes — per-shipment licence formalities are simplified because the goods enter under a dedicated CBEC regime. Product registration is still required where the category demands it.

Who holds the import qualification? A China import record-filed entity must lodge the declaration and hold accountability. GOODSINFINITE TRADE LIMITED can act as that importer of record, so a brand can sell into China without a mainland company.
