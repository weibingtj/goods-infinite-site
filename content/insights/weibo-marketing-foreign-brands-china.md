## Weibo is not "China's Twitter" — it is the country's public-opinion layer

Weibo marketing is the practice of using China's dominant public microblogging platform — the arena where breaking news, celebrity events and public argument break — to build brand visibility, reputation and search presence rather than direct-response sales. The Twitter analogy misleads: X has no hot search list that sets the national agenda for six hours, and Weibo sells no verification badge as a marketing product. What it sells is a position inside a conversation millions of people are already having.

The platform's own numbers frame the opportunity honestly. In June 2026 Weibo reported 561 million monthly active users and 254 million daily active users ([Weibo Q2 2026, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1595761/000110465926098504/tm2623262d1_ex99-1.htm)); a year earlier those were 588 million and 261 million ([Weibo Q2 2025, HKEX](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0814/2025081400642_c.pdf)). Roughly 27 million monthly actives disappeared while daily actives barely moved — a stable habit-driven core on a thinning casual audience. You do not build a Weibo presence for reach; you build it to be present when your category is discussed.

## The three jobs Weibo does better than any other Chinese platform

A China hot search (热搜) is a live ranking of the topics generating the fastest-rising discussion volume on Weibo, used by brands, media and the public as the default index of what the country is paying attention to. It behaves like a national timer: a topic enters the list, holds attention for a few hours, and is gone.

A Weibo Super Topic (超话) is a topic-scoped community with its own feed and moderators, used by brands and fan communities to concentrate ongoing discussion instead of scattering it across the public timeline. It is the part of Weibo that is quietly growing: Q2 2026 results show Super Topic daily active users and discussing users both up double digits year on year, with video watch time also up double digits against flat overall monthly actives.

The third job is search. Weibo's stated 2026 priorities are hot events, social and search, with its AI search product handling multi-turn questions and being embedded into post, video and hot-topic browsing. Weibo is therefore no longer only a feed you buy into — it is a corpus that Chinese search engines and AI assistants read from, including the criticism.

## What a foreign brand can actually buy on Weibo

Weibo advertising is sold as a menu of placements — two premium formats, hot search and splash, plus four working formats: feed advertising, internal search, content heating and creator contracts — used by brands for launch reach, awareness and brand-term defence. The split matters because only the premium two reach people who do not already follow you.

Weibo publishes no rate card, so every price you are quoted is a broker number. Composite agency quotes for 2026 put feed advertising and Weibo internal search at roughly RMB 0.5–3 per click, enterprise verification at RMB 600 plus an annual review fee, and reserved hot-search placements in the RMB 100,000-plus per day range — several times higher in a launch week. Treat all of it as directional, not published.

What the platform's money says about demand is more useful than the price list. Advertising and marketing revenue was US$381.0 million in Q2 2026, down 1% year on year after growing 2% a year earlier; handset and online-game demand fell while internet services and automobile grew, and Alibaba's Weibo ad spend rose 10% to US$39.2 million. Content marketing and celebrity marketing are where Weibo says its difference lies.

## Why most overseas brands cannot open a Weibo ad account themselves

A Weibo blue-V enterprise account (企业蓝V) is the verified business identity that unlocks advertising, analytics and the creator marketplace, and it is issued only against a mainland Chinese business licence. The barrier is not the RMB 600 fee or the five-day review but the document set behind it: a business licence with matching scope, the legal representative's identity, a corporate bank account, a landing page with an ICP filing, and any sector licence the product requires.

A foreign brand therefore has two routes, and they are not equivalent. It can establish a mainland entity and operate its own account, buying control of the data and the audience relationship at the cost of setup time; or it can work through an authorised agency or Tmall partner that contracts as advertiser under its own licence. The second is faster, but the account, the data and the followers belong to the agency — while the content still carries your name for labelling and liability.

## The rules that decide what you can say, and what you can never buy

Weibo advertising compliance is the set of labelling, endorsement and ranking rules that govern what a brand may publish or buy on the platform, enforced by SAMR, the CAC and the platform itself. Three rules matter more than the rest.

Under SAMR Order No. 72, the Measures for the Administration of Internet Advertising in force since 1 May 2023, content that shares knowledge, experience or product testing and carries a shopping link must be conspicuously marked 广告 — advertising ([SAMR, 2023](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10506/202306/content_6885261.html)). That covers creator posts your agency arranged, not only paid placements; the penalty runs through Article 25 to Article 59 of the Advertising Law — correction plus a fine of up to RMB 100,000 on the publisher.

Endorsement is the second rule. The Advertising Law requires that an endorser has actually used the product and bars endorsers under ten years old. Weibo is where celebrity-led campaigns work best, so an ambassador campaign needs a documented usage basis, not just a signed contract.

The third rule is ranking. The CAC's Provisions on the Ecological Governance of Network Information Content, in force since 1 March 2020, ban traffic fraud, hijacking and account manipulation ([CAC Order No. 5, 2020](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2020/content_5492511.htm)), and name microblog hot lists as a key area where platforms must not present scandal-mongering or mismatched headlines. In November 2024 four departments ordered platforms to publish the algorithms behind their hot search rankings and crack down on paid comment armies ([CAC, 2024](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2024-11/24/c_1734143936205514.htm)); in July 2025 the CAC's Qinglang campaign named paid list-pushing — 刷榜打榜买榜 — as a target ([CAC, 2025](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-07/29/c_1755503642582366.htm)). Reserved, labelled positions are legitimate; a guaranteed organic hot search is a different product.

## When Weibo should be your lead China channel, and when it should not

A lead China channel is the one platform a brand commits its first 6 to 12 months of content and budget to, and Weibo is the right lead only when Chinese demand for the category is event-driven rather than search-driven. That fits sports and outdoor, automobiles, consumer electronics, entertainment-adjacent food and drink, and any brand whose entry story rests on an ambassador, a sponsorship or a public launch.

Search-led categories fit elsewhere. Beauty and skincare, home, and mother-and-baby brands are usually better served leading on Xiaohongshu, where decisions are made by comparison and search, with Douyin leading where the transaction itself is the trend. Whichever route you take, one part of Weibo is not optional: Chinese search engines and AI assistants read it, and negative posts there have a long half-life in results for your brand name.

## A 90-day Weibo sequence for a brand with no China team

A 90-day Weibo sequence is a staged entry plan: audit in month one, choose the operating route in month two, and run one concentrated campaign in month three rather than a drip. Start by auditing mention volume under your brand name, the negative-to-positive ratio, and whether resellers or grey-market accounts already occupy your brand term. Most Western brands find their Chinese name is in someone else's hands.

Month two is structural — choose between a mainland entity operating its own account and an agency contracting as advertiser, and confirm whether you need a verified brand account before planning spend. Month three is one well-built moment: publish the launch narrative, seed eight to fifteen mid-tier creators through the creator marketplace so the content is contracted and labelled correctly, then pair one reserved placement with search ads defending your brand term.

## How to measure Weibo without fooling yourself

Weibo measurement is the practice of judging a campaign on demand-side evidence — brand-term search volume, share of voice and sentiment ratio — rather than follower counts or impressions. Track brand-term search volume on Weibo and Baidu, exposure and dwell time on your topic page, share of voice against named competitors, the negative-to-positive ratio, and whether your store sees an organic-session lift in the 72 hours after a hot moment.

Follower counts are a poor proxy in China, where purchased audiences are cheap and a single event can move numbers by six figures without moving a single purchase. Because Chinese platforms expose less attribution than Western ad networks, pair platform metrics with a demand check you control: brand-term search lift, branded traffic, and the volume of unprompted mentions.

## FAQ

**Should a foreign brand be on Weibo?** Yes, but rarely as a lead channel. Weibo is where Chinese public opinion about your category forms, and posts there are indexed by Baidu and quoted by Chinese AI search products, so monitoring is mandatory whether or not you advertise. Buy media there when your Chinese demand is event-driven — launches, ambassadors, sponsorships, trade shows — and lead with Xiaohongshu or Douyin when demand is search-led or transaction-led.

**Can a foreign company open a Weibo advertising account without a Chinese entity?** Not directly. Advertising, analytics and the creator marketplace all sit behind an enterprise blue-V account, which is issued against a mainland business licence with a legal representative, a corporate bank account and a landing page with an ICP filing. Brands without a Chinese entity work through an authorised agency or Tmall partner that contracts under its own licence, while the brand remains responsible for what the content says.

**How much does Weibo advertising cost?** Weibo publishes no rate card, so all figures are broker quotes. Composite agency quotes for 2026 put feed advertising at roughly RMB 0.5–3 per click, Weibo internal search at RMB 0.5–3 per click, enterprise verification at RMB 600 with an annual review fee, and reserved hot-search placements in the RMB 100,000-plus per day range, quoted far higher during major launches. Treat them as directional: the same placement is quoted at very different levels in a launch week.

**Can you buy a place on the Weibo hot search list?** Reserved advertising positions inside the hot search list can be bought in advance, and they are labelled as advertising. Manipulating the ranking itself is not marketing — the CAC's content governance rules ban traffic fraud, fake accounts and account manipulation, the 2024 four-department algorithm action ordered platforms to publish their ranking logic and crack down on paid comment armies, and the 2025 Qinglang campaign names paid list-pushing (刷榜打榜买榜) as a target. Any agency promising a guaranteed organic hot search result is selling you a regulated act.

**Is Weibo still worth it in 2026, given falling active users?** The decline is real — monthly actives fell from 588 million in June 2025 to 561 million in June 2026, while daily actives barely moved, from 261 million to 254 million. What that describes is a stable habit-driven core and a thinner casual audience. For a brand, Weibo has shifted from a reach channel to a reputation and heat channel, and its value should be judged on share of voice and search lift rather than impressions.
