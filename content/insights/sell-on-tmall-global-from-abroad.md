## Why Tmall Global is the default on-ramp for brands selling into China from abroad

For an overseas brand, the fastest way to reach Chinese consumers used to mean forming a Chinese company, renting office space, and building a local team — a 6–12 month commitment before the first sale. Tmall Global inverts that sequence: it is Alibaba's cross-border storefront where a foreign or Hong Kong entity sells directly to mainland shoppers, with goods cleared per order and delivered from a bonded zone. This guide walks through exactly how to sell on Tmall Global from abroad, what you do and do not need, and where a Tmall Partner fits. Our [cross-border e-commerce China guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/cross-border-ecommerce-china.html) covers the 1210 model that makes this possible.

## Tmall Global is Alibaba's cross-border retail platform that lets an overseas or Hong Kong entity sell directly to Chinese consumers without a mainland business licence

Tmall Global (天猫国际) is the cross-border arm of Tmall where foreign brands operate flagship stores aimed at Chinese buyers, with goods imported under cross-border e-commerce rules rather than general trade. The defining advantage for an overseas seller is the merchant of record: you can register as an overseas company — for example a Hong Kong trading entity such as GOODSINFINITE TRADE LIMITED — and avoid forming a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in mainland China. China's cross-border e-commerce trade reached about RMB 2.38 trillion in 2023 according to the General Administration of Customs, and Tmall Global is one of the primary storefronts capturing that flow ([GACC](https://www.customs.gov.cn)).

## Cross-border vs. domestic Tmall: what "from abroad" actually means

The phrase "sell on Tmall Global from abroad" only makes sense once you separate the two Tmall routes:

- **Tmall Global (cross-border).** You are an overseas/HK entity. Goods sit in a bonded zone (1210) or ship by direct mail, cleared per order, with cross-border tax applied at checkout. No mainland licence required for the store itself.
- **Domestic Tmall / Tmall Flagship (general trade).** You need a China-incorporated entity, a Chinese business licence, and full product registration (NMPA, GACC, CCC, etc.) before listing. This is the "local company" route.

For a first entry, cross-border is lower risk: you prove demand on Tmall Global, then upgrade to a domestic store only once the data justifies the heavier regime. Our [China market entry guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/guide-china-market-entry.html) lays out this staged sequence.

## The entity and documents you need to register from abroad

To open a Tmall Global store as a foreign brand you typically prepare:

- **Overseas or Hong Kong business licence** proving the company is registered outside mainland China.
- **Brand ownership or authorisation** — trademark certificate or a licence from the rights holder.
- **Importer of record / fulfillment arrangement** — a bonded (1210) or direct-mail plan, often run through a China import agent or your HK trading company.
- **Category approvals where required** — special cosmetics need NMPA registration, imported food needs GACC manufacturer registration, under China's pre-market rules ([NMPA](https://www.nmpa.gov.cn), [GACC](https://www.customs.gov.cn)).

Crucially, you do not need a mainland WFOE to launch cross-border, which is why this route is the standard answer to "how do I sell on Tmall Global from abroad."

## The Tmall Partner (TP) model: running the store without a local team

A Tmall Partner (TP, 代运营) is an Alibaba-accredited agency that sets up and operates your flagship store end to end. For a foreign brand with no local staff, the TP is often the missing operational layer:

- Store design, listing, and pricing in line with Chinese shopping norms.
- Coordination with bonded warehousing and per-order customs clearance (1210).
- Promotion planning around Tmall's mega-sales (6.18, Double 11) and everyday traffic.
- Customer service and returns in Mandarin.

Most overseas brands run Tmall Global entirely through a TP, which removes the need to hire a China e-commerce team on day one. ## Fulfillment: how orders actually reach the buyer

Once a shopper orders, the goods are released from the bonded zone (if 1210) or dispatched via direct mail, cleared per order, and delivered by a domestic carrier (Cainiao, SF, JD Logistics) in 1–3 days from a well-positioned zone. ## Compliance and tax you still cannot skip

Selling from abroad relaxes the entity requirement but not the rules. You remain subject to cross-border tax per order, product-specific registration for regulated categories, and China's strict advertising and labelling standards once you market on-platform. ## FAQ

**Can a foreign brand sell on Tmall Global without a China company?** Yes. Tmall Global is Alibaba's cross-border channel and accepts an overseas or Hong Kong entity as the merchant of record. A China importer of record (or your Hong Kong trading company) handles customs and bonded fulfillment, so no mainland WFOE is required for the store.

**What is the difference between Tmall Global and a domestic Tmall store?** Tmall Global is cross-border: goods clear per order (often under 1210 bonded or direct mail) without a mainland business licence. A domestic Tmall store requires a China-incorporated entity, Chinese business licence, and full product registration before sale.

**What does a Tmall Partner (TP) do for a foreign brand?** A TP is an accredited agency that sets up and operates your flagship store — design, listing, pricing, bonded/fulfillment coordination, promotion, and customer service — letting many foreign brands run Tmall Global with no local operations team.

**How long does it take to open a Tmall Global store from abroad?** With documents ready, onboarding typically runs a few weeks to two months depending on category and whether product registration is required. Cross-border categories generally move faster than general-trade imports.

**Do I still need product registration to sell on Tmall Global?** It depends on the channel. Cross-border (1210) retail relaxes many pre-market rules for general cosmetics and supplements, while special cosmetics, food, and infant formula still need NMPA or GACC approvals.
