China Cross-Border E-Commerce Positive List: The 1,476 Tax Codes That Decide What You Can Sell

The Cross-Border E-Commerce Retail Import Commodity List — the 'positive list' — is the single gate that decides whether your product can enter China through 1210 bonded or 9610 direct-purchase channels. This guide sets out the legal basis (Notice 486 of 2018), the four versions of the list from 1,240 to 1,476 tax codes, the tax mechanics that ride on it (5,000/26,000 yuan limits, 0% duty, VAT at 70%), and the remark-level restrictions — 98 items bonded-only, quantity caps, endangered-species proof — that catch brands who checked only the tariff code.

Published 2026-11-16 · Last updated 2026-11-18 · By Bing Wei, China Market Entry & Cross-Border Commerce Specialist

Definition: what the "positive list" actually is

The Cross-Border E-Commerce Retail Import Commodity List (跨境电子商务零售进口商品清单) — universally shortened to the "positive list" (正面清单) — is the closed catalogue of goods that may lawfully enter China through the cross-border e-commerce retail import channels: 1210 bonded warehouse import (网购保税进口) and 9610 direct purchase (直购进口). It works exactly in the direction its nickname suggests: Notice 486 of 2018, the six-department framework notice that defines the modern retail-import regime, conditions the channel on goods that are (1) inside the list, (2) for personal use only, and (3) consistent with the retail import tax policy. Anything outside the list cannot be declared through either channel — no matter how small the order, how genuine the consumer, or how loudly the platform markets it.

This matters to a foreign brand more than any marketing decision, because channel fit is decided before the first container moves. A product inside the list can ride the bonded model — stock in a special customs supervision zone, pay duty per unit sold, deliver in one to three days. A product outside the list needs a completely different legal structure: a general-trade importer of record, full duty and import VAT, and whatever first-import licences, registration or filing the product category requires. The four versions, and what each one changed

The list was created in 2016 together with the retail import tax policy and has been adjusted by joint announcement four times. The history is not trivia — brands still quote 2019-version guidance in 2026, and the deltas are exactly where clearance surprises live.

The 2018 announcement and the 2019 announcement each expressly repeal the prior versions, so the current legal state is the 2019 edition as adjusted by the 2022 announcement — 1,476 lines. No newer joint adjustment to the list itself has been published; state media reporting into 2026 still describes the list at "over 1,470" lines. Treat adjustments as dynamic, though: the list moves with the tariff schedule, and the version in force on the day of declaration is the one that counts.

What the list controls — and what it does not

Three boundaries matter.

The list controls the channel, not admissibility in general. A listed good enters through 1210/9610 as a personal-use import, and under Art. 3 of Notice 486 such goods are supervised as personal articles and are exempt from first-import licence, registration or filing requirements — with carve-outs for goods from epidemic areas and emergency risk measures. That exemption is why the bonded channel is so much faster to launch for consumer goods. It does not, however, abolish the surrounding framework: quality-safety risk monitoring continues, the 30/45-day return clocks apply, and platform and domestic agent obligations under Notice 486 are unaffected.

The list controls goods, not business models. Retail-import goods are consumer personal-use items; resale into the domestic market is prohibited, and bonded goods may not be handed over offline outside the special zone ("bonded + offline pickup" outside the zone is disallowed in principle). If your real intent is wholesale distribution or offline retail, the product being on the list does not help you — you need a general-trade structure and an importer of record.

The list rides on the transaction data. Notice 486 conditions the regime on the "three documents" — order, payment and logistics information transmitted to customs and matched automatically. The list decides what can enter; the data integrity decides whether it clears.

The tax mechanics that ride on the list

Being on the list is the entry ticket to a separate tax regime, created by the 2016 retail import tax notice (effective 8 April 2016) and upgraded by Notice 2018 No. 49:

  • Limits: 5,000 yuan per single transaction; 26,000 yuan per person per year (raised from 2,000/20,000).
  • Rates within limits: import duty temporarily set at 0%; import VAT and consumption tax charged at 70% of the statutory amount, with no exemption threshold.
  • Taxable value: the actual transaction price, including retail price, freight and insurance.
  • Payer and withholding: the individual consumer is the taxpayer; the e-commerce enterprise, platform or logistics enterprise may act as the withholding agent and carries the related payment-back liability.
  • Over-limit single item: one indivisible item priced above 5,000 but below 26,000 yuan may still come through the channel at full goods rates and counts toward the annual total.
  • Annual breach: once the personal annual total is exceeded, the transaction is handled as general trade.
¥5,000 · ¥26,000 · 0% · 70%The four numbers that define the bonded channel's economics: single-transaction and annual per-person limits, the within-limits duty rate, and the fraction of statutory VAT/consumption tax actually charged — with the full goods-rate regime applying to a single indivisible item above the single limit but below the annual oneSource: MOF/GACC/STA Notice 财关税〔2016〕18号, as amended by Notice 财关税〔2018〕49号

For most consumer goods the practical all-in rate lands near 9.1% of the transaction price (13% VAT × 70%), which is the number to put in your landed-cost model — and a substantially different one from general trade, where duty plus full VAT apply from the first carton. The remarks: where compliant-looking products fail

The single most expensive mistake in this regime is checking only the tariff code. Every line in the list carries a remark (备注), and remarks carry real legal content. The 2022 adjustment revised 206 remarks, and the resulting constraints fall into three families:

  • Channel restrictions. 98 line items are restricted to bonded warehouse import only — they cannot move through 9610 direct purchase at all. Frozen aquatic products, bird's nest, royal jelly and other sensitive foods are the classic examples; the policy logic is that bonded storage allows inspection and tracing that direct mail does not.
  • Certificate conditions. Lines that may involve endangered wild fauna and flora carry a condition: goods importable only if the importer can provide the non-endangered-species certificate from the濒危物种进出口管理办公室. A supplement or cosmetic containing such ingredients fails here even though the tax code sits comfortably on the list.
  • Quantity caps. Sugar-type product codes carry a per-person annual cap — 2 kilograms aggregated across the designated codes — applied at the individual quota level, on top of the money limits.

Add the ordinary risk that tariff schedules are renumbered and remarks re-cut between editions, and the operational rule writes itself: verify the eight-digit code against the list version in force at declaration time, then read the remark, every time. Stock planning for the bonded channel should treat remark changes like regulatory events, not footnote material — see our cross-border stock planning guide.

If your product is not on the list

Out-of-list status closes the bonded channel but not the market. The remaining routes, in ascending order of commitment:

  1. General trade (0110) through a licensed importer of record: full duty and import VAT, product-specific approvals (GACC food manufacturer registration, NMPA filing/registration, CCC as applicable), Chinese-language labels — but unlimited channels: domestic e-commerce, offline retail, distribution. Our compliance overview covers the approval stack.
  2. Domestic store model built on a local entity or partner — Tmall domestic / Douyin operations with a mainland licence holder.
  3. A structural re-think of the SKU itself. Because the list moves at the tariff-line level, sometimes a formulation, pack size or product family sits on a different line that is inside the list. That is a compliance-engineering question, not a loophole to exploit — the declared code must reflect the actual good.

If you are unsure which side of the line your product sits on, resolve it before shipping, not at the port. See importing into China without a local company for how the general-trade path can be run without forming a WFOE, and the market-entry guide for the overall decision framework.

What we deliberately do not assert

  • We do not provide a per-product verdict for any specific tariff code. Applicability depends on the code plus the remark in force at declaration, and both move with the tariff schedule.
  • We do not quote any pending change to the transaction limits. Articles circulating in early 2026 claimed the limits were rising to 8,000/40,000 yuan; no official amendment to that effect was verifiable at publication, and the 5,000/26,000 figures in Notices 2016 No. 18 and 2018 No. 49 remain the operative policy. If a new joint notice lands, this page will be revised.
  • We do not assert that being on the list eliminates every other requirement. Notice 486 exempts first-import approvals for the retail-import channel, but category-specific quality-safety monitoring, platform obligations and the remarks regime all continue to apply.
  • We do not describe the personal postal articles channel as a sales route. It is a personal-use regime with its own (higher, flat) import tax and no brand-operable compliance structure.

FAQ

What is the China cross-border e-commerce positive list? The Cross-Border E-Commerce Retail Import Commodity List — the closed catalogue of goods that may enter China through 1210 bonded and 9610 direct-purchase retail import. It was created with the 2016 retail import tax policy and adjusted by joint MOF-led announcements in 2018, 2019 and 2022. Goods outside the list cannot use the channel.

How many items are on the current list? 1,476 tariff lines: 1,240 in the two 2016 batches, 1,321 in the 2018 edition, 1,413 in the 2019 edition, and 1,476 after the 2022 adjustment added 29 items and deleted one.

Can I still sell a product that is not on the list? Not through cross-border retail import. The lawful routes are general trade through an importer of record with full approvals, or a domestic-store model on a local entity or distributor. Personal postal articles are a separate personal-use regime, not a brand sales channel.

What tax treatment applies to positive-list goods? Within the 5,000-yuan single and 26,000-yuan annual limits: 0% duty and VAT/consumption tax at 70% of the statutory amount. A single indivisible item between the limits enters at full goods rates; beyond the annual total, general trade applies. The consumer is the taxpayer; platform, logistics or declaration enterprises may withhold.

Do the same rules apply to 1210 bonded and 9610 direct purchase? Yes — one list governs both. But 98 line items are restricted to bonded import only, and remarks can add certificate or quantity conditions that bind both channels.

Why can a listed product still fail at clearance? The remark attached to its tariff line. 206 remarks were adjusted in the 2022 change alone: endangered-species certificate conditions, 2 kg/person/year sugar caps, bonded-only restrictions. Code-remark mismatches, not the code itself, are the usual rejection cause.

What was added in the most recent adjustment? The 2022 adjustment added 29 items — ski equipment, household dishwashers, tomato juice, video game consoles and accessories, golf clubs, camera tripods, wigs — deleted swords and similar weapons, updated codes for tariff-schedule changes, and adjusted 206 remarks.
