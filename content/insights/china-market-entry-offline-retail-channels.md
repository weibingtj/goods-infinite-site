## What offline retail means in the China market

Offline retail in China is the set of physical store formats — supermarkets, membership warehouse clubs, convenience chains, department stores and pharmacies — through which packaged goods reach consumers without a cross-border e-commerce parcel, and it still takes the large majority of Chinese consumer spending. In 2025 total retail sales of consumer goods reached RMB 50,120.2 billion, up 3.7 percent year on year, of which online physical-goods sales were RMB 13,092.3 billion, or 26.1 percent of the total ([National Bureau of Statistics, December 2025 release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260120_1962354.html)).

The more useful signal sits inside that share. By retail format across above-designated-size operators, 2025 growth was uneven ([NBS, same release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260120_1962354.html)):

- Convenience stores: **+5.5 percent** — the fastest-growing large format.
- Supermarkets: **+4.3 percent** — still the volume engine for packaged food and daily goods.
- Specialty stores: **+2.6 percent**.
- Department stores: **+0.1 percent** — flat, and now a brand-building channel rather than a volume one.
- Brand-exclusive stores: **−0.6 percent** — the only format that shrank.

Scale is concentrated. Sam's Club reached 63 stores by the end of April 2026 and 67 by mid-May, on 2025 sales above RMB 140 billion with more than 10.7 million paying members, while Costco still operates seven mainland stores ([21st Century Business Herald, May 2026](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260527/herald/6d8b9e4cbd349a4cced153962d141c32.html)). Convenience is more fragmented but far larger in store count: the top 100 chains operated 208,000 stores at the end of 2025, up 5.6 percent, led by Meiyijia with 40,147 ([China Chain Store & Franchise Association, 2026 China Convenience Store Top 100](https://www.china.com.cn/aboutus/2026-08/04/content_118632936.htm)). The overlay channel is growing fastest — instant retail reached RMB 971.4 billion in 2025, up from RMB 781 billion in 2024, and is forecast to pass RMB 1 trillion in 2026 ([MOFCOM Research Institute, Instant Retail Development Report 2025](https://www.ce.cn/cysc/zljd/gd/202603/t20260319_2838062.shtml)) — so a shelf decision is now also an online availability decision.

## Why cross-border goods cannot simply be put on a shelf

A cross-border e-commerce retail import is a parcel sold to an individual consumer for personal use under China's cross-border retail framework — and under the joint notice that governs it, that parcel may not be resold. The notice, issued jointly by MOFCOM, customs, tax and market-regulation authorities, limits goods bought through the cross-border retail channel to personal use and prohibits resale ([GACC policy briefing confirming Shangfa Cai Fa No. 486 of 2018](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/2026-01/13/article_2026041015241860378.html)). Two consequences follow:

- **Bonded goods cannot be routed into stores.** In principle a bonded import may not operate an online-bonded-plus-offline-self-pickup model outside a customs supervision zone, so the warehouse-to-shelf shortcut does not exist.
- **The retail limits stay with the consumer.** RMB 5,000 per order and RMB 26,000 a year apply per individual.

Shelf presence therefore means general trade (customs code 0110): duty and 13 percent import VAT paid up front, a Chinese-language label meeting the mandatory standard for the category, category registration or filing where required, and a mainland supplier on the invoice. ## What a Chinese category buyer asks for before listing

A listing pack is the document set a retailer's category buyer reviews before approving a new SKU for the shelf, and it is where most first listings stall. Buyers across formats now ask for broadly the same file:

- **Supplier qualification:** a mainland-registered entity with import and export rights, a food business licence where food is involved, and the ability to issue a 13 percent VAT special invoice.
- **Lot documentation:** the customs declaration, inspection and quarantine certificate and tax-payment certificate — evidence that the goods entered under general trade, not cross-border retail.
- **Product compliance:** Chinese label artwork in the mandatory format (GB 7718 for prepackaged food, GB 18401 and GB 31701 for textiles), GB test reports, a registration or filing certificate for regulated categories, and enough remaining shelf life — two-thirds of labelled life at delivery is a common minimum.
- **Brand chain of title:** a China trademark registration certificate or an authorised-distribution letter.
- **Barcode and article master data:** a GS1-compliant retail barcode mapped into the retailer's article master — imported goods may use the overseas manufacturer's registered code, but goods produced in China under a foreign-registered code must be filed with the national barcode technical body.
- **Commercial and marketing support:** ex-warehouse supply price, front margin, annual rebate, delivery into the regional distribution centre, returns terms, and sampling or in-store demonstration support.

## The commercial terms — and the rules that cap them

A retail listing agreement in China is a contract between a retailer and a supplier covering supply price, margin, payment terms, promotional contributions and returns, and two instruments set the boundaries — one binding since 2006, one published in May 2026. The binding one is the Fair Trading Measures between Retailers and Suppliers (Order No. 17 of 2006, issued by MOFCOM with the NDRC, the Ministry of Public Security, the State Taxation Administration and SAIC) ([full text, MOFCOM](https://sczxs.mofcom.gov.cn/article/cbw/ddfg/ag/201208/20120808294156.shtml)):

- **Payment terms are capped at 60 days.** Article 14 requires the period to be agreed in the contract and states that it may not exceed 60 days from receipt of the goods; retailers may not delay payment because one product line missed its target or a renewal is pending.
- **Most listing fees have limits.** Charges for signing or renewing a contract, barcode fees, and festival or store-anniversary fees where no service is provided are prohibited; a promotional service fee must be agreed in advance, matched to a service actually performed, invoiced and taxed.
- **Your pricing and customer base are yours.** A retailer may not restrict the prices at which you sell to consumers or other operators, nor restrict your supply to other retailers.
- **Staff and returns are negotiable.** A retailer may not require you to send staff into its stores unless you agree and they work only on your goods, with the cost borne by the retailer where the arrangement is its requirement; you may also refuse returns caused by the retailer's own handling.

The May 2026 instrument is softer but increasingly cited in negotiations: the Domestic Trade Transaction Guidelines (trial), issued by 17 national industry associations and chambers under the guidance of MOFCOM, the NDRC, the Ministry of Justice and the Cyberspace Administration ([International Business Daily summary](https://sw.wuhan.gov.cn/xwdt/mtbd/202605/t20260513_2763928.shtml)). Across seven chapters and 38 articles it recommends payment within 30 days of delivery and no longer than 60, requires overdue interest referenced to the one-year loan prime rate, bars buyers from conditioning payment on matters outside their control such as "waiting for a third party to pay", forbids forcing smaller suppliers to accept commercial bills or accounts-receivable vouchers instead of cash, sets a seven-day acceptance window from delivery, and states that promotions must comply with Order No. 17 and may not be imposed on suppliers without agreement.

One fee changed by name in 2026. Under the revised Commodity Barcode Measures, in force since 1 May 2026, sellers may not charge suppliers entry fees, shelf-placement fees or information-processing fees in the name of product barcodes ([SAMR amendment to the Commodity Barcode Measures, gov.cn](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12786/202606/content_7071608.html)).

## How to sequence an offline launch

An offline launch plan is a reverse-scheduled sequence that starts from the retailer's buying calendar, and a first listing typically takes six to nine months:

1. **Months 0–2 — settle the route.** Confirm general-trade entry, appoint the importer of record, confirm the registration or filing path for the category, and file the China trademark if it is not already registered.
2. **Months 2–4 — build the listing pack.** Label artwork and printing, plus GB-standard testing. This is where timelines slip, because artwork revisions cascade.
3. **Months 4–6 — reach the buyer.** Clubs buy through regional offices, supermarkets and convenience chains through national or regional category buyers; China International Import Expo in Shanghai each November, SIAL China and FHC Shanghai are the most efficient rooms for those conversations.
4. **Months 6–9 — terms, delivery, rollout.** Negotiate against the rules above, deliver into the distribution centre, and launch two or three highest-velocity SKUs rather than the full range.

## FAQ

**Can I sell cross-border e-commerce goods in a physical store in China?** No. Under Shangfa Cai Fa No. 486 of 2018, goods bought through the cross-border retail channel are for personal use only and may not be resold, and bonded imports in principle may not operate an online-bonded-plus-offline-self-pickup model outside a customs supervision zone. A shelf presence requires general-trade import, duty paid, and any category registration completed.

**How quickly must a Chinese retailer pay a foreign supplier?** Article 14 of the Fair Trading Measures between Retailers and Suppliers caps the agreed payment period at 60 days from receipt of the goods and blocks delay on grounds such as a product line missing its target. The May 2026 Domestic Trade Transaction Guidelines recommend 30 days, cap terms at 60 days, and require overdue interest referenced to the one-year loan prime rate.

**Which listing fees can a Chinese retailer legally charge?** A promotional service fee agreed in advance and matched to a service actually provided — in-store promotion, advertising, posters — is lawful if it is contracted, invoiced and taxed. Fees for signing or renewing a contract, barcode fees, and festival or store-anniversary fees where no service is provided are prohibited, and the revised Commodity Barcode Measures ban entry, shelf-placement and information-processing fees charged in the name of barcodes.

**Can a Chinese retailer control my retail price or stop me supplying other retailers?** No. Article 7 of Order No. 17 prohibits a retailer from restricting the prices at which you sell to consumers or other operators and from restricting your supply to other retailers. A retailer also cannot require you to send staff into its stores unless you agree and they work only on your goods, with the cost borne by the retailer where the arrangement is its requirement.

**Do I need a Chinese entity to get listed offline?** You need a mainland-registered supplier that can hold the import and registration documents and issue a 13 percent VAT special invoice. That can be your own WFOE or a contracted importer or distributor acting as supplier of record, and the goods must enter under general trade with a compliant Chinese-language label.
