A quota certificate is issued to that Chinese end user and names the end user, the port of declaration and the trade mode. An overseas brand's route in is to sell to a quota-holding end user, to sell through a Chinese trading company, or to accept the out-of-quota rate.
  - q: What is the actual duty on out-of-quota cotton from 1 January 2026?
    a: Out-of-quota cotton is not simply charged the 40% most-favoured-nation rate. Note 2 to the 2026 Tariff Quota Commodity Tariff Rate Table applies a sliding provisional duty: where the dutiable value is at or above RMB 14.000 per kilogram the duty is specific, at RMB 0.280 per kilogram; below that threshold the ad valorem rate is Ri = 9.0/Pi + 2.69% × Pi − 1, where Pi is the dutiable value in RMB per kilogram, rounded to three decimals and capped at 40%.
  - q: Does storing quota goods in a Chinese bonded warehouse avoid the quota?
    a: It defers the paperwork, not the requirement. Art. 9 of the Interim Measures exempts goods entering a bonded supervision site or special customs supervision area from the quota certificate, but Art. 20 provides that when those goods leave the site or the area for import, customs handles them against the Agricultural Product Import Tariff Rate Quota Certificate as ordinary imports. Bonded storage therefore buys time and duty deferral on goods that will be exported or re-sold abroad; it does not create quota for a domestic sale.
  - q: What happens if an allocated quota is not used?
    a: Art. 23 requires the end user to return unused volume to the issuing agency by 15 September, and Art. 30 cuts the following year's allocation in proportion to the volume left unused and not returned. Art. 32 treats failure to return the customs-endorsed first copy of the certificate within 20 working days of the final declaration as non-completion, with the same proportional cut. Art. 28 makes forging, altering or trading certificates a criminal matter plus a two-year bar on applications, and Art. 33 calculates the duty evaded on smuggled quota goods at the out-of-quota rate.
  - q: Is there a separate quota that lets a factory import cotton for processing and re-export?

## A Chinese tariff rate quota is two duty rates on one customs line, not a ban — and which rate applies is decided by a certificate, not by the goods

Importers usually meet the phrase "tariff rate quota" in a 65% duty bill and conclude that the product is restricted. It is not. [Art. 10 of the Tariff Law](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5234556/content.html) sets four ordinary import rates — most-favoured-nation, agreement, special preferential and ordinary — and then provides separately that goods subject to tariff quota administration carry a **tariff quota rate**. [Art. 14](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5234556/content.html) does the work that matters commercially: inside the quota the quota rate applies (or a provisional rate where one exists), and outside the quota the ordinary hierarchy in Arts. 12 and 13 applies. Same product, same HS line, two very different numbers, and out-of-quota import is entirely lawful.

The allocation rules then sit one layer below the tariff. The [Interim Measures on Agricultural Tariff Rate Quota Administration](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zfxxgk/zc/gz/art/2021/art_8fc930eebea04fdd86dd804f3885db09.html) — MOFCOM and NDRC Order No. 4 of 2003, revised in 2019 and again in 2021 — fix the annual volume by reference to China's WTO goods schedule (Art. 2), make the quota global rather than country-specific (Art. 5), pull **every** trade mode into scope (Art. 6), split state-trading from non-state-trading volume for wheat, corn, rice, sugar and cotton (Art. 4), and hand the certificate to a named Chinese end user. The [2026 tariff adjustment plan](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202512/content_7053062.htm) then confirms that quota administration continues for eight categories and that the rates are unchanged.

Three things in that table are routinely missed. First, the quota rate is set **per HS line, not per product family** — wheat flour inside quota is 6%, wheat groats 9%, wheat pellets 10%, while the grain itself is 1%. A milled or pelletised form of the same commodity can cost six to ten times more duty even with a valid certificate. Second, the out-of-quota column is a most-favoured-nation rate, so it is the ceiling rather than the only number: an applicable agreement rate can be lower outside the quota. Third, fertiliser is in the quota table but is administered under its own regime, and its in-quota rate is currently overridden by a 1% provisional rate — a reminder that [Art. 13 of the Tariff Law](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5234556/content.html) puts provisional rates ahead of the base rate.

## Who can hold a certificate — and why an overseas brand cannot be the applicant

This is the commercial bottleneck, and it is not a tariff question. Art. 39 of the Interim Measures defines the end user as the producer, trader, wholesaler or distributor that directly obtains the quota. [NDRC Announcement No. 5 of 2025](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/zgdwjjmywg/art/2026/art_433a548ac05c4809b661a00c4540af4d.html) adds two gates for 2026: registration with the market regulation authorities **before 1 October 2025**, and no violation record under the Interim Measures. On top of that, each product carries a capacity or performance test:

- **Wheat** — held a 2025 quota with actual imports, or 2024 milling/food-processing capacity of 200,000 tonnes or more.
- **Maize** — held a 2025 quota with imports, or 2024 feed-processing capacity of 200,000 tonnes, or 450,000 tonnes for other processors.
- **Rice** — held a 2025 quota with imports, or 2024 food-processing capacity of 100,000 tonnes.
- **Sugar** — held a 2025 quota with imports, or raw-sugar refining capacity of 600 tonnes a day, or use of imported sugar as a processing-trade input.
- **Cotton** — from 1 January 2026, holders with 2025 in-quota import performance that meet the conditions receive an allocation equal to their 2025 in-quota volume automatically, measured on the single-window quota system's reconciliation data.

Note what these tests are: registration, plant capacity, processing history. A brand owner with no Chinese production asset has nothing to apply with. Where the allocation is a state-trading quota — 90% of wheat, 60% of maize, 50% of rice, 70% of sugar, 33% of cotton — the volume must in principle be imported through a designated state-trading enterprise; Art. 22 releases that constraint only if no import contract is signed by 15 August, after which the holder may commission any enterprise with trading rights, or import itself.

Four routes therefore exist in practice, and they are not equally good:

The fourth route deserves a warning attached to it. Art. 18 lets customs release quota goods against a certificate whose trade mode reads "processing trade", but Art. 27 routes an unauthorised domestic sale of those bonded inputs or finished goods straight into the Customs Law penalty provisions, and [Art. 36 of the Tariff Law](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5234556/content.html) adds deferred-tax interest on top of the duty when processing-trade inputs or their products are sold domestically. Processing trade is a manufacturing regime, not a cheap import channel.

## Cotton: the only quota line with a published formula

Cotton is the one category where the out-of-quota cost is not a flat percentage, and it is the part of the schedule most often mis-stated. The Ministry of Finance's tariff glossary defines a sliding duty (滑准税) as a rate that moves **inversely** to import price — the higher the price, the lower the rate — and records that China applies it to a defined quantity of out-of-quota cotton imports. [Note 2 to the 2026 quota table](https://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/202512/P020251229510521470214.pdf) then supplies the arithmetic:

Two consequences follow. A landed-cost model built on "40% out-of-quota" will over-quote duty on high-priced cotton and under-quote it on cheap cotton, sometimes by a wide margin — the formula has to be computed per shipment against the declared dutiable value. And because the sliding duty is formally a **provisional** rate applied to a defined quantity, the quantity itself is administered: [NDRC Announcement No. 2 of 2026](https://www.ndrc.gov.cn/xxgk/zcfb/gg/202603/t20260316_1404162_ext.html) sets a 300,000-tonne cotton sliding-duty processing trade quota, issued against a contract, limited to enterprises with 50,000 spindles or 8,000 tonnes of spunlace capacity, with certificates valid three months from issue and no later than 28 February 2027, and an express prohibition on resale.

## The bonded-warehouse trap: deferral is not quota

This is where bonded models and quota goods collide, and the collision is written into two adjacent articles of the same measure. Art. 9 exempts goods entering a **bonded supervision site or special customs supervision area** from the quota certificate. Art. 20 then provides that quota goods leaving such a site or area for import are handled by customs against the certificate as ordinary imports.

Read together, the position is precise: bonded entry removes the certificate requirement at the point of entry, and it removes the duty at that point too. It does not manufacture quota. Goods can sit in a bonded warehouse or a comprehensive bonded zone and be re-exported, sold to another bonded party, or used in processing for re-export without a certificate. The moment they are declared for domestic sale, the certificate requirement returns — and an importer who has warehoused 500 tonnes of out-of-quota sugar has warehoused a 50% duty bill, not avoided it.

That distinction matters most to brands that have been told "put it in a bonded warehouse and sort the paperwork later". For general merchandise this is reasonable advice. For the eight quota categories it inverts the risk: the warehouse holds the goods, the clock runs on the quota year, and the certificate is issued to a Chinese end user who may not be the party holding the stock. We cover the mechanics of the storage options themselves in [bonded zone versus bonded warehouse](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-bonded-zone-vs-bonded-warehouse.html); the quota point is that storage and quota are decided by different authorities on different calendars.

The economics of that last row are why quota holders rarely sit on unused volume: the sanction is not a fine you can budget for, it is a proportional reduction of your future access, which is the scarce asset. For a foreign brand the lesson is to contract with an end user whose quota you can see being used, and to build the certificate-return discipline into the supply agreement rather than assume the buyer will handle it.

## Where free trade agreements fit

Two mechanisms touch quota goods, and neither is a general exemption. [Note 1 to the 2026 quota table](https://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/202512/P020251229510521470214.pdf) states that where a product subject to a tariff quota under a free trade agreement in force for China is concerned, the **quota rate follows that agreement** — so the 1% or 15% in the table is the default, not necessarily the final answer. Separately, MOFCOM runs **country-specific quotas** for particular origins: [the 2026 rules for New Zealand wool and wool tops and for Australian wool](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/zc/art/2025/art_1873e4ee8c39421c9091f5cea6113aa4.html) are published as a distinct instrument from the global quota, with their own redistribution timetable.

What an agreement does not do is remove quota administration. Outside the quota, the ordinary hierarchy applies, which is where an origin claim still has value: [Art. 13 of the Tariff Law](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5234556/content.html) applies the lower of the agreement rate and a provisional rate, and applies the MFN rate where it is lower than the agreement rate and no provisional rate exists. That mechanism is set out in more detail in [rules of origin and FTA preferential duty](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-rules-of-origin-fta-preferential-duty.html), including the point that a change of working or processing inside a bonded zone can extinguish a preferential claim for quota goods.

## What we deliberately do not assert

- We do not claim that out-of-quota imports of any category are prohibited. Art. 14 of the Tariff Law provides for out-of-quota rates, and the 2026 table publishes them.
- We do not give an out-of-quota duty figure for cotton without the formula. The rate is computed per shipment from the dutiable value, and "40%" is a cap, not a description of the scheme.
- We do not state quota approval rates, in-quota utilisation rates or the share of non-state-trading volume actually taken up. No official source we could verify publishes them for 2026.
- We do not describe the fertiliser quota as being governed by the Interim Measures. Fertiliser sits in the same rate table but under a separate allocation regime, and we have not verified its current procedural rules, so we state only the rates.
- We do not assert that a bonded warehouse, a comprehensive bonded zone or a Hong Kong entity can generate quota. Art. 39 allocates to a Chinese end user; Art. 20 makes the certificate a condition of domestic sale out of bond.
- We do not quote fees, agent commissions or allocation "market prices". Quota trading is prohibited by Art. 28, and any pricing that circulates online for quota volume describes an unlawful transaction.

## FAQ

**Is out-of-quota import banned?** No. Art. 14 of the Tariff Law applies the quota rate inside the quota and the Arts. 12–13 hierarchy outside it. You may import out of quota; you pay 65% on grains, 50% on sugar, 38% on wool, 40% or sliding on cotton.

**Can my Hong Kong company apply for the quota?** The allocation notices require registration with the mainland market regulation authorities before 1 October 2025 plus a capacity or import-performance test, and the certificate names a Chinese end user. A Hong Kong trading company can be the seller, not the quota holder.

**What duty applies to out-of-quota cotton in 2026?** RMB 0.280/kg where dutiable value is at or above RMB 14.000/kg; otherwise Ri = 9.0/Pi + 2.69% × Pi − 1, rounded to three decimals and capped at 40%.

**Does a bonded warehouse help?** It defers duty and, under Art. 9, defers the certificate on entry. Art. 20 brings the certificate back when goods leave for domestic sale. Bonded storage works for re-export, not for creating quota.

**What if the quota is not used up?** Return it by 15 September; otherwise next year's allocation is cut in proportion (Art. 30). Failing to return the customs-endorsed certificate within 20 working days is treated the same way (Art. 32).
