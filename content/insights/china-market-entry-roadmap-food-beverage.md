## Why food and beverage needs its own roadmap

Generic China entry advice fails food and beverage brands because this category carries two obligations most others do not: **facility-level registration with China Customs**, and **label conformity assessed at the border**. Both sit on the critical path, both take months, and neither can be compressed once a shipment is booked. At the same time, food is one of the categories where the cross-border e-commerce channel offers a genuinely fast alternative entry. Understanding which obligations attach to which channel is the difference between selling in one quarter and stalling for a year. This roadmap sets out the sequence for a foreign food or beverage brand entering China from a standing start.

## China market entry for a foreign food brand is a staged sequence in which trademark protection and facility registration must begin before any channel decision, because both have lead times measured in months

Entering the China market for foreign brands in the food and beverage category means running three tracks in parallel rather than in sequence: a **legal track** (trademark, facility registration), a **product track** (formulation review, labelling, testing) and a **commercial track** (channel, pricing, partner). Brands that run these sequentially add six to nine months for no benefit, because the legal track has the longest lead time and no dependency on the commercial one. The correct first move is therefore not choosing a platform — it is filing a trademark and starting facility registration.

## Months 1–2: the legal track, started immediately

Two actions should begin in week one:

- **File your trademark in mainland China.** China operates a first-to-file trademark system, meaning the first applicant generally prevails regardless of prior use elsewhere. Filing before you approach distributors or agents is essential; register in both Latin characters and a Chinese-language mark, since consumers and platforms will use a Chinese name whether you choose it or not ([CNIPA](https://www.cnipa.gov.cn)). Our [trademark registration guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-trademark-registration-foreign-brand.html) covers the process and pitfalls.
- **Determine your GACC facility registration route.** Under GACC Decree 248, every overseas establishment producing food for export to China must be registered with China Customs. Eighteen higher-risk categories — including dairy, meat, aquatic products, edible oils, condiments, nuts, dried fruit and health food — require recommendation by the competent authority of the exporting country; other categories may self-register through the CIFER portal ([GACC](http://www.customs.gov.cn)). Establish which bucket you are in before anything else, because the recommendation route depends on a foreign government's timetable, not yours.

Both actions are inexpensive relative to their impact, and both are irreversible bottlenecks if deferred. The full mechanics are set out in our [GACC food registration guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/gacc-food-registration.html).

## Months 2–4: the product track — labelling is where launches are lost

Chinese-language labelling is the most frequent cause of imported food being detained at a Chinese port, and the causes are almost always procedural rather than substantive. A compliant imported prepackaged food label must carry, in Chinese: product name, ingredient list in descending order, net content in the prescribed format, country of origin, name and address of the domestic importer or distributor, production and expiry dating, storage conditions and a conforming nutrition panel. Additive names must use the standardised Chinese designations, and any claim that implies a health function is prohibited unless the product holds the corresponding approval.

Three rules that save the most time:

1. **Design the Chinese label before the first production run**, not after. Retro-labelling in a bonded warehouse is possible but adds cost and time to every shipment.
2. **Have the label reviewed against the applicable national standards** by someone who has cleared your category before. A one-week review routinely prevents a four-week detention.
3. **Strip home-market claims early.** Wording that is routine in the EU or US frequently breaches Chinese advertising and food-labelling rules, and enforcement sits with the market regulator ([SAMR](https://www.samr.gov.cn)).

Our guide to [Chinese-language labelling requirements](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-import-product-labelling-requirements.html) sets out what must be printed before customs will release goods.

## Months 3–5: the commercial track — choose the channel that matches your evidence

The channel decision follows from how much demand evidence you actually have, not from ambition.

**Cross-border e-commerce (recommended first step).** Retail imports through the cross-border channel are treated as goods for personal use, exempting them from first-import registration, filing and Chinese-label pre-approval requirements that apply to general trade, provided the product appears on the cross-border retail import positive list maintained by China's finance, customs and commerce authorities ([MOFCOM](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn)). You need no China entity and no distributor — an import agent and bonded fulfilment suffice. Realistic timeline: **8–14 weeks to live**. See [cross-border e-commerce in China](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/cross-border-ecommerce-china.html) for the mechanics.

**General trade.** Required for offline retail, food service and wholesale distribution. Adds GACC facility registration, full label conformity, first-import testing and an importer of record with the appropriate food-business qualifications. Realistic timeline: **6–12 months**, and the volume economics only work at container scale. Whether you need a licence and which one is covered in [do you need a China import license](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-import-license.html).

For most brands the answer is both, sequenced: cross-border to prove the category and price point, general trade once monthly reorder data justifies the fixed cost.

## Months 4–6: platform and fulfilment

With the channel set, the platform choice narrows quickly. Food and beverage performs well on Tmall Global and JD Worldwide for considered purchases and on Douyin for impulse and discovery-led products. The comparison is set out in [Tmall Global vs JD Worldwide vs Douyin](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/tmall-global-vs-jd-worldwide-vs-douyin.html).

Fulfilment for food carries two category-specific requirements worth naming early:

- **Shelf life management.** Chinese platforms and consumers are unusually strict on remaining shelf life at delivery; many platforms enforce minimum-remaining-life thresholds at inbound. Short-dated stock sitting in a bonded warehouse is dead inventory.
- **Temperature control.** Chilled and ambient-sensitive products need a bonded facility with the right handling capability, which materially narrows the city choice. Our [bonded warehouse guide](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/bonded-warehouse-cost-process.html) covers cost and city selection.

## Months 6–12: demand generation, then distribution

Only once product is legally landed and fulfillable does marketing spend make sense. For food and beverage, Xiaohongshu drives category education and trust for imported products, while Douyin drives volume through live commerce. Both are covered in our [social platform comparison matrix](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-social-platform-comparison-matrix.html).

Distribution comes last, deliberately. Appointing a national exclusive distributor before you hold a registered trademark, demand data and a settled price architecture is the hardest error in this category to unwind, because the distributor accumulates the customer relationships and — where the trademark was never filed — sometimes the brand itself. Our guide to [finding a China distributor](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-distributor-for-foreign-brands.html) sets out how to structure that appointment when the time comes.

## The roadmap in one view

- **Month 1:** File trademark. Determine GACC registration route. Classify product against the positive list.
- **Month 2:** Begin facility registration. Commission Chinese label design.
- **Month 3:** Label compliance review. Appoint import agent. Select bonded city.
- **Month 4:** Open cross-border store. First bonded shipment.
- **Month 5:** Go live. Begin seeding content.
- **Months 6–9:** Scale marketing spend against measured conversion. Complete GACC registration in parallel.
- **Months 9–12:** Evaluate general trade on real reorder data. Shortlist distributors.

The principle underneath the schedule is straightforward: start the slow legal work immediately, sell through the fast channel while it completes, and let real data — not projections — decide whether to fund the expensive general trade build-out. ## FAQ

**How long does it take a foreign food brand to start selling in China?** Through cross-border e-commerce, roughly 8 to 14 weeks for a prepared brand, since that route avoids first-import registration and needs no China entity. General trade realistically takes 6 to 12 months once GACC facility registration, Chinese labelling and first-import testing are factored in.

**Do I need GACC registration to sell food in China?** For general trade, yes — all overseas food production facilities must register with China Customs under GACC Decree 248, effective 1 January 2022. Eighteen higher-risk categories need competent-authority recommendation; others self-register via CIFER. Cross-border retail import is generally exempt.

**Should a food brand enter China through cross-border e-commerce or general trade?** Start cross-border, graduate to general trade. Cross-border delivers access in weeks with no entity and no first-import registration, letting you validate demand cheaply. General trade becomes necessary for offline retail and food service, but only once demand data justifies the fixed cost.

**What is the biggest compliance risk for imported food in China?** Chinese-language labelling — the leading cause of detention at Chinese ports, usually for mundane reasons such as missing importer details, wrong net-content format or an unapproved claim. Correcting artwork pre-shipment is the highest-return compliance spend available.

**Do I need a Chinese distributor to enter the China food market?** Not initially. A cross-border launch needs an import agent and bonded fulfilment, not a distributor. Appoint one only after you hold a registered trademark, demand evidence and a settled pricing structure.
