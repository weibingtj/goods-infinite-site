## A Chinese customs duty guarantee is a promise to pay, not a payment — and it releases goods before the duty is final

The question importers actually ask is narrower than the law: *can I take the container now and argue about the classification later?* Chinese law answers yes, in one specific instrument. [Art. 66 of the Customs Law](https://www.ccg.gov.cn/zcfg/202405/t20240515_178.html) requires customs to release goods once the consignee or consignor has provided a guarantee commensurate with the legal obligation it owes — subject to exceptions where law or administrative regulation provides otherwise. [Art. 2 of the Customs Affairs Guarantee Regulation](https://www.gov.cn/flfg/2010-09/19/content_1709252.htm) (State Council Decree No. 581 of 2010, revised in 2018) defines the mechanism as a party undertaking to perform a legal obligation, and Art. 14 caps the amount at the maximum duty that could be payable. Two consequences follow, and both are commercially significant. First, the guarantee is a **ceiling tied to exposure**, not a prepayment of a number nobody has calculated yet. Second, the obligation being guaranteed is procedural as much as financial — supplying the document, completing the step, paying when the amount is fixed.

The duty side of the same mechanism sits in [Art. 43 of the Tariff Law](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5234556/content.html), in force since 1 December 2024. Duty on import and export goods is payable within fifteen days of declaration; where the conditions set by customs are met *and a guarantee is provided*, payment may instead be consolidated by the end of the fifth working day of the following month. Deferred payment is available for up to six months on application, again against a guarantee. And in the fourth paragraph, the release mechanism is stated plainly: if duty is not yet paid and the taxpayer applies for a guarantee under the relevant laws and administrative regulations in order to obtain release, customs **shall** process it.

## What a guarantee actually buys: three procedures, not one

Before December 2021 the system was use-by-use: a separate instrument for consolidated tax payment, another for the payment term, another for unresolved valuation or origin. [GACC Announcement No. 100 of 2021](https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/qtwj/202204/t20220412_2674414.html), effective 1 December 2021, collapsed that into one enterprise-level guarantee covering three named purposes.

Item 7 is where most importers *want* to meet it, because consolidated payment is a working-capital instrument: it converts dozens of duty payments per month into one.

## The part that is not negotiable: a guarantee cannot replace a document

This is the sentence that should be read twice. The second paragraph of [Art. 4 of Decree 581](https://www.gov.cn/flfg/2010-09/19/content_1709252.htm) provides that where the state imposes restrictive rules on goods entering or leaving China and a required licence or permit cannot be produced — and in other cases where laws or administrative regulations prohibit guarantees — **customs shall not grant guarantee-based release**. [Art. 66 of the Customs Law](https://www.ccg.gov.cn/zcfg/202405/t20240515_178.html) says the same. A guarantee is not a compliance shortcut; it is a timing instrument. It cannot stand in for a CCC certificate, an import licence, a tariff rate quota certificate, or a registration that the product itself needs before it may be sold in China. Where those regimes bite, [the licence or registration must exist first](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-import-license.html), and no amount of bank paper changes that.

Art. 5 of the same Regulation cuts the other way: for eight categories of specific business — road transport of customs-supervised goods, temporary import and export, goods imported for repair and exported for processing, leased goods, transit goods, storage of supervised goods outside a supervision area, mortgaging supervised goods to a financial institution, and handling customs business for bonded goods — a guarantee is not optional. Fail to provide one and customs will simply decline to process the business.

The filing itself is deliberately cheap. Customs publishes the procedure for [applying to release goods in advance against a guarantee](https://online.customs.gov.cn/static/pages/guides/001029002001/001029002001.html) through the customs integrated service platform or the Single Window, requires a single guarantee application — one copy, electronic or paper, self-prepared — and lists the **fee as none**. What costs money is not the filing; it is the instrument, and that price is set by the bank or the insurer.

The asymmetry in that table is the risk. A cash deposit is converted into duty almost immediately after expiry. A bank or insurance instrument gives customs a window of up to six months, bounded by the instrument's own guarantee period — which is exactly why Art. 67 requires the guarantee period to be **no shorter than** the period approved by customs. An instrument that quietly expires before the customs clock does is not a guarantee; it is a document that fails on the day it is needed.

<tr><td><strong>Tariff guarantee insurance policy</strong> (关税保证保险单)</td><td>Premium only; no deposit, no bank facility</td><td>Electronic data from a networked insurer</td><td>The policy is issued by an insurer; customs accepts electronic data only from institutions that have completed the connectivity testing</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

Announcement 100/2021 is unusually precise about mechanism, and the precision is where filings fail. Art. 3 requires the guarantee letter's beneficiary, or the policy's insured, to **include the customs authorities at both the enterprise's registration location and the declaration location**. A policy naming only the head office's port is not accepted at another port. Art. 4 gives the customs duty function at the registration location the job of filing the guarantee and generating a filing number; electronic data transmitted by a networked institution has the same effect as the original, and customs stops checking originals. Art. 5 then splits the workflow: consolidated payment and payment-term guarantees are selected by filing number on the declaration screen, while valuation-classification-origin guarantees go through the "tax element guarantee filing" module on the Single Window and require customs approval *before* the filing number is selected or a cash deposit is paid. Only when the limit is debited, or the deposit is noted, can a declaration that otherwise meets release conditions be released against the guarantee.

Two provisions make the instrument worth holding rather than using once. The limit **recovers automatically** when duty is paid or the guarantee is written off, so a single filing revolves through the year. And one letter or policy can be shared across different duty-guarantee procedures at the declaration ports named in it. Art. 6 adds the exit: where the guaranteed obligation is discharged and the institution agrees, the filing can be cancelled — electronically by a networked institution, in writing by the enterprise where the filing was manual.

## What customs can do if the guarantee is not honoured

Three escalating consequences, and they should be read together.

The first is operational. Art. 7 of Announcement 100/2021 allows customs to **stop** an enterprise from using guarantee letters and policies for guarantee-based release if it fails to pay duty within the prescribed period. The same article allows the customs duty function at the registration location to stop filing instruments from a financial institution that refuses to honour its guarantee, does not cooperate with duty collection, or whose solvency is in doubt. A bank facility that has been quietly withdrawn is a clearance stoppage waiting to happen.

The second is coercive. If a taxpayer shows clear signs of moving or concealing taxable goods or other property within the payment period, or if there is another risk that duty cannot be paid, [Art. 44 of the Tariff Law](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5234556/content.html) allows customs to order a guarantee; where none is provided, and with the approval of the director of the competent customs office, customs may instruct a banking institution in writing to freeze deposits or remittances up to the duty amount, or seal or seize goods or property of equivalent value. This is the reverse of the voluntary instrument — a guarantee demanded, not offered.

The third is personal, and it is the one overseas directors underestimate. [Art. 49 of the Tariff Law](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5234556/content.html) provides that where a taxpayer has neither paid the duty and late fees nor provided a guarantee to customs, customs may, with approval, notify the immigration authorities to restrict the taxpayer or its legal representative from leaving China. In practice, a foreign brand's China exposure is not only the goods; it is the ability of its people to travel.

For outright abuse the penalty sits in Art. 23 of Decree 581: a guarantor or guaranteed party that uses deception or concealment to provide a guarantee is ordered to perform the obligation and fined between RMB 5,000 and RMB 50,000; in serious cases the guaranteed party may be suspended from the relevant customs business, or have its registration for that business revoked.

## The AEO exemption — and the 2026 change you have to check

[Art. 10 of Decree 581](https://www.gov.cn/flfg/2010-09/19/content_1709252.htm), as amended by the State Council in March 2018, provides that an advanced certified enterprise recognised by customs under GACC rules **may apply to be exempted from guarantees**, following the prescribed procedures. Art. 30(5) of [GACC Order No. 251](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12746/202605/content_7069427.html) listed that right among the twelve management measures for advanced certified enterprises, alongside an average inspection rate below 20% of the rate applied to enterprises under ordinary measures.

That framework changed on 1 April 2026. [GACC Order No. 282](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12746/202605/content_7069427.html) restructures credit into five tiers — advanced certified, certified, ordinary, dishonest and seriously dishonest (Art. 4) — and provides in Art. 8 that both advanced certified and certified enterprises are China's Authorised Economic Operators. Art. 32 then states that the specific management measures for each tier are to be formulated and published separately by the GACC. The statutory basis for exemption, in Decree 581 Art. 10, is unchanged; the operative list of measures is not. Anyone relying on the exemption should confirm the measures in force on the date of application rather than a summary written before April 2026, which is why we treat [AEO status](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-aeo-certification-importer-of-record.html) as a filing question and not a slogan.

The mirror image matters too. Art. 31(2) of Order 251 required dishonest enterprises to provide **full guarantees** for processing trade business, and Announcement 100/2021 Art. 2 opens the guarantee letter and policy route to consignees and consignors of import and export goods **other than dishonest enterprises**. A guarantee strategy that depends on the insurance policy is therefore not available to every entity in a group.

## Why a 1210 cross-border brand usually posts no guarantee at all

This is the structural point that changes the answer for consumer brands. In ordinary trade — [whether the importer is a WFOE or a Hong Kong entity acting as importer of record](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/hong-kong-entity-china-import.html) — the importer is the duty payer and posts the guarantee. In cross-border retail import it is not.

[Art. 4(5) of the 486 Notice](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm) (Shang Cai Fa 2018 No. 486) allocates the roles: the consumer who buys the goods is the duty payer, and the cross-border e-commerce platform, the logistics enterprise or the customs declarant acts as the withholding agent, **provides the duty guarantee to customs**, and bears the corresponding top-up tax obligation and related legal liability. The platform must be registered in China, and the brand must entrust a domestic registered service provider that registers with customs and answers for the truth of the declaration. So the guarantee exists — it is simply held by the domestic operator, and the brand's real exposure is the service contract behind it, not a customs filing. That is a materially different risk profile from a general trade importer posting a six-month guarantee against a disputed valuation, and it is the reason [the 1210 route](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/what-is-1210-bonded-import.html) suits brands without a Chinese balance sheet.

## A workable sequence

1. **Establish who the duty payer is.** Ordinary trade: the importer of record. Cross-border retail: the consumer, with a domestic withholding agent. Everything else follows from this.
2. **Decide which of the three purposes you need.** Consolidated payment is a treasury decision; valuation, classification and origin is a dispute-management decision; payment-term is a timing decision. One filing can cover all three, but the workflow entry point differs.
3. **Choose the instrument against the capital, not the paperwork.** Insurance premium versus bank facility versus locked cash — the six-month conversion rule in Art. 69 makes the cash-deposit route the least forgiving.
4. **Check the beneficiary geography.** The registration location *and* the declaration location must appear on the letter or policy, or the guarantee will not work where the goods actually land.
5. **Confirm the guarantee period covers the customs period.** Art. 67 requires the instrument's period to be no shorter than the approved period. Build in the extension, not the minimum.
6. **Verify the entity's credit tier before assuming the exemption or the policy route.** The AEO measures are being republished under Order 282, and dishonest enterprises are outside Announcement 100/2021 altogether.
7. **Diarise the release step.** Five working days after performance, and a refundable deposit that sits uncollected for three months moves to public announcement, then to the treasury after a year (Decree 581 Art. 21). Unclaimed guarantees are a real line item.

## What we deliberately do not assert

- We do not quote any premium rate, bank commission or facility fee for a guarantee letter or a tariff guarantee insurance policy. No official schedule sets them; they are commercially negotiated and change.
- We do not state that the AEO exemption from guarantees applies automatically or to every procedure. Decree 581 Art. 10 frames it as an application, and the tier-by-tier measures under Order 282 are published separately.
- We do not claim that any guarantee cures a missing licence, certificate or registration. Art. 4 of Decree 581 prohibits it directly.
- We do not publish a list of insurers authorised to issue tariff guarantee insurance policies. The operative test is connectivity with customs, not a static list.
- We do not assert that a guarantee extends the six-month temporary import and export period, or that it converts a temporary admission into a permanent one. Those are separate rules under the Tariff Law and the temporary import and export measures.
- We do not treat the 486 Notice as transferring the consumer's status as duty payer to the platform. The consumer remains the payer; the platform is the withholding agent.

## FAQ

**Does a guarantee mean I have prepaid the duty?** No. It is an undertaking to perform the obligation, capped at the maximum duty that could be payable (Decree 581 Art. 14). Duty is assessed and paid afterwards.

**Can a guarantee replace a missing import licence or CCC certificate?** No. Decree 581 Art. 4 and Customs Law Art. 66 both prohibit guarantee-based release where a required licence or permit cannot be produced.

**Which instruments are accepted?** Cash deposit, a bank or non-bank financial institution guarantee, and a tariff guarantee insurance policy. Bank and insurer instruments must be joint and several liability guarantees (Order 272 Art. 67).

**Can one guarantee cover several ports and several procedures?** Yes since 1 December 2021 — one enterprise-level filing, used nationwide across consolidated payment, payment-term and valuation-classification-origin guarantees, with a revolving limit (Announcement 100/2021 Arts. 4–5).

**How long does a guarantee run?** Generally six months, extendable with customs approval (Order 272 Art. 67). Deferred payment of duty is a separate clock, capped at six months by Tariff Law Art. 43.

**What happens if the guarantee expires unpaid?** A deposit is converted into duty within five working days; a bank or insurance instrument is called on within six months of expiry and no later than the instrument's own guarantee period (Order 272 Art. 69).

**Does an AEO advanced certified enterprise need to post guarantees?** Decree 581 Art. 10 allows it to apply for exemption. Order 282, in force from 1 April 2026, delegates the tier-specific measures to a separate GACC publication, so confirm the measures in force when you apply.
