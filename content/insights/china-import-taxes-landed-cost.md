## China import tax is three charges, not one

China import tax is the combined customs duty, import VAT and consumption tax levied on goods entering the country, calculated on a customs dutiable value rather than on the price you sell at. Most foreign brands treat it as a single rate they can look up, and that is where landed-cost models go wrong: the three charges have different bases and different recovery rules, and your import route swaps one set of rules for another. Get the structure right and landed cost becomes predictable to within a few percent, which is what makes pricing and distributor margins defensible.

## How customs duty is calculated: the dutiable value and the tariff rate

Customs duty in China is an ad valorem charge calculated as the dutiable value of the shipment multiplied by the tariff rate for that HS line and country of origin. The dutiable value is a CIF figure — transaction price plus international freight and insurance up to the port or bonded zone of entry — so freight sits inside the tax base, not on top of it.

Which rate applies is decided by origin and classification:

- **MFN rate** — the default for WTO members, and the ceiling to model against.
- **Provisional rate** — below-MFN, applied to 935 tariff lines in 2026, mainly key components, advanced materials and certain medical products; withdrawn for micro motors, printing machines and sulphuric acid, which returned to MFN ([GACC interpretation of the 2026 Tariff Adjustment Plan, effective 1 January 2026](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/2025-12/31/article_2025123119401553789.html)).
- **Agreement rate** — preferential rates under the 24 free-trade agreements China has in force with 34 trading partners, which can take a line to zero.
- **Special preferential rate** — zero tariff on all lines from the 43 least-developed countries with which China has diplomatic relations.

The 2026 plan brings the tariff schedule to 8,972 lines. Duty below RMB 50 on a single consignment is waived, so samples often clear duty-free ([GACC tariff administration guidance](http://shenyang.customs.gov.cn/eportal/ui?pageId=434597&msgDataId=5c427e87453949c0a2016ddf059a6966)); and because the rate follows the HS line, classification is a pricing decision as much as a compliance one.

## Import VAT is the largest line — and usually recoverable

Import VAT is the value-added tax charged at the border on the composite assessable value — CIF plus duty plus consumption tax — at either 13 percent or 9 percent under China's VAT Law, in force since 1 January 2026.

**Import VAT = (CIF + customs duty + consumption tax) × VAT rate**

Thirteen percent is the default for goods and imports. Nine percent is a closed list: agricultural products, edible vegetable oils and edible salt, water, heating, coal gas and natural gas, books, newspapers and periodicals, and feed, fertiliser, pesticide, agricultural machinery and agricultural film ([STA, VAT Law rate schedule in force from 1 January 2026](https://hunan.chinatax.gov.cn/show/20260211055577)). Food and beverage brands should check which list their line falls into first.

The rate matters less than the recovery rule. Import VAT is creditable input tax for a general taxpayer in China, so a mainland importer deducts it against the VAT it collects on domestic sales. That makes the genuinely unrecoverable cost of a general-trade import **duty plus consumption tax** — the distinction most brands miss when benchmarking a distributor's quote, and the reason a distributor can work on a thinner margin than the border tax figure suggests.

## Consumption tax applies to 15 categories only

Consumption tax in China is a single-stage excise levied at importation and manufacture on 15 defined categories of goods, and it is charged outside the tax base — which is why the formula divides rather than multiplies. It applies to unit values, not brands, and is never creditable.

**Consumption tax (ad valorem) = (CIF + customs duty) ÷ (1 − rate) × rate**

Most consumer goods carry none: apparel, homeware, toys, electronics and most skincare sit outside the list. The categories that bite for imported consumer brands are narrow but sharp ([STA consumption tax rate schedule](https://xinjiang.chinatax.gov.cn/xwdt/ztzl/xwqy/rdjd/202306/t20230620_116721.htm)):

- **High-end cosmetics — 15 percent**, applied when the dutiable unit value reaches RMB 10 per millilitre or gram, or RMB 15 per piece, excluding VAT. A cream below the threshold pays nothing; priced above it, the whole value is taxed.
- **Alcohol — wine 10 percent**, spirits 20 percent plus RMB 0.5 per 500 ml, rice wine RMB 240 per tonne, beer RMB 220 to 250 per tonne.
- **High-end watches 20 percent**; jewellery 5 percent for gold and diamonds, 10 percent for other precious stones; passenger cars 1 to 40 percent by engine size, plus an extra rate on ultra-luxury models; golf equipment and yachts 10 percent.

## Cross-border e-commerce tax: the simplified regime behind the 1210 model

Cross-border e-commerce retail import tax is a separate, simplified regime for 1210 bonded and 9610 direct-mail orders, in which customs duty is temporarily set at zero and import VAT and consumption tax are charged at 70 percent of the statutory amount. It exists for personal-use imports, not commercial trade, and it is the single largest tax difference available to a foreign brand entering China.

The mechanics come from two notices — 财关税〔2016〕18号 and 财关税〔2018〕49号 — applied by customs as follows ([GACC cross-border e-commerce tax policy Q&A, January 2026](http://www.customs.gov.cn/customs/2026-01/13/article_2026041015241877353.html)):

- The single-transaction limit is **RMB 5,000**; the annual limit per individual is **RMB 26,000**.
- Within the limits, duty is 0 percent and VAT and consumption tax are charged at 70 percent of the statutory amount — an effective 9.1 percent for a 13 percent VAT good, or RMB 9.1 of tax on a RMB 100 order.
- The dutiable value is the **actual transaction price**, including retail price, freight and insurance.
- An order above RMB 5,000 but under the annual limit containing one item may still use the channel, at full goods rates and counted against the annual total; beyond it, shipments are managed as general trade.

A published worked example shows the interaction. An eye cream at RMB 425 for 20 g has a dutiable unit value of RMB 21.25 per gram, above the RMB 10 threshold, so 15 percent consumption tax applies: 425 ÷ (1 − 15%) × 15% = RMB 75, charged at 70 percent = RMB 52.50. VAT is computed on the full consumption tax figure, also at 70 percent: (425 + 75) × 13% × 70% = RMB 45.50. Total tax is RMB 98, or 23.1 percent of the consumer's price ([Ningbo Customs guidance, November 2025](http://wuhan.customs.gov.cn/hhht_customs/566254/cjsfw/kjdswgbsjkyw/xfz/index.html)).

The catch is structural: cross-border goods are for personal use and **cannot be resold**, and every order consumes part of the buyer's RMB 26,000 quota. That eventually forces a successful brand onto general trade — model the tax gap as a scaling cost, not a permanent advantage.

## Five variables that move your China landed cost

A China landed-cost model is only as accurate as its inputs, and five of them move the answer materially.

1. **HS classification.** Duty and consumption tax are line-level facts, not brand-level ones; one error can shift duty by ten percentage points or pull a product into the excise list. See our [customs clearance walkthrough](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-customs-clearance-process.html).
2. **Country of origin.** FTA origin can take duty to zero, and an origin attracting retaliatory duty does the opposite — origin follows manufacturing location, not paperwork.
3. **Route.** General trade, 1210 bonded and 9610 direct mail carry three different treatments, and the right answer changes as volume grows.
4. **Consumption-tax status.** Check unit-value thresholds before setting shelf price: RMB 11 per millilitre instead of RMB 9.5 costs 15 percent on the entire value.
5. **What sits inside the dutiable value — and whether the VAT is creditable.** Freight, insurance and certain royalties are in the tax base; and on general trade through a mainland importer the import VAT is recoverable, while on cross-border retail it never is. Two models with identical headline rates can differ by more than 10 percent of unit cost on this alone.

## FAQ

**How much tax do you pay to import goods into China?** It depends on the route, not just the product. On general trade you pay customs duty on the CIF value, consumption tax on the 15 excise categories, and import VAT at 13 percent or 9 percent on the composite value of CIF plus duty plus consumption tax. On cross-border e-commerce retail within the RMB 5,000 single-order and RMB 26,000 annual limits, duty is set at zero and VAT and consumption tax are charged at 70 percent of the statutory amount — an effective 9.1 percent for most goods.

**What is the difference between China import duty and import VAT?** Import duty is a customs charge on the dutiable value (CIF) multiplied by the tariff rate for your HS line and country of origin, and it is never recoverable. Import VAT is charged on the composite assessable value and is creditable input tax for a general VAT taxpayer in China, so the genuinely non-recoverable cost on general trade is duty plus consumption tax.

**Which imported products pay consumption tax in China?** Only 15 categories. High-end cosmetics is the one that most often surprises beauty brands: 15 percent when the dutiable unit value reaches RMB 10 per millilitre or gram, or RMB 15 per piece, excluding VAT. The other common categories are alcohol, high-end watches, jewellery, passenger cars by engine size, golf equipment and yachts.

**Why is the tax on cross-border e-commerce so much lower than general trade?** Because it is a deliberately simplified regime for personal-use imports below the limits, not a commercial one: duty is temporarily zero and VAT and consumption tax are charged at 70 percent of the statutory amount. The trade-off is that cross-border goods cannot be resold and each order consumes part of the buyer's RMB 26,000 annual quota, which caps volume.

**Does the freight cost affect China import tax?** Yes. China's customs dutiable value is a CIF value — transaction price plus international freight and insurance up to the port or bonded zone of entry — so freight sits inside the tax base. That is one reason high air-freight shipments carry a higher tax figure, and why sea freight or bonded consolidation can lower the total even when the goods cost the same.
