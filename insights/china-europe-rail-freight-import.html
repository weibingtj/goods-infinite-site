China–Europe Rail Freight for Importers: What the 2024 Numbers Mean for Your China Entry

A practical guide to the China–Europe Railway Express (中欧班列) for brands importing from Europe into China: 2024 volume, routes, transit times, customs facilitation, cost vs sea and air, and when rail beats the alternatives.

Published 2026-08-25 · Last updated 2026-08-26 · By Bing Wei, Operations Director

Why European brands should look at rail, not just sea and air

For an overseas brand moving product from Europe into China, the default mental model is "sea if cheap, air if urgent." The China–Europe Railway Express (中欧班列) has quietly become the third option — and for a large band of mid-value, time-sensitive goods it is the best of both. In 2024 the network completed 19,000 trips and moved over 2 million TEUs (twenty-foot equivalent units), up 10% and 9% year on year (State Council / gov.cn, January 2025), reaching 227 cities in 25 European countries and over 100 cities in 11 Asian countries.

That is not a niche corridor any more. It is a mature, scheduled logistics backbone — and it changes the calculus for a Europe-sourced China entry.

The China–Europe Railway Express is the scheduled rail corridor between China and Europe, used to move containerised cargo in roughly half the time of ocean shipping

The China–Europe Railway Express is a network of fixed block-train services linking Chinese cities (notably Chongqing, Chengdu, Xi'an, Zhengzhou, and the Tianjin-adjacent hubs) with European destinations such as Duisburg, Hamburg, Lodz, Madrid and London. It is a scheduled service, not a charter: containers are consolidated into regular departures, which is what makes transit times predictable.

The Ministry of Commerce (mofcom.gov.cn) frames the corridor as core Belt and Road logistics infrastructure, and the National Development and Reform Commission reports the cumulative network now serves 26 European countries and 232 cities (NDRC, 2025), with high-value goods — auto parts, machinery, electronics — making up over 60% of China-bound cargo in 2024.

Routes are the geographic corridors, used to match your European origin to the nearest China gateway

Three main routes carry the volume:

  • Northern corridor via Kazakhstan–Russia–Belarus to Poland and on to Western Europe — the workhorse for German and Benelux origins.
  • Central corridor via Kazakhstan–Azerbaijan–Georgia (or Turkey) — a growing alternative that avoids certain border bottlenecks.
  • Southern / Trans-Caspian corridor across the Caspian — expanding rapidly, with Trans-Caspian services up over 30% in early 2025 (NDRC).

For a brand importing into North China, the Tianjin gateway and the Chengdu/Xi'an hubs feed bonded zones in Tianjin, Shanghai, Ningbo, Guangzhou and Qingdao efficiently. GOODSINFINITE links rail arrival at these zones to 1210 bonded fulfillment for 1–3 day domestic delivery.

Transit time is the door-to-door duration, used to decide whether rail beats sea or air

Typical performance:

  • Rail: ~15–20 days door-to-door Western Europe → China bonded/inland port. The fastest scheduled services reach Duisburg from Xi'an in about 10 days (NDRC, 2025).
  • Sea: ~30–45 days, at the lowest cost.
  • Air: ~3–7 days, at the highest cost.

Rail therefore sits in the sweet spot: half the time of sea, a fraction of the cost of air. For a European skincare brand launching in China, or a machinery importer feeding a WFOE, that gap is often the difference between a 6-week and a 3-week cash cycle.

Customs facilitation is the border process, used to keep rail cargo moving instead of queuing

Rail's weak point used to be border dwell time. China has closed that gap with the GACC "Customs–Rail" (关铁通) and "Authorised Economic Operator Mutual Recognition" (安智贸) programmes (customs.gov.cn), which let pre-cleared, sealed containers pass participating borders with minimal inspection. The General Administration of Customs reports these measures have materially cut crossing time on the corridor.

On arrival in China, the cargo still clears customs like any import: commercial invoice, packing list, rail waybill, certificate of origin, and any category licences (NMPA for cosmetics, CCC for regulated electronics, SAMR for special foods). Pre-classifying HS codes and pre-filing licences is what turns "facilitated" into "fast."

Cost positioning is the rate trade-off, used to pick rail for the right shipment profile

Rail is not cheaper than sea, and not faster than air — but it wins on the combined metric. Use it when:

  • The goods are mid-value (air rates would erode margin, but sea transit would strand working capital).
  • The shipment is time-sensitive (a launch, a promotion, a replenishment).
  • Volume is container-scale but not enough to fill a whole vessel booking economically.

For low-value, non-urgent bulk, sea still wins. For emergency or very high-value, air still wins. Rail owns the middle.

Operational realities are the practical caveats, used to avoid surprises

  • Book ahead. Scheduled departures fill; plan 2–3 weeks of lead buffer.
  • Consolidate. Less-than-container loads are possible but costlier per unit — full containers are the efficient unit.
  • Pre-clear licences. Cosmetics, supplements and regulated electronics need filings before the train arrives, not after.
  • Match the gateway. Route to the bonded zone nearest your end customers to protect the delivery-speed advantage.
For Europe-sourced brands, rail is no longer the exotic option — it is the default for the large middle band of shipments where speed matters and air does not pay.

FAQ

Can I import European goods into China by rail? Yes. The China–Europe Railway Express reaches 227 European cities and carries general cargo, machinery, auto parts, electronics and high-value consumer goods. Goods still clear Chinese customs on arrival.

How long does China–Europe rail freight take? About 15–20 days door-to-door from Western Europe to a China port or bonded zone — roughly half of sea and a fraction of air cost. The fastest scheduled services reach Duisburg from Xi'an in about 10 days.

Is rail cheaper than air and faster than sea? Generally yes on both: a fraction of air cost, about half the time of ocean shipping.

What papers do I need for rail import into China? The same core set as any China import — invoice, packing list, rail waybill, certificate of origin, plus any category licences — with GACC 'Customs–Rail' pre-clearance speeding the border.

Does rail work with 1210 bonded import? Yes. European goods can be railed to a China free-trade zone and entered under 1210 for per-order clearance, or cleared as general trade. GOODSINFINITE links rail arrival to bonded fulfillment in five cities.
