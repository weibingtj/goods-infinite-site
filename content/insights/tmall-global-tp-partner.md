## Why the TP question comes up before the store goes live

Most foreign brands discover the term "TP" the moment they get serious about Tmall Global. The store application is manageable, the [eligibility and cost picture is documented](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/tmall-global-store-setup.html), and then reality lands: the merchant back end is entirely in Chinese, customer enquiries must be answered in Mandarin within platform response targets, and the promotional calendar runs on mechanics no overseas team has seen before. That gap is what the TP ecosystem exists to fill. This guide explains what a Tmall Global TP partner actually does, how the commercial models work, how to vet one properly, and when hiring one is the wrong move.

## A Tmall Global TP partner is an Alibaba-recognised service provider that operates a foreign brand's flagship store on the brand's behalf, supplying the local team for listings, customer service, campaigns and data

A Tmall Partner (天猫服务商, commonly shortened to TP) is an agency formally recognised within Alibaba's service-provider ecosystem and engaged to run a brand's store day to day. The brand remains the merchant of record and the store owner; the TP supplies the operating capability that sits behind the storefront. A typical scope covers six workstreams:

- **Store setup and visual design** — building the flagship shopfront, category navigation and campaign pages to Tmall's design conventions.
- **Listing and content localisation** — Chinese product titles, attribute fields, detail pages and search-relevant copy.
- **Customer service (客服)** — Mandarin pre-sales and after-sales response through Alibaba's Wangwang messaging tool, measured against platform service metrics.
- **Campaign and promotion management** — registering for 6.18, Double 11 and Double 12, structuring coupon and bundle mechanics, and negotiating platform traffic slots.
- **Paid traffic operation** — running Alibaba's in-platform advertising products against an agreed target return on ad spend.
- **Reporting and category analytics** — reading the store's data back into pricing, assortment and forecasting decisions.

The cross-border channel this sits inside is regulated rather than informal: retail imports through it are governed by the cross-border e-commerce retail import framework jointly administered by China's Ministry of Commerce, Customs and other agencies ([MOFCOM](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn)), which is why a competent TP also needs to understand the bonded fulfilment layer, not just marketing.

## What a TP does not do — and why that boundary matters

The most expensive misunderstanding in this market is assuming a TP replaces your entire China function. It does not. Three things almost always sit outside the scope:

1. **Product compliance and registration.** A TP is not a regulatory consultancy. Whether your goods need registration or filing before sale depends on category and channel, and it remains the brand's legal responsibility — see our [China product registration roadmap](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/china-product-registration-foreign-brands.html).
2. **Import of record and bonded fulfilment.** Physical movement of goods into a bonded warehouse, customs declaration and last-mile delivery are handled by an import agent or logistics partner. Some larger TPs bundle this through affiliates; many do not.
3. **Brand strategy and pricing authority.** A TP optimises within the strategy you set. If you outsource positioning and price architecture, you will typically get discount-led volume that is hard to unwind later.

## The three fee models, and what each one incentivises

TP commercial terms cluster into three shapes, and each creates a different behaviour:

- **Fixed monthly retainer.** Predictable, and appropriate when GMV is still small. Frequently quoted in the USD 3,000–10,000 per month range depending on headcount and scope. The weakness is that the TP earns the same whether the store grows or stagnates.
- **Commission on GMV.** Roughly 5–15% of store sales, sometimes tiered. Aligns the TP with growth, but pushes toward discounting because gross GMV — not margin — is the metric being paid on.
- **Hybrid (reduced retainer plus commission).** The most common structure for serious launches and usually the fairest: the retainer covers the fixed operating team, the commission rewards genuine growth.

Whichever shape you pick, treat **media spend as a separate, transparent pass-through** with monthly platform-side reporting. Advertising is often the largest first-year line item, and a TP that marks up media without disclosure is the most common source of disputes. The same open-book logic we apply to [transparent import pricing](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/transparent-import-pricing.html) should govern your TP relationship.

## How to vet a TP partner in five checks

Due diligence here is not complicated, but it is routinely skipped:

1. **Verify the legal entity, not the brochure.** Check the operating company's business licence and standing in the national enterprise credit information system maintained by China's market regulator ([SAMR](https://www.samr.gov.cn)). The entity signing your contract should be the entity with the track record.
2. **Confirm the service-provider grade.** Ask for the TP's listing and grade in Alibaba's official service marketplace, and confirm the categories it is authorised and experienced in. A TP that is excellent in home appliances is not automatically credible in skincare or infant nutrition.
3. **Demand category references you can call.** Two or three brands in your category, at comparable stage, contacted directly. Case-study decks are marketing collateral, not evidence.
4. **Interview the actual operating team.** Business development sells; a separate junior team often delivers. Ask who your store lead and customer-service staff will be, and how many other stores they carry.
5. **Read the exit clause first.** Confirm store account ownership, admin credential control, data and creative-asset handover, and a notice period of 60–90 days. If exit terms are vague, expect the handover to be painful.

## Store ownership: the structural point most brands get wrong

The Tmall Global store must be registered to **your** overseas entity, with your brand holding the master administrator account and granting the TP sub-account access only. Where a TP opens the store under its own entity "to speed things up", the brand ends up renting access to its own China customer base — and switching partners means starting again with zero review history and zero followers. This is not a hypothetical risk; it is the most common cause of stranded China operations. Note that operating through a Hong Kong or other offshore entity is entirely workable here and often preferable, as covered in our guide to [using a Hong Kong entity to import into China](https://www.goods-infinite.com/insights/hong-kong-entity-china-import.html).

## When to hire a TP — and when to skip one

**Hire a TP when** you are launching your first China store, have no Mandarin-speaking commercial team, need customer service coverage against platform SLAs from day one, and want to be live before a major shopping festival.

**Skip or in-source when** monthly GMV comfortably supports two or three dedicated hires, your assortment is narrow and stable, or your category is so technical that outside operators cannot represent the product credibly. Many brands run a sensible arc: TP for the first 12–24 months to buy speed and local knowledge, then a hybrid model where an in-house lead sets strategy and the TP executes customer service and campaigns.

Either way, sequence matters. Do not appoint a TP before your compliance route and fulfilment model are settled — a store that cannot legally receive and ship inventory is an expensive shopfront. ## FAQ

**What is a TP partner on Tmall Global?** TP stands for Tmall Partner — an Alibaba-recognised service provider (代运营) that operates a brand's Tmall Global store on its behalf, covering store setup, listings, Mandarin customer service, campaigns such as 6.18 and Double 11, and reporting. The brand keeps ownership of the store and its data.

**Do I need a TP to sell on Tmall Global?** No. Tmall Global permits self-operation, but most foreign brands use a TP at launch because the back end is Chinese-language, customer service must respond in Mandarin within platform SLAs, and campaign mechanics shift every season. Brands often in-source once volume justifies a dedicated team.

**How much does a Tmall Global TP cost?** Typically a monthly retainer of roughly USD 3,000–10,000 depending on scope, a GMV commission of about 5–15%, or a hybrid of both. In-platform advertising spend is a separate pass-through cost and is frequently the largest first-year line item.

**How do I verify a TP is genuinely certified?** Request its profile and grade on Alibaba's official service marketplace, confirm its category track record, obtain two or three referenceable brands you can contact, and verify the operating entity's business licence through the SAMR national enterprise credit system.

**Who owns the store and the customer data if I use a TP?** The brand should. Register the store to your own overseas entity, retain master admin credentials, and contract explicitly for customer data, content and creative to remain with the brand on exit. A store held under the TP's own account is the biggest structural risk in this model.
